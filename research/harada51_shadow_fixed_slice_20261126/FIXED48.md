## Complete 625-element colour alphabet, with a 48D period of covolume 25

Take the even part $H_0$ of the explicit $H=A_9(C_{51})$ in [Harada, §4.3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.29304). The simultaneous cyclic shift on the first 25 and last 25 coordinates is an isometry. Its 3D fixed space $F$ consists of constant blocks and the independent single central coordinate. For an integer numerator $w=3x$, put $a=\sum w_{0:25}$, $t=w_{25}$, and $b=\sum w_{26:51}$. The actual normal squared norm is $(a^2+b^2+25t^2)/225$.

The image of $H_0$ in those integer labels is exactly
$$P=\{(a,t,b):t-a\equiv0\pmod3,\quad b-t+2a\equiv0\pmod9,\quad a+t+b\equiv0\pmod2\}.$$
The first two relations use the circulant row sum 107, and the third is even norm since $w^2$ is divisible by nine. Conversely any such triple is realized: choose first-block numerator with sum $a$, use the bottom code row to set $t$, then change the last-block sum by any multiple of 9 using $9e_{26}$. Equivalently $P$ has a triangular basis $(1,1,8),(0,3,3),(0,0,18)$ with determinant 54. The label volume scaling to the normal metric is $1/(15\cdot3\cdot15)=1/675$, so the projected lattice covolume is $2/25$. Hence $K_{48}=H_0\cap F^\perp$ has covolume 25 (ambient covolume 2). It is even with minimum 6: the paper supplies the parent minimum 5, and the verifier checks a literal norm-six numerator in this kernel.

The labels of $H_0\cap F$ are precisely the members of $P$ with $a,b$ divisible by 25. Necessity follows by constant coordinates. Conversely, with both divisible by 25 take each block coordinate constant and solve the code residue for the last block by its sum, since 25 is invertible modulo 9 and the circulant maps constants to constants. Parity is unchanged. So the horizontal-projection classes of $H_0$ over $K_{48}$ are exactly $(a,b)\bmod25$ and form $(\mathbb Z/25)^2$. All residue pairs occur: the parameter 18 in the third triangular direction is invertible mod25. This is the **canonical projected palette**, not the entire much larger torus $F^\perp/K_{48}$. Projecting the odd half of $H$ adds no colours, because $3e_{25}$ lies in $F$ and toggles parity. The shadow generator $s$ also lies in $F$, so neither does projecting $H_0^*$.

## Upper witnesses, recertified exactly

For 307 distinct pairs of opposite nonzero phase differences, give a literal integer numerator $w$ in $H_0$. Its projected squared norm is
$$\frac{25\sum w_i^2-a^2-b^2-25t^2}{225}.$$
Membership is checked using the published generator and exact parity. There are **ten** nonzero un-witnessed charges, retained as possibly long without assuming anything about them. The 614 remaining costs are **upper witnesses**, not a claim that all 614 distances were minimized. The numerators are multiples of 18: the congruences for $P$ make $a^2+b^2+25t^2$ a multiple of 9, and parity makes it even. The witness itself is nonzero in its projected coset because its residue is nonzero.

For a real squared minimum $\mu$, an interval $(l/225,u/225]$ allows us to forbid every difference with witnessed numerator $\le l$. Any admissible set of $M$ classes is a clique in the remaining graph. There are two inexpensive exact upper certificates:
1. If a collection $B$ has all its pair differences forbidden, each translate of $B$ can contain at most one chosen phase. Counting translates gives $M\le\lfloor625/|B|\rfloor$.
2. By translation anchor at 0. Partition **all** its allowed neighbors into colour buckets with pairwise forbidden differences in each bucket. Then $M\le1+$ number of buckets. This includes all unwitnessed charges.

The 26 intervals cover $0<\mu\le6$. On the last nine, an independent exact maximum-clique branch-and-bound tightens the upper numbers: every greedy colour count is a verifiable bound on its remaining branch, with no node limit. The largest upper expression for the relative 48D density,
$$\frac{M}{25}(\mu/6)^{24},$$
is $0.0836369725681\ldots$ after taking worst interval endpoints, certified **less than 9%** by comparing integer powers. No more than two canonical colours can coexist at the full parent squared minimum 6. Since these are upper witnesses, largest allowed cliques need not be realizable and no sharpness is claimed for 48D.

## 49D: arbitrary height and even several fibres per height

Keep the same full $K_{48}$ fibres, label each by an arbitrary canonical colour, and assign any real height. Normal sites need not be periodic and different colours can share the same height if geometry permits. For any cut $\beta=l/225<\mu$, every interval of width $\sqrt{\mu-\beta}$ contains at most its above clique cap $M_l$: otherwise some pair would be horizontally witnessed at squared distance at most $\beta$, and its strict vertical squared separation within a half-open interval is less than $\mu-\beta$. Identical labels also cannot share the interval since their true horizontal distance is zero. Repeat intervals along the line for intensity $\le M_l/\sqrt{\mu-\beta}$. For very small $\mu$, count separately by label, with intensity $\le625/\sqrt\mu$. With covolume 25 the ratio to the live 49D record is at most $(\sqrt2/25)(\mu/6)^{49/2}$ times the intensity. On $0<\mu\le5.1$ the separate-colour ratio bound is at most 0.292035. For the rest, using rational intervals of width $0.001$, the verifier selects any valid cut from the already replayed 48D caps. Since it uses the left endpoint for the minimum width and the right endpoint for the radius factor, it uniformly bounds all real radii, not just a grid. The certified worst ratio from that part is 0.316802, hence the entire family has upper density **less than one third** of the live record.

**Reproduction:** run `python -B verify48.py` in the package. It uses the standard library, exact integer arithmetic, [phase witnesses and interval certificates](certificate48.json), and the same published Harada minimum input as the main note. The proof does not run the exploratory floating-point enumeration or require trust in it. For exact output, comparisons to 9% and one third use rationals; decimals in the prose are for scale only.


## One useful 50D constructive screen

At $\mu=6$, suppose every occupied normal site in $\mathbb R^2$ carries exactly two different canonical fibres (not merely that two is an upper bound). If two such sites were at distance $<\sqrt{4/25}$, their four phase labels would be distinct and form a clique at cut $1314/225=5.84$, contradicting the exact cap two. A repeated phase is impossible at that separation. Thus these double sites are separated by at least $2/5$. Draw edges for site distances $<\sqrt6/5$. An edge triangle would furnish six distinct labels forming a clique at cut $1296/225=5.76$, where the cap is four. The edges do not cross: in a convex quadrilateral one angle is at least a right angle, so one diagonal has squared length at least twice $(2/5)^2$, larger than $(\sqrt6/5)^2$. A third vertex cannot lie on an edge, since its two endpoint gaps would each be at least $2/5$. The finite straight graph is therefore triangle-free planar, with at most $2N$ edges on $N$ vertices.

Intersect the Voronoi regions of any finite double-site subset with disks of radius $\sqrt6/10$. Only graph edges can cut caps from those disks, with height bounded by the smallest possible separation $2/5$. Counting at most $4N$ such directed caps gives mean truncated cell area at least $0.1539\ldots$ and total **fibre** intensity at most twice the reciprocal. The 50D live center-density benchmark is $(3/2)^{25}/\sqrt3$, requiring fibre intensity $25/\sqrt3$ here. The exact cap estimate in the checker puts the ratio **below 91%**. This excludes the proposed all-paired construction at full radius, not mixed single/double occupancy or arbitrary radii in 50D.
