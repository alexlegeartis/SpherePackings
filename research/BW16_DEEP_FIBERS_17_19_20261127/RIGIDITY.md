# Individual deep shells and strict periodic rigidity

This note gives an analytic reason that coupled infinitesimal shape changes cannot improve the explicit unit-gap stacks of the main note. It concerns every **fixed finite supercell** of those packings, including arbitrary motions of individual sites and any infinitesimal strain of the full period. It is not a global density upper bound and does not give a uniform neighborhood as the supercell grows. Apart from the disclosed standard cubic comparison, it does not close 19D.

## Each individual deep phase already has a perfect, eutactic contact shell

Let \(S_c=\{h\in c+B:\|h\|^2=3\}\) for a deep phase \(c\). Its shell has 512 vectors and is centrally symmetric because \(2c=0\) modulo \(B\). It is eutactic with equal weights: the mean of \(hh^{\mathsf T}\) is \((3/16)I\). Moreover, \(S_c\) is perfect in the elementary sense that if a symmetric form \(Q\) has \(h^{\mathsf T}Qh=0\) for every \(h\in S_c\), then \(Q=0\). Its difference set \(S_c-S_c\) generates \(B\) over \(\mathbb Z\).

**Even numerator case.** In the \(y=2v\) model of the main note, \(v\) has six entries of magnitude 1. There are 16 possible supports, the translates of one weight-six support of a nondegenerate binary quadratic function on \(\mathbb F_2^4\); each support has 32 signings with prescribed sign parity. Translation gives the same degree for each coordinate. Conditional on the support, signs have zero pair correlations, giving the covariance conclusion. Every pair of coordinates occurs in some support: a support and its translate by any nonzero difference overlap in two points, since the corresponding nonconstant affine derivative has weight 8 and the supports each have weight 6.

On a fixed support the distinct pair-sign characters on the parity class are distinct and nontrivial: distinct pairs have symmetric difference of size 2 or 4, not 0 or 6. Thus the condition \(h^{\mathsf T}Qh=0\) forces every off-diagonal entry on that support to vanish. The remaining diagonal equations are the sums on the sixteen translated supports. Their convolution matrix on \(\mathbb F_2^4\) is invertible: its Fourier eigenvalues are 6 at zero and \(\pm2\) otherwise, by the Walsh spectrum of the bent quadratic. Hence all diagonal entries vanish.

Flipping two signs on one support can realize the differences \(2(\pm e_i\pm e_j)/\sqrt2\) for every coordinate pair, by the support property and harmless extra sign parity correction. This generates \(2D_{16}/\sqrt2\). Differences of two weight-six supports generate the residual code \(C\): the derivative of the nondegenerate quadratic for a nonzero translation runs through each nonzero linear part, and translating the base point can give either affine constant, by the nondegenerate alternating form. These difference residues span \(C\). Together the two types generate \(B\).

**Odd numerator case.** Start from signs \(\sigma_i=\pm1\) given by a fixed word plus all affine binary words. At one distinguished coordinate \(k\), use \(-3\sigma_k\) instead of \(\sigma_k\); this gives the 512 short vectors \(y/\sqrt8\). The signs on each pair are independently balanced over the affine words, and each \(k\) is used equally, giving the same covariance. With \(k\) fixed, average \(y^{\mathsf T}Qy=0\) over those words to obtain \(\operatorname{tr}Q+8Q_{kk}=0\). Sum on \(k\) and then substitute, giving diagonal zero. In the off-diagonal part use the linear Walsh character indexed by a nonzero difference \(d=i+j\) of coordinate indices in \(\mathbb F_2^4\). Its eight unordered pairs form a perfect matching. Write their baseline signed coefficients as \(b_{ij}\) and their sum as \(E_d\). Distinguished coordinate \(k\) changes precisely its own matching coefficient by a factor \(-3\), hence the equation is \(E_d-4b_{k,k+d}=0\). Summing over \(k\) gives \(8E_d=0\), so every off-diagonal entry vanishes. Finally, changing the distinguished coordinate with the baseline fixed gives all \(2(\pm e_i\pm e_j)/\sqrt2\) in \(S_c-S_c\), because the two baseline signs can be set independently by affine additions. At fixed distinguished index, changing the affine word gives every codeword residue modulo \(2D_{16}/\sqrt2\). Again the differences generate \(B\).

## No first-order density-preserving deformation of a fixed finite supercell

Use the actual \(t=1\) stacks from the main note: normal stock \(\mathbb Z\), the unit-minimum triangular lattice, or the unit-minimum FCC lattice. Put \(k=1,2,3\) respectively. Every normal nearest edge has horizontal phase difference of depth 3, so all \((h,z)\) with \(\|z\|^2=1\), \(h\in S_c\), are actual contact vectors of squared norm 4. Each complete horizontal lattice \(B\) at a layer also contains its usual squared-norm-4 contacts. To analyze arbitrary finite-index supercells of the indicated product period, it suffices to pass to a contained product supercell \(U\times V\); any proposed deformation for the original supercell is also periodic for this one.

Work at fixed squared minimum 4, with first variation \(Q\) of the Gram form of a full affine strain, and independent first variations \(u_i\) of the site positions in the unstrained coordinates. The blocks of \(Q\) are horizontal, mixed and normal, written \(Q_H,Q_{HN},Q_N\). Every contact vector \(w\) from site \(i\) to site \(j\) imposes
\[
w^{\mathsf T}Qw+2w\cdot(u_j-u_i)\ \ge\ 0.
\]
A nondecreasing density imposes \(\operatorname{tr}Q\le0\). Sum the horizontal contact constraints, for the \(D_{16}\)-root contacts \(2(\pm e_i\pm e_j)/\sqrt2\) alone, over every site. Displacement terms cancel, and these root directions are isotropic, so \(\operatorname{tr}Q_H\ge0\). Sum the cross constraints over the 512 horizontal contacts on each directed nearest-normal edge, and over all sites and normal edges. The horizontal average covariance is \((3/16)I\), the signed mean is zero, and normal shell directions have average \(zz^{\mathsf T}=I/k\) and signed mean zero at each layer. Each horizontal shift permutation is a bijection of the horizontal positions modulo \(U\), ensuring displacement terms cancel. Thus
\[
(3/16)\operatorname{tr}Q_H+(1/k)\operatorname{tr}Q_N\ \ge\ 0.
\]
Together with \(\operatorname{tr}Q\le0\), since \(1-3k/16>0\), this makes both traces zero. All used inequalities are equalities; this also holds for every standard horizontal contact in \(B\), not just its \(D_{16}\) part, by averaging the equal-sign-parity hyperplane contacts as well. Sum the horizontal equality for each fixed \(D_{16}\)-type direction over all horizontal positions: this gives \(h^{\mathsf T}Q_Hh=0\) for each such root direction, so \(Q_H=0\).

Consider a nontrivial horizontal Fourier character \(\chi\) of the finite group \(B/U\). The horizontal contact equations on each layer give \((\chi(h)-1)h\cdot a=0\) for its horizontal velocity mode \(a\) and each horizontal contact \(h\). The norm-four contact vectors not orthogonal to any nonzero \(a\) generate \(B\), even for a complex velocity. For the \(D_{16}\) roots, an orthogonal root can be written as the difference of two nonorthogonal roots: use a third coordinate \(k\) to cancel one endpoint, choosing its sign so the dot product is nonzero. This is possible unless all coordinates of \(a\) vanish. It follows that the chosen contacts generate \(2D_{16}/\sqrt2\). For the residue code, fix a coordinate where \(a\ne0\). In every affine hyperplane containing that coordinate some even sign choice has nonzero dot product (the individual sign characters are distinct). The nonconstant affine functions taking value 1 at that coordinate span \(C\): two such functions generate each value-zero affine function, and three of them with nonzero slopes \(r,s,r+s\) sum to the all-one word. Hence \(\chi\) would be trivial if \(a\ne0\), a contradiction. The horizontal velocity has no nontrivial horizontal mode.

Now fix a nearest-normal step \(z\) from layer \(i\) to layer \(j\). For a nontrivial character, the cross equalities reduce to
\[
z\cdot\big(\chi(\ell_h)b_j-b_i\big)=0,\qquad h\in S_c,
\]
where \(b_i,b_j\) are the normal-velocity modes and \(\ell_h\in B\) is the horizontal lattice offset relative to the two layer shifts. Their offset differences are exactly \(S_c-S_c\), which generate \(B\). Thus two \(\chi(\ell_h)\) values differ, giving \(z\cdot b_i=z\cdot b_j=0\). At every layer the \(z\)'s span the normal space, so all nontrivial normal-velocity modes vanish too.

For the trivial horizontal mode, subtract the cross equalities at \(h\) and \(-h\). Because the shell spans horizontally, \(\Delta u_H=-Q_{HN}z\) on each nearest-normal edge. Summing along paths that wrap around the period in the nearest directions gives \(Q_{HN}z=0\) for each such direction, hence \(Q_{HN}=0\) and \(u_H\) is constant. The normal equations are \(z^{\mathsf T}Q_Nz+2z\cdot\Delta u_N=0\). The same period loops give \(z^{\mathsf T}Q_Nz=0\) for every nearest direction, so \(Q_N=0\) (the length-one directions of \(\mathbb Z,A_2,D_3\) are perfect).

The resulting zero edge extensions leave only an infinitesimal normal Euclidean motion. In dimension one this is immediate. The triangular tiling is a face-sharing assembly of full rigid triangles. FCC is the regular tetrahedral–octahedral honeycomb: a tetrahedron is infinitesimally rigid, and an octahedron with vertices \(\pm e_i\) is also infinitesimally rigid. For the latter, write the two velocities at \(\pm e_i\) as \(a_i\pm b_i\). Expanding the equations for the four edges between axes \(i,j\) in the two signs forces \(a_i=a_j\) across every coordinate after using all pairs, and \(b\) skew-symmetric. A shared triangular face fixes the difference of two 3D infinitesimal rigid fields; it must be zero. Periodicity rules out a nonzero infinitesimal rotation, so the normal velocities are also constant.

Finally, if there were arbitrarily nearby nontrivially deformed packings of at least the same density for a fixed supercell, normalize each to squared minimum 4, quotient translations and rotations, and divide all Gram/displacement deviations by their combined norm. A convergent subsequence would give a nonzero solution of the preceding first-order inequalities, which do not admit one. This proves strict local maximality under the stated fixed-supercell perturbations. The same simpler argument with \(S_c\) alone in 16D gives strict local maximality of every fixed-period depth-three motif under primitive-period strains and independent motif shifts: each active contact shell is perfect and equal-weight eutactic, and the depth-three graph of such a motif is complete.
