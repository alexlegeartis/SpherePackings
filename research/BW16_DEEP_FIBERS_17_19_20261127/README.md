# Barnes–Wall deep phases and complete fibers in 16–19 dimensions

**No density record.** The most promising scalar compression with complete Barnes–Wall fibers had about 17% optimistic headroom in 19D, and the ordinary FCC stack misses the published record by only 0.64%. We identify the finite phase geometry that a better normal packing would have to use, prove sharp canonical results in 16–17D and exclude the scalar canonical family in 18D and rule out the three standard cubic normal stocks in 19D. Other normal shapes, noncanonical phases, and incomplete horizontal fibers are outside the main 19D exclusion.

Compare center densities; occupied density equals it times the unit-ball volume. The pinned comparators are [Barnes–Wall BW16](https://www.math.rwth-aachen.de/~Gabriele.Nebe/LATTICES/BW16.html) and [laminated Λ17](https://www.math.rwth-aachen.de/homes/Gabriele.Nebe/LATTICES/density.html), both 1/16; [Bierbrauer–Edel B18](https://www.yvesedel.de/Papers/Sphere.pdf), $3^9/2^{18}=0.0750846863\ldots $; and [Chen–Hu–Li–Wang–Wu's 19D antipode](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.02394v1#S3.SSx4), $13^{19/2}/(3^9 5^{21/2})=0.0889581021\ldots $, occupied $0.00414736914586272$ in the live table.

## A 256-vertex phase space with a sharp nine-sheet bound

Take $B=(C+2D_{16})/\sqrt2$, where $C=\operatorname{RM}(1,4)$ consists of the 32 affine binary evaluation words on $\mathbb F_2^4$, and $D_{16}$ is the even-coordinate-sum integer lattice. The codewords of weight 8 and 16 and their pairwise even intersections make this a lattice. Its squared minimum is 4 and covolume 16, by the binary code minimum and the covolume of $2D_{16}$. In $B^*$, write a point as $y/\sqrt8$ with $y=2v+p\mathbf1$, $p\in \{0,1\}$, $v\in \mathbb Z^{16}$. Duality against $2D_{16}$ requires all $y_i$ have the same parity; duality against $C$ is $y\cdot c\equiv 0\pmod4$ for each $c\in C$, i.e. $w=v\bmod2\in C^\perp$.

A class in $B^*/B$ is specified by $(p,b,w+C)$, where $b\equiv\sum_i(v_i-w_i)/2\pmod2$. Changing $w$ by $c\in C$ does not affect $b$ because $w\cdot c$ is even. Addition XORs $p$, sums the two binary quotient classes, and XORs the two $b$'s together with $w\cdot w'\bmod2$ (the extra all-one carry when both $p=1$ is in $C$ and its coordinate carry parity is even). In particular the group has exponent two, size 256 and the norm modulo 2 is its nondegenerate quadratic form $q$; its polar form is $2\langle x,y\rangle\bmod2$. Nondegeneracy of the dual-lattice discriminant pairing proves the assertion.

- For $p=0$ and $w+C\ne0$, the minimum squared norm is half the minimum weight of $w+C$; an arbitrary sign choice realizes either carry parity. The quotient $C^\perp/C$ is the space of quadratic functions of four binary variables modulo affine functions. A nonzero alternating quadratic part has rank 2 or 4. The minimum weight among affine additions is respectively 4 or 6: a rank-2 part reduces to $x_1x_2$ and rank 4 to $x_1x_2+x_3x_4$, and their Walsh maxima are 8 and 4. For $w+C=0$, the other carry $b=1$ has squared minimum 2, via $v=2e_i$.
- For $p=1$, squared minimum is 2 if $b=0$, realized by $y_i=\pm1$; the signs are the chosen word $w$, of even weight. Changing one entry from sign $\sigma$ to $-3\sigma$ flips $b$ and adds squared length 1. The parity restriction gives exact minimum 3 for $b=1$.

Thus the true squared depths are 0,2,3 with 1,135,120 phases. For distinct phases, having depth three is exactly $q(a-b)=1$. If $m$ mutually deep phases are translated to include 0, their $m-1$ remaining vectors have $q=1$ and polar Gram $I+J$ over $\mathbb F_2$. Rank ≤8 gives $m\le 10$. For $m=10$, the nine vectors have their only relation as the all-ones vector (their span has dimension 8); but the sum has quadratic value $9+\binom92=1\pmod2$, contradiction. Thus $m\le 9$, and the checker gives the explicit nine-clique `[0,12,22,26,192,206,210,221,252]` in its 256-label order. **Unique completion:** normalize an eight-clique to 0 plus seven vectors. If extendable, they must be independent: the only possible relation is all-ones, which pairs oddly with any completing vector. Their span's one-dimensional perpendicular is generated by their sum $s$, with $q(s)=0$. The two simultaneous polar solutions differ by $s$; pairing with $s$ is one, so exactly one has $q=1$. This proves the claimed uniqueness, whenever the eight-clique is extendable.

At $k=0$, i.e. in 16D itself, a canonical motif of squared minimum 3 is strictly below the Barnes–Wall density: the ratio is at most $9(3/4)^8<1$. At squared minimum 2, all 256 phases are allowed and $256(2/4)^8=1$. A singleton also has ratio 1. This is a complete fixed-period canonical result, not an unrestricted 16D upper bound.

## Wider geometry: arbitrary normal placements and scalar horizontal metric

At sphere radius 1, use complete translates of (\sqrt tB) in each occupied normal position in $\mathbb R^k$, $k=1,2,3$. Then $t\ge 1$ by the minimum of a single fiber. The normal positions may be entirely irregular, and for $t\ge 4/3$ can coincide at different labels. Let $\rho$ be their upper density counted with multiplicity; the center density in dimension $16+k$ is at most $\rho/(16t^8)$. This follows by integrating complete periodic horizontal fibers (the horizontal count in any large box divided by volume tends to $1/(16t^8)$, uniformly in translations), or directly with any periodic count.

Write $v_k$ for unit-ball volume, and $P_1=1,P_2=2/\sqrt3,P_3=\sqrt2$ for the optimal normal point densities at unit separation, from the 1-, 2- and 3-dimensional sphere-packing theorems. Three independent bounds on the total center density are
```
A_k(t) = P_k / [16 t^8 $4-3t$^$k/2$]       $t<4/3$,
B_k(t) = 9·2^k / [16 v_k t^8 $4-2t$^$k/2$] $t<2$,
C_k(t) = 256 P_k / [16·2^k t^8]            (all t).
```
The first uses the actual deepest squared distance ≤3, so normal separation must be at least `sqrt(4−3t)`. In fact it works for arbitrary real horizontal shifts, since the [Barnes–Wall covering radius](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.06092), in this normalization, has square 3. The second integrates the count in every open normal ball of radius `sqrt(4−2t)/2`: all points in that ball must have pairwise deep horizontal differences, so there are at most nine. The third applies ordinary packing separately to each label (same-label normal separation at least 2) and adds. For $B,C$ it is essential that the entire phase alphabet is canonical.

- **17D:** apply $A$ on ([1,13/10]), $B$ on ([13/10,15/8]), then $C$; all bounds are below $1/16$ except equality of $A$ at 1. There is a nonlattice equality family at $t=1$: unit normal steps, any sequence of labels from the nine-clique with no consecutive repeats. For example the period-four sequence `0,192,0,206` is not a lattice translate, since horizontal differences of consecutive normal fibers are not constant. Only adjacent layers need checking. This reproduces density, not a new record.
- **18D:** apply $A$ on ([1,32/25]), $B$ on ([32/25,7/4]), then $C$; all strictly below B18. A concrete ordinary $t=1$ triangular stack has center density $1/(8\sqrt3)=0.0721687836\ldots $; use four different deep labels on the two coordinate parities. The differences of triangular squared norm 1 or 3 all change the parity, and the rest are already at squared distance ≥4.
- **19D:** apply $A$ up through $73/60$; apply $B$ from $51/40$ through $31/20$, and $C$ thereafter. A gain can therefore occur only for $73/60<t<51/40$. The earlier $A$ bound again applies for off-grid shifts. At $t=1$, a full FCC stack has center density $\sqrt2/16=0.0883883476\ldots $ (0.64% below the new comparator). In raw FCC coordinates $x\in D_3$, use eight mutually deep labels indexed by the two independent coordinate parities and the extra bit $\sum_i\lfloor x_i/2\rfloor\bmod2$. The first bit-pair separates every nearest edge and every squared-normal-3 edge; the extra bit separates the squared-normal-2 axial edges. Exact periodic checks are included.

The comparisons use endpoint calculations: $t^{16}(4-3t)^k$ has only a maximum, and $t^{16}(4-2t)^k$ increases up to $32/(16+k)$. The file checks the required rational inequalities (using $\pi>157/50$ in the upper bound $B$), so the displayed scale delimiters do not depend on a floating-point search.

## 19D: dismiss the compressed FCC, BCC and cubic designs without assuming additive colors

Normalize normal squared lengths by $4-2t$; a pair at normalized squared distance <1 must be deep. The required number density in these normalized coordinates is
$
T(t)=16\delta_{19}^{\rm record}t^8(4-2t)^{3/2}.
$
It is increasing throughout the candidate interval, and at the left endpoint it exceeds $8\sqrt2$, $5^{3/2}$ and $2(11/4)^{3/2}$, checked exactly below. In this argument assume the normal stock is completely filled, with one horizontal fiber at each site and arbitrary labels, not necessarily linear or periodic.

- **FCC:** if the unit edge squared length is scaled to $a$ in the normalized units, point density is $\sqrt2/a^{3/2}$. To beat the comparator, $a<1/4$. The center and its 12 neighbors form a 13-point set of diameter at most $2\sqrt a$, and would require a deep clique larger than nine.
- **Simple cubic:** if the axis squared length is $a$, its density is $1/a^{3/2}$; necessarily $a<1/5$. The ten points `{0,e1} × {0, ±e2, ±e3}`, meaning the ten sums of these two sets, have squared diameter $5a$, another forbidden deep clique.
- **BCC:** with cube side squared $a$, point density is $2/a^{3/2}$, so $a<4/11$ is necessary. Eight corners of any cube are a deep clique (diameter squared $3a$). Its center and the center of the neighboring cube across one face both lie within squared distance $11a/4<1$ of every one of the eight corners. Both complete that same eight-clique, so their labels must be equal. These centers have normalized squared separation $a$, which gives actual squared separation $a(4-2t)<4$, forbidden for equal labels.

This covers the entire candidate scalar window for those three fixed shapes, including every choice of scale. It does not rule out an affine deformation of the normal stock, partial row deletion, or different non-Bravais arrangements. The global 19D two-bound relaxation `min(A,B)` has optimistic maximum about $0.10389$ near $t=1.2371$: the decisive obstruction for the cubic designs is geometric overlap of local cliques, not simply a loose volume estimate.
