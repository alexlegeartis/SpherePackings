# Exact projected-color optimum, even after arbitrary common index-two refinements (20D, 21D)

Keep precisely the primitive normal embeddings in the accompanying local-rigidity proof. Let L be the horizontal section, let A=K*/K be the discriminant color group of D=125 or 36 complete horizontal L-cosets in the projected Leech lattice, and fix ANY index-two subgroup L' of L. Among all unions of translates of L' inside that projected lattice, including those selecting either half, both halves or neither for each of the D parent colors, the original five- or four-site construction maximizes center density at all radii. This statement is about those exact colors in their Euclidean metric and those refinements. It does not replace the local-strain theorem or address arbitrary higher indices, independent sublattices that differ by color, general off-grid sites or arbitrary deformations.

Let w(c) be the minimum squared norm of a normal representative of c, and q a minimizing pairing profile. For every color, the explicit Leech shell of norm-four vectors with exactly q is nonempty. Matching the normal lift to q by a K-translation and using the Leech minimum four gives projected squared distance exactly 4−w(c) between complete distinct colors. The zero class has nonzero pure norm-four contacts and no smaller same-color displacement. For every q the affine differences of this minimal shell span all of L/2L: in the replay their images under the 24 mod-two integral pairings with the fixed ambient Leech roots have rank d=20/21, the full dimension of L/2L. Differences belong to L. As a result, every nonzero character of L is nonconstant on shell differences relative to any one contact: both values occur on the affine shell. Any choices of lifts merely toggle that character value consistently. Thus the minimum between distinct colors remains 4−w for each pair of halves, whatever common index-two subgroup and markings are chosen. On the zero shell the character takes both values on actual pure norm-four vectors, giving the same-half minimum four and the opposite-half distance four. Filling the two halves of every touched parent color cannot lose the normalized radius (and only increases occupancy), so it suffices to handle full colors. The maximum squared minimum in scope is automatically four by the same-half pure contacts.

A full-color set at minimum 4−b/D must be a clique in the Cayley graph with allowed nonzero differences satisfying D w(c)≤b. Along a constant graph the density is largest at its left endpoint b. For D=36 the threshold values are 0,12,15,24,36,39,48,60,63; at its first six thresholds the exact anchored clique sizes are 1,2,4,5,6,7. At the last three the trivial upper bound 36 suffices. For D=125 the threshold values are 0,50,100,150,200,250; the first three maxima are 1,5,10. At 150 a forbidden difference of order five puts a cycle in every coset of its order-five group; at most 2 per cycle, at most 50 total. At 200 and 250 the trivial 125 suffices. Compare the square of density, N²(1−b/(4D))^d/D, in rational arithmetic. The unique winning thresholds are b=15 and b=50, where the original sites attain the specified upper bound. This is not a uniqueness claim about the point set.
