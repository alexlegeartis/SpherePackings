# A five-site parent cluster improves the 95D MW92 lattice bound

The following gives a 95-dimensional **lattice** packing of occupied density at least **2.129324206844210e-21**, improving the verified predecessor **2.128470378304569e-21** by strictly more than **0.040114%**. The horizontal shifts are certified to exist by the covering argument, not supplied as a numerical 95D Gram matrix. This is a density lower bound, not optimality. No 94D bound changes.

## Exact inputs and parent coordinates

Let \(L=\mathrm{MW}_{92}\) be the [characteristic-47 Mordell–Weil lattice of Shioda (1991)](https://doi.org/10.2307/2374791), in the normalization of [Schütt–Shioda, Theorem 13.35 and §13.4.2](https://link.springer.com/book/10.1007/978-981-32-9301-4). It is even integral of minimum squared norm 16 and covolume \(47^7\). In the elliptic model \(y^2=x^3+1+t^{48}\) over \(\mathbb F_{47}[\omega]\) there is the orthogonal automorphism \(J:(x,y)\mapsto(\omega x,y)\), \(I+J+J^2=0\). All bad fibres are irreducible: \(1+t^{48}\) has simple finite roots, giving type II cusps, and the chart \((s,s^{16}x,s^{24}y)\), \(s=1/t\), is smooth at zero. In particular there are no reducible-fibre corrections in the height pairing. A section with polynomial coordinates \(x,y\) of degrees at most 16 and 24 is disjoint from the zero section, including at infinity, and its height is 16.

Use the literal archived parent basis in [gram96.json.gz](../mw92_cusp_d4_20261122/gram96.json.gz) (Git blob e02f3fde8c0be66bc2b5d85ce12913caf8f9dfc1). Its \(\mathrm{MW}_{92}\) basis Gram is the field `horizontal_gram`; its 210 sections and corresponding coordinates are `source_point_polynomials` and `source_coordinates_in_horizontal_basis`. Take indices 0 and 6 (zero-based). The first is \(P=(-\omega^2t^{16},1)\); below are both basis coordinate rows (the second section is called \(Q\)). For two distinct such sections the intersection multiplicity is the degree of the gcd of the two coordinate differences plus
\(\min(16-\deg(x_P-x_Q),24-\deg(y_P-y_Q))\), with the order of a zero polynomial taken as infinity. At a finite simple cusp a section cannot meet the singular point: if both specialized coordinates vanished, \(y^2-x^3\) would vanish to order at least two, while \(1+t^{48}\) has order one. At every finite common point, the length of the ideal generated by the two coordinate differences is the sum of their common vanishing orders. The same reasoning in the infinity chart gives the displayed minimum there. The Shioda pairing is \(8-P\cdot Q\), because both sections miss the zero section and the arithmetic genus term in this model is \(8\). See also the [explicit height audit](../mw92_cusp_d4_20261122/README.md). The replay verifies \(P,-JP,Q,-JQ\) satisfy the equation over the exact finite field and produces their Gram:
\[
H=\begin{pmatrix}16&8&4&2\\8&16&2&4\\4&2&16&8\\2&4&8&16\end{pmatrix}.
\]

### The five-site partition

Put \(v_1=P,v_2=-JP,v_3=Q,v_4=-JQ\). The points \(0,v_1,v_2,v_3,v_4\) lie in the ball about \(c_0=(v_1+\cdots+v_4)/4\) of squared radius \(17/2\). In fact \(\|c_0\|^2=15/2\), and each \(\|c_0-v_i\|^2=17/2\), since every row of \(H\) sums to \(30\).

Their four nonzero classes are linearly independent in \(L/5L\). If a linear combination belongs to \(5L\), pairing with the \(v_i\) shows that its coefficient class is in the kernel of \(H\bmod 5\). That kernel is spanned by \(u=(1,1,1,1)\), \(w=(1,-1,1,-1)\): the other eigenvalues of \(H\) are \(18,6\), whereas \(Hu=30u\), \(Hw=10w\). Lift the coefficients as \(\alpha u+\beta w\); changing the integral lift does not change whether the vector is in \(5L\). Its norm would be \(120\alpha^2+40\beta^2\), divisible by 25 if it belongs to \(5L\). Dividing by 5 and reducing modulo 5 gives \(\alpha^2=3\beta^2\), so \(\alpha=\beta=0\bmod 5\). This proves the rank claim.

Therefore assign \(v_i\mapsto i\) by a linear functional \(L/5L\to\mathbb F_5\); its kernel in \(L\) has index five. The five-site cluster has exactly one representative of every residue, so its translates by that kernel partition \(L\). Consequently one coset of balls of radius \(r\) is covered by one coset of balls of radius \(\sqrt{17/2}+r\) per five original centres, with the new centres translated by \(c_0\). This is an actual periodic grouping, not a bound on an unequal-ball covering density.

## How the fixed-order and generic lattices are obtained

We use the same [audited equal-ball threshold](../mw92_cfr_taylor13_20261123/) \(A=11.00098526\) and [ordinary equal-ball Coxeter–Few–Rogers theorem](https://doi.org/10.1112/S0025579300002059) as the [previous verified 95D package](../mw92_paired_cover_9495_20261126/). They assert that a periodic equal-ball covering of \(\mathbb R^{92}\) cannot have density as low as \(\mathrm{vol}(B_{92}(\sqrt A))/47^7\). Use \(d(x)=\operatorname{dist}(x,L)\). We reproduce the exhaustive dichotomy and indicate its inherited exact proofs.

Choose \(a\) with \(d(a)^2>A\), which exists because otherwise one such equal ball per \(L\)-cell would give a covering. Put \(s=16-A\), \(T=I-J\), \(t_0=16-3s\).

* If \(d(Ta)^2\ge t_0\), the generic first two shears are \(a,-Ja\), with vertical norm \(s(i^2+ij+j^2)\). At Eisenstein norm 1 the horizontal distance is \(d(a)\), at norm 3 it is \(d(Ta)\), and otherwise a nonzero Eisenstein norm is \(\ge4\) with \(4s>16\). This works at the exact shear scale \(s\), without the prior deliberate rounding to \(5\).
* Otherwise take \(h=T^{-1}\) of a shortest representative of \(Ta\bmod L\), put \(u_0=a-h\), and note \(Tu_0\in L\), \(\|h\|^2<t_0/3\), using \(T^\top T=3I\). For the three equally near orbit points of a closest lattice point under the affine map \(v\mapsto Jv+Tu_0\), the displacements from \(u_0\) sum to zero. Averaging the squared distances after adding \(h\) gives \(d(u_0)^2>A-t_0/3=32/3\). Also \(3d(u_0)^2\) is an even integer, since \(T(u_0-v)\in L\) for \(v\in L\). Thus \(d(u_0)^2\ge34/3\), and \(u_0\bmod L\) has exact order three by \((I-J^2)T=3I\). Let \(p=14/3\), \(B=10.78402070\), \(q=16-B\). The two-strong-one-weak covering estimate in the predecessor finds \(b\) with \(d(b)^2,d(b-u_0)^2>B\), \(d(b+u_0)^2>B-2p\): otherwise cover by those three kinds of balls, pair the one weak phase using a primitive minimum vector of norm 16 to make dimers, and use \(2+\sqrt{B-2p}<\sqrt B\), \((5/2)B^{46}<A^{46}\). Its vertical Gram is \(\begin{pmatrix}p&p/2\\p/2&q\end{pmatrix}\) and first two shears are \(u_0,b\).

For either case, adjoining an independent third real vertical generator with horizontal shear \(c\) yields the full lattice generated by \(L\times\{0\}\) and the three vertically projected basis vectors with the indicated shears. The exact vertical Grams are:
\[
G_g=\begin{pmatrix}s&s/2&-s/2\\ s/2&s&-s/2\\-s/2&-s/2&16-D_g\end{pmatrix},
\quad D_g=10.51475582;
\qquad
G_f=\begin{pmatrix}p&p/2&p/2\\p/2&q&q/2\\p/2&q/2&16-D_f\end{pmatrix},
\quad D_f=10.5357 .
\]
If \(k>0\) and the vertical norm \(Q_G(i,j,k)<16\), only \(k=1\) occurs; the replay verifies this using \(i_l^2\le Q_G(G^{-1})_{ll}\) and checked inverse bounds. Negation covers \(k<0\), and \(k=0\) is the independent 94D argument (also checked). With \(k=1\), every forbidden phase for \(c\) is centred at minus the corresponding two-shear combination, with squared radius \(16-Q_G(i,j,1)\). Take the maximum when labels collide; for fixed-order \(i\) is only needed modulo 3.

* **Generic:** six distinct labels need at most \(D_g\). Another six labels need \(D_g-2s\). The minimum-vector triangle \(0,v,-Jv\) has squared circumradius \(16/3\) and partitions \(L\) under an index-three kernel: if the classes of \(v,Jv\) are independent modulo 3 assign both 1; if dependent, \(J^3=I\), primitivity and \(\mathbb F_3^\times\) give equality and hence the same assignment. The ball containment inequality \(\sqrt{16/3}+\sqrt{D_g-2s}<\sqrt{D_g}\) is exact. There are then at most \(6+6/3=8\) equal-radius balls per \(L\)-cell if no \(c\) exists.
* **Fixed-order:** six labels require at most \(D_f\); label \((1,0)\) requires \(D_f-2p\); labels \((0,1),(1,1),(0,-2),(1,-2)\) require \(D_f-2q\). Use respectively no clustering, the two-site dimer with radius 2, and the **five-site cluster above**. The checked inequalities are \(2+\sqrt{D_f-2p}<\sqrt{D_f}\) and \(\sqrt{17/2}+\sqrt{D_f-2q}<\sqrt{D_f}\). The equal-radius count is at most \(6+1/2+4/5=73/10\).

In either bullet, for multiple kinds of clusters intersect their finite-index lattice periods; the resulting periodic covering has the stated count per \(L\)-cell (coincident centres can only reduce the count). The exact inequalities \(8D_g^{46}<A^{46}\), \((73/10)D_f^{46}<A^{46}\) contradict the inherited equal-ball threshold. Thus in either case there is an actual \(c\) outside the forbidden union and the resulting lattice has minimum squared norm 16. The determinant does not depend on which certified shear offsets are chosen. Use of closed balls and strict cover-density inequalities gives strict distances wherever positive horizontal clearance was required.

## Exact comparison and replay

The two squared tail covolumes are
\[
\det G_g=\frac{3578813470795833160453541}{50000000000000000000000},\qquad
\det G_f=\frac{128730411665656171}{1800000000000000}.
\]
The first (approximately \(71.5762694\)) is larger than the second (approximately \(71.5168954\)) and below the [live verified predecessor](../mw92_paired_cover_9495_20261126/result.json), whose worst squared tail covolume is \(644703354722796061/9000000000000000\) (blob 6c3c84b10b4ed611ea9a89ceebff16118c5d7d92). Their total lattice covolumes are \(47^7\sqrt{\det G_g}\) and \(47^7\sqrt{\det G_f}\). The radius-2 occupied density floor thus uses
\(2^{143}\pi^{47}/(95!!\,47^7\sqrt{\max(\det G_g,\det G_f)})\).
The unchanged derived Shioda MW92 \(\times A_3\) product has squared tail covolume \(2048\); it is a comparison, not an attribution of this 95D numerical bound to Shioda.
