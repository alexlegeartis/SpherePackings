# The periodic equality case in the 22D canonical projection

In the notation of [ANTICODE.md](ANTICODE.md), let Y⊂P=Λ22* be any nonempty full-rank-periodic packing with squared minimum μ≤4 in this fixed Euclidean realization. If it attains the Conway–Sloane density, then Y is a translate of the original three-coset V22 or its central inverse. This is NOT a claim about arbitrary 22D packings; nor does it classify infinite aperiodic arrangements attaining the same upper density.

The strict first and third branches in the anticode bound imply μ=11/3 and a selected fraction exactly 1/4 of the sites of P. Full-rank periods belong to P (subtract a point of Y from its translate), hence form a finite-index period group in P. In every translate in P of every four-point anticode S⊂P of squared diameter ≤3 there is at most one selected point. Averaging the count over P modulo the period group gives 4·(1/4)=1. Therefore EVERY translate hits exactly once. This finite-torus average counts marked stencil positions with multiplicity; feasibility prevents any multiplicity from a selected point at congruent distinct stencil positions.

Now if S and S' are two such tetrahedra differing only in their fourth vertex u versus v, for every t∈P subtract their identical unit counts to obtain 1_Y(t+u)=1_Y(t+v). Thus v−u is a period of Y. The standalone verifier [verify_periodic_equality.py](verify_periodic_equality.py) gives **23 explicit pairs** of tetrahedra. It independently checks all raw vertices (including sign-modified ones) belong to the Leech lattice, that their projections are four distinct points at pairwise squared distance exactly 3 for both tetrahedra, and that exactly three of their projected vertices coincide. In each pair the displacement has zero normal component: its first three raw coordinates are equal. The 23 displacements consequently belong to L=Λ22 and are forced periods. Raw horizontal coordinates are (x₂,x₃,…,x₂₃) with x₀=x₁=x₂. The conversion to physical covolume multiplies any raw determinant by √3/8^11, because the first raw coordinate repeats three times and the overall linear length scale is 1/√8. Since covol(L)=√12, the whole L has raw determinant 2·8^11=2^34. Euclidean integer row elimination gives exactly 2^34 for the rank-22 sublattice generated by the 23 explicit differences; hence they span ALL of L, and L is a period of Y.

There are therefore exactly three whole cosets of L among the twelve in P. To determine all allowed triples use the actual twelve projected anticode points, not a lower spacing estimate. For the normal basis 4(e₀−e₁)/√8,4(e₁−e₂)/√8 of √2 A₂, its Gram is [[4,−2],[−2,4]] and determinant 12. Dot coordinates of a raw Leech vector are p=(x₀−x₁)/2,q=(x₁−x₂)/2. Its label in K*/K is ((4p+2q) mod12,(2p+4q) mod12), the adjugate times the integral dot pair; equal labels mean equal L cosets. The twelve points meet all twelve labels once. From the zero mark, three nonzero labels have actual squared-distance witness 3 and two have an actual witness 8/3, so these five differences are forbidden in an equality triple. The other six display 11/3 witnesses. Direct enumeration of the 220 label triples gives exactly 24 whose three pairwise differences avoid the five forbidden labels: precisely the translates and negatives of the label triple represented in first-three-raw coordinates by (0,0,0), (2,0,0), (0,−2,0). This is the original Conway–Sloane triangle which is feasible by their construction, so all 24 candidates indeed give equality and are translates/inverses of one packing.

The checker is self-contained pure Python; the explicit replacements and 12-point anticode together require under 5 KB. The larger 29-replacement construction per vertex is not needed. The checker here needs no group classification, no raw coordinate download, and no optimality hypothesis about the Leech lattice (only its explicit realization and covolume one).
