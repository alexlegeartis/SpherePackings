# What whole horizontal stocks can do in dimensions 16–18

Sphere 142, 27 November 2026. No density record. Two different constructions were tested against the public table pinned at [499b0411](https://github.com/alexlegeartis/SpherePackings/tree/499b0411c3c3d1e7399fafbbd53b78e79c2e679f). All densities below are **center densities**, namely radius to the power dimension times centers per unit volume. The actual packing fraction multiplies by the unit ball volume. The 16D and 17D targets are both $1/16$ (Barnes–Wall and $\Lambda_{17}$); see [Leech–Sloane for 16D](https://neilsloane.com/doc/Me14.pdf), [Leech 1964](https://doi.org/10.4153/CJM-1964-065-1), and [Conway–Sloane](https://annals.math.princeton.edu/1982/116-3/p07) for laminations. The 18D comparison $(3/4)^9$ is the construction of [Bierbrauer–Edel](https://emis.de/ft/43246) and is sufficient here. None of these comparisons asserts global optimality.

## 1. A mixed 16D D4-normal lift and an exact nonlinear equality packing

Take $D_4=\{z\in\mathbb Z^4:\sum z_i\equiv0\pmod 2\}$, with normal positions $z/\sqrt2$ and normal covolume $1/2$. At each normal position put either no centers or an arbitrary translate of one of the full horizontal stocks $W+2\mathbb Z^{12}$ and $H+2\mathbb Z^{12}$, with the same coordinate axes. Here $W$ is the concrete 144-word Witt/Julin stock (Hadamard pair differences and complements, six disjoint weight-two pairs and their complements), and $H$ is the 128-word binary code whose parity-check columns are the five-bit labels $16,\ldots,27$. Minimum required squared distance is 4. The 14D motivation had been to trade eight ordinary $H$ sites for the extra weight at each $W$ site; this ratio follows from the respective gains 16 and losses 128. In this 16D problem the exact maximum is $1/16$, even without periodic placement of the types or horizontal shifts.

**Horizontal distance facts.** The tiny checker gives literal $W,H$. It verifies that $W+H$ modulo 2 is the complete even parity set, that $W+W$ includes all vectors of weights 0, 4, 8, 12, and that every nonzero even syndrome of $H$ has a two-bit representative (syndrome zero has the zero representative). Internal minima are 4 in both stocks. To compute a covering distance from a point in the torus, let its nearest binary corner have weight $w$ and coordinate errors $\delta_i\in[0,1/2]$. Flipping a set $B$ of bits contributes $\sum_i\delta_i^2+\sum_{i\in B}(1-2\delta_i)$ to squared torus distance.

* For $W+H$, this is distance to the even-sum integer lattice. Let $M=\max_i\delta_i$. If a parity correction is needed, take its cheapest bit: the upper bound $12M^2+1-2M$ is at most 3 by convexity on $[0,1/2]$, with equality only when all coordinates have error $1/2$. Without correction the same conclusion follows from the sum of squares.
* For $W+W$, use only the four certified weight layers above; complement so $w\le6$. For $w=0,4$ no correction is needed. For $w=1$ or $2$, with probability $1/4$ remove all current ones, and otherwise add $4-w$ uniformly from the zeros. For $w=3$ add one uniformly; for $w=5$ remove one uniformly; for $w=6$ choose with equal probability to remove two uniformly or add two uniformly. Each coordinate has flip probability $p\le1/4$, so its averaged cost $\delta^2+p(1-2\delta)$ is at most $1/4$ (check the endpoints of the convex function). Thus the covering square is at most 3. For equality, every coordinate with $p<1/4$ must have $\delta=1/2$. The only $p=1/4$ coordinates occur for $w=1,2$ among the current ones. The all-add correction then shows strict inequality if any of those has $\delta<1/2$. Consequently all-half modulo $\mathbb Z^{12}$ is the only maximizing fractional phase in either stock difference.

**Normal link.** Every normal unit edge out of a $W$ site must have the maximizing half-integral difference phase. All occupied vertices of its link therefore have the same coordinate fractions. For an edge within this link, no further $W$ can be an endpoint, since it would need a half-integral difference; two $H$ endpoints require odd integral sum of their difference, since an even integral difference can be brought within squared distance 2 by the syndrome fact. The occupied link is bipartite by integral parity. Each independent set in it is a collection of unit vectors in $\mathbb R^4$ with pairwise inner products at most zero; this follows because any positive inner product among distinct $D_4$ unit roots is $1/2$, exactly the link edge. An independent $m$-set has Gram $G$ with $\operatorname{tr}G^2\le2m$ by $a^2\le-a$ for $a\in[-1,0]$ and $\boldsymbol1^\top G\boldsymbol1\ge0$. Hence $m^2\le4\operatorname{tr}G^2\le8m$. At most 16 of the 24 root neighbors of any $W$ site are occupied. Each vacant site can be charged by at most 24 $W$ sites. Double count in expanding boxes (the lattice boundary is $O(R^3)$ for a side-$R$ box). If $n_W,n_0$ count $W$ and vacant sites, then $n_0\ge n_W/3-O(R^3)$. Relative to all-$H$, total horizontal stock count changes by $16n_W-128n_0$, nonpositive up to boundary. Thus even the limiting upper density is at most $128/(2^{12}(1/2))=1/16$.

**Literal equality construction which is not a lattice.** All sites use $H$, but the gluing is nonlinear. Write $e=z_0+z_1\bmod2$, $\nu=z-e(1,0,1,0)$, and
\[
 k=((\nu_0+\nu_1)/2,(\nu_0-\nu_1)/2,
      (\nu_2+\nu_3)/2,(\nu_2-\nu_3)/2)\in\mathbb Z^4.
\]
Put $p=\sum k_i\bmod2$ and let $a=\bigoplus_{i:k_i\text{ odd}}i\in\{0,1,2,3\}$; when $p=1$ swap $1$ and $2$ in this last label. The stock is the coset of syndrome $a\mathbin{\oplus}(28p)$, with an additional $e\,\boldsymbol1/2$ translate. The two normal types have every horizontal difference half integral, costing 3. Within each type, squared normal distances are $\|\Delta k\|^2$; a norm-1 difference changes the parity flag, forcing the horizontal syndrome into the omitted set $28,\ldots,31$ and therefore cost at least 3. Norm 2 toggles two coordinates with distinct two-bit labels, giving a nonzero even syndrome and cost 2; this remains so under the parity-wise swap. Norm 3 has odd parity, so cost at least 1. This also gives a direct independent derivation of the verifier's finite check of all short normal differences. The period contains raw $4\mathbb Z^4$ and horizontal $2\mathbb Z^{12}$. It contains the origin but is not closed under sums: the same-type points above raw $(1,1,0,0)$ and $(1,-1,0,0)$ have exclusive-or of syndromes different from that above $(2,0,0,0)$. A lattice translate containing zero would be closed under sums. The construction only matches the incumbent; we do not claim its isometry class is new.

## 2. A universal cover for two-bank 16D perfect-code fibers

Let $C,C'$ be arbitrary even-parity binary length-16 size-2048 codes of Hamming minimum 4. They need not be linear. The two-bank horizontal stock
\[
 P=(C+2\mathbb Z^{16})\ \cup\ 
       (\boldsymbol1/2+C'+2\mathbb Z^{16})
\]
has squared minimum 4 (the across-bank squared distance is at least 4) and unit-radius center density $1/16$. Its **covering radius squared is at most 3**. The farthest hole in one bank cannot be far from the other.

Indeed, a perfectness count says each odd word has a unique Hamming-1 neighbor in $C$: the $2048\cdot16=32768$ covered words are disjoint. Round the point to a nearest integer word $y$ with coordinate distances $\delta_i\in[0,1/2]$, $S=\sum\delta_i$ and $Q=\sum\delta_i^2\le S/2$. If $y\in C\bmod2$ the squared distance is at most $Q$; if $y$ is odd it is at most $Q+1$. If $y$ is even and not in the bank, each odd Hamming-1 neighbor of $y$ matches a codeword at Hamming distance exactly 2 from $y$. The resulting correction pairs partition the 16 coordinates: each such codeword accounts for exactly the two odd neighbors obtained from its two flipped positions. Averaging the eight costs bounds the squared distance by $Q+2-S/4$. Thus a bank covers within 3 whenever $S\le4$. In the half-translated bank the sum becomes $8-S$, so at least one bank meets this condition.

This covers arbitrary normal stacking of these complete layers, with independently rotated or translated horizontal stocks, no periodicity requirement. If the squared contact requirement is $\mu>3$, two normal sites must be spaced by at least $\sqrt{\mu-3}$: pick a point in one stock and use covering in the other. Consequently for $k=1,2$ normal dimensions the $(16+k)$-dimensional center density is at most
\[
 B_k\frac{(\mu/4)^{(16+k)/2}}{(\mu-3)^{k/2}},\qquad
 B_1=1/16,\quad B_2=1/(8\sqrt3).
\]
The constants are the actual one-dimensional spacing capacity and planar triangular packing bound (normal torus periodicity is not required). The uniform horizontal box-count error is a boundary error, even with varying rotations and code choices, since each fundamental cube has the same side and radius and bounded stock count. In particular, for $\mu=4$ this matches the 17D benchmark and gives only $1/(8\sqrt3)$ in 18D, strictly less than $(3/4)^9$. Logarithmic differentiation shows the formula is nondecreasing on $[3(16+k)/16,4]$; the same no-improvement statement therefore holds throughout this interval.

The 17D equality case can be made literal. Use the extended Hamming code with columns $16,\ldots,31$ in both banks. Let $S=\{0,\ldots,7\}$ and $x=\boldsymbol1_S/2+e_0+e_8$. The distance squared to each bank is exactly 3 (direct integer verifier). To see the lower bound, the half-integral free bits within one eight-column block span only the syndromes of that block: the parity and the value-8 syndrome bit cannot correct the single odd integral bit in the complementary block. This requires an integral flip, in addition to $8/4=2$; the one offending bit can be flipped and the others chosen to give codeword zero. For the other bank the roles reverse. Translating successive rows by $x$ at unit normal spacing is a packing; all nonconsecutive rows have vertical distance at least 2.

## 3. A different 17D gate left open

The [catalogued Plesken–Pohst $\Lambda_{16}.2$](https://www.math.rwth-aachen.de/~Gabriele.Nebe/LATTICES/LAMBDA16.2.html) has Gram determinant 512 and minimum 4. A shift $x$ with every step $h$ normal gives center density $1/(\sqrt{512}h)$, not the two-bank geometry above. It beats the 17D target if $h^2<1/2$, provided
\[
 h^2\ge\max\{4-d(x,L)^2,\;(4-d(2x,L)^2)/4,\;4/9\}.
\]
The third and further spacings then certify themselves. For example, $d(x,L)^2\ge32/9$ and $d(2x,L)^2\ge20/9$ would permit $h^2=4/9$ and a density gain of $3/(2\sqrt2)-1$. A hosted numerical discriminant/ray scout only reached the boundary value $(3.5,2)$ jointly, not a certified improved candidate. It does not bound the continuous covering radius or rule out nonadditive/heterogeneous layers. The two exact theorems above concern only their explicitly fixed stocks/scales, not these alternatives or the unrestricted problem.

A boundary packing is exact: let $x=a/8$ in catalogue basis, where $a=(6,4,6,4,4,4,4,4,4,0,4,0,0,0,0,4)$. Integer $LDL^\top$ sphere enumeration certifies $d(x,L)^2=7/2$ (1,152 witnesses) and $d(2x,L)^2=2$ (30 witnesses), and also independently confirms determinant 512 and horizontal minimum 4. The fully rational enumerator computes $L,D$ exactly, clears denominators, then recursively bounds each remaining integer coordinate by the exact integer square root of its nonnegative remaining squared norm. Thus $h^2=1/2$ gives the incumbent density, not an improvement.

The first-shell displacement vectors about $x$ have zero mean and span all 16 dimensions (rank modulo the prime 101 suffices and is replayed). For every nonzero perturbation direction, one of these shell vectors has strictly negative derivative of squared distance: their mean zero rules out all derivatives nonnegative unless all are zero, and their span rules out the latter. The largest possible minimum derivative over unit perturbations is strictly negative by compactness. The common quadratic term is second order, so this is an isolated local maximum for $d(x,L)$; no sufficiently small perturbation of this step can reach the positive gate. This is a local statement only.
