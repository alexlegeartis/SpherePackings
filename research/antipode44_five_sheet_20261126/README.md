# Sharp 44D antipode certificate for at most five projected cosets

This is optimality **inside the normal-diameter certificate** with at most five sheets. It is not an upper bound on 44D packings, nor on the true distances of the same periodic sets. The known incumbent is the Chen–Hu–Li–Wang–Wu construction [New Sphere Packings from the Antipode Construction](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.02394); its center-density square is exactly 25(157/110)^44/605. Source: [Cohn's table](https://cohn.mit.edu/sphere-packing/) and the cited authors, not a new density record.

Let an even unimodular 48-dimensional parent have minimum 6; let K be its primitive rank-four normal section, whose complementary period has the same covolume V. More generally the proof only uses that K is even integral of minimum at least 6. For any M≤5 distinct classes of K*/K of squared torus diameter β<6, the ordinary antipode certificate gives center density M ((6−β)/4)^22 / V. This quantity is ≤ the incumbent; for equality the effective normal and packet Gram are unique up to a change of basis/permutation. Actual class distances may be better than the formula guarantees.

**Coherent lifts and degenerate clusters.** Hermite’s sharp four-dimensional bound gives V≥18. Even M=5 cannot match the record at β=2/3 or above. Choose shortest representatives about a common origin in the packet; each pair difference then agrees with its shortest lift because a discrepancy is a nonzero period of length ≤3√β<√6. Let r be the affine rank after lifting. Put Z=K* intersect the span of all packet differences, W=K intersect the orthogonal space. Primitivity or a basis extension gives 1/V=covol(Z)/covol(W). The respective squared lower bounds on covol(W) for ranks 1,2,3,4 are 6,27,108,324 by Hermite.

For r=0 there is one point. For r=1 the diameter of M distinct lattice sites is at least (M−1)covol(Z). For r=2,3 take independent difference edges of a simplex from the packet. The parallelepiped volume is at least covol(Z), and at most sqrt(r+1)(β/2)^(r/2), by the regular-simplex diameter-volume bound (center the r+1 vertices, bound the trace of the covariance by the sum of pairwise squared distances, then use AM–GM for its eigenvalues). These bounds already suffice for M≤4. For five sites of rank two, triangulate their convex hull using all five lattice sites; there are at least three nondegenerate triangles, each of area ≥covol(Z)/2, all inside a bounding square of area ≤β. Thus covol(Z)≤2β/3. For five sites of rank three, if any nonzero tetrahedral determinant in lattice units is ≥2 in absolute value, halve the simplex bound. Otherwise the signed maximal minors of the augmented affine matrix span its unique dependence and belong to {-1,0,1}. The coefficients sum to zero and not all five can be nonzero; support two would identify distinct sites. The support is four, two plus and two minus. Thus there is a parallelogram and an off-plane apex. Its area is half the cross product of its diagonals, at most β/2; apex altitude is at most √β. Therefore covol(Z)≤β^(3/2)/2. In every rank r≤3 maximize β^(r/2)(6−β)^22 at β=6r/(44+r). The attached exact checks put each bound strictly below the incumbent.

**Full rank, hence exactly five.** Move one vertex to zero and denote the four independent edges by h_i. Their span in K* has index j. Let v_i be j times the basis dual to the h_i in Euclidean space; define v5=−Σv_i. These five vectors belong to K (adjugate in a basis), are nonzero, and constitute j times the facet normals. If A is the area (3-volume) of a tetrahedral facet, the four-simplex has altitude j/(6VA); hence ||v_i||=6VA for the corresponding facet. The maximal tetrahedral volume at diameter √β gives ||v_i||²≤V²β³/2. Any matching-or-better certificate then requires
```
  ||v_i||² ≤ 25 β³ ((6−β)/4)^44 / (2δ₀²).
```
The maximum at β=18/47 is <8.31. All five norms are even and thus 6 or 8. For an incident edge to the opposite vertex, the scalar product with its corresponding v_i has absolute value j, so j²≤β||v_i||². If j≥2, then β≥1/2; the displayed cap thereafter decreases, and at 1/2 it is below 8. All five then have norm 6, giving β≥2/3, already impossible. Therefore j=1 and the facet normals form a genuine lattice superbase.

**Exact enumeration and equality.** Relabel the five diagonal values in nondecreasing order. For v1 through v4 enumerate their symmetric Gram matrix G, where |g_ij|≤(g_ii+g_jj−6)/2 by the minima of v_i±v_j. Determine the last off-diagonal by the required squared norm of v5, impose its pair bounds too, and discard nonpositive matrices. The resulting relaxed superset contains 25,274 matrices. Put B=adj(G), d=det G, and t=max of B_ii and B_ii+B_jj−2B_ij: β=t/d. Integer powering compares (6d−t)^44/d^45 with 3454^44/605^45. None exceed the target. The 12 labelled equalities all have five norms 6 and reduced Gram the Laplacian of the complete five-vertex graph with the five cycle-edges doubled, up to simultaneous permutation. Then d=605, β=176/605=16/55. This is the realized Chen et al. family at δ₀=509.618982642864…. A different ordinary or torus packet needs at least six sheets to improve via this certificate, or one must certify a stronger true horizontal separation, use non-discriminant shifts, or change the parent construction. The theorem does not assert any six-sheet gain.
