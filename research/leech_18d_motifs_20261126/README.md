# 18D: two exact Leech-section motifs and the missing seventh layer

November 26, 2026. No density change. The [pinned project row](https://github.com/alexlegeartis/SpherePackings/blob/b7ba41f68e8625c31278b581655d1eaa5a6f0d86/LOWER_BOUNDS_1_96.md) is [Bierbrauer–Edel's \(B_{18}\)](https://emis.de/ft/43246), center density \((3/4)^9=0.075084686279296875\) and occupied density approximately \(0.00616789812533125\). In the [18D remark of Chen–Hu–Li–Wang–Wu](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.02394v1#S3), an antipode packing has center density \(5^9/(2^8 3^{21/2})\approx0.0745962334057\); the remark does not specify their normal embedding. We independently recover **that density** with a six-coset motif and prove it is the exact optimum on our projected period for unions of complete canonical cosets. We do not claim isometry, precedence, or an upper bound on general 18D packings.

## The constructive target

For a primitive six-dimensional sublattice \(K\) of the Leech lattice \(\Lambda\), take \(L=\Lambda\cap K^\perp\), covolume \(\sqrt{\det K}\), and the complete translates of \(L\) in \(\pi_{K^\perp}\Lambda\). These canonical cosets are labeled by the discriminant group \(K^*/K\). For \(D=\det K\) and \(m\) selected translates with least squared separation \(\mu\), the center density is \(m(\mu/4)^9/\sqrt D\). At \(D=243\), a seven-coset packet at \(\mu=10/3\) would beat \(B_{18}\) by 15.9%; keeping six would require \(\mu>3.3357514735\ldots\). Thus after reverse-engineering the near tie, the search targeted new short-shell patterns around the normal, not small perturbations of the published \(B_{18}\) stock.

The other candidate mechanism considered was a third independent residue band in the binary \(B_{18}\) construction. With inherited squared minimum 24 and fixed \(4D_{18}\) period, 1025 instead of 1024 centers would suffice; however using only the two original parity bands with different binary sheet functions still forces orthogonal spans of the two sets of code differences (subtract the four cross-band parity equations on a rectangle). The Leech approach offers the larger identifiable gain and was tested first.

## Two explicit normal embeddings and exact horizontal distances

[bases.json](bases.json) gives 24-coordinate numerator rows for two normal bases, physical coordinates divided by \(\sqrt8\). Their Gram matrices are integrally equivalent to \(3G_{E_6}^{-1}\) and \(2G_{E_6}\), with \(D=243,192\), respectively. These are the normal isometry types of [Höhn–Mason nos. 35 and 33](https://arxiv.org/html/1505.06420v3); exact coordinate embeddings here do not rely on those data. The first basis comes from one \((4,4,0^{22})\), one \((-3,1^{23})\) with its \(-3\) in a disjoint coordinate, and four signed Golay octads with the required pairwise dot products. The second comes from an \(A_5\) chain of five \((4,-4)\) differences on six sites of an octad followed by one signed copy of that octad.

For the six physical normal rows \(B_i\), let \(z_i=\langle B_i,y\rangle\), \(G=\operatorname{Gram}(B)\), \(J=\operatorname{adj}(G)\). The label is \(Jz\bmod D\), and its least normal squared length \(q\) is \(\min z^\top Jz/D\) over representatives. All \(D\) labels and their minima are found in \([-3,3]^6\); none larger than \(8/3\), so \(|z_i|^2\le4q\) excludes any shorter representative outside that box. The exact normal spectrum, displayed as (least squared normal norm: count), is

- \(D=243\): 0:1, \(2/3\):72, \(4/3\):90, 2:80.
- \(D=192\): 0:1, \(2/3\):54, 1:36, \(5/3\):72, 2:27, \(8/3\):2.

A complete norm-four Leech enumeration finds a lift at the minimum normal norm of **every label in both spectra**, thereby proving primitivity: projection hits every class of \(K^*/K\), already contains \(K\), hence equals \(K^*\), equivalent to saturation in the unimodular parent. For a nonzero label reduce the normal part of any lift to its shortest representative by subtracting a vector in \(K\), leaving the horizontal part unchanged. The Leech minimum then gives \(h\ge4-q\) for its horizontal squared norm; the enumerated witnesses give equality. The origin coset itself has minimum exactly 4. No cover-radius estimate is substituted for these true intercoset distances.

## Exact all-radii ceilings on the two explicit carriers

For \(D=243\) the group is a five-dimensional ternary vector space. At \(8/3<\mu\le10/3\) the allowed differences must be the 72 labels with \(q=2/3\), the level 1 of the quadratic form \(Q=(3q)/2\bmod3\). After translating one vertex to zero, seven vertices would give six vectors in dimension five with bilinear Gram matrix \(2\mathbf1\mathbf1^\mathsf T-I\), which is invertible over \(\mathbb F_3\) for six vectors. Thus the packet has at most six sites, and [verify.py](verify.py) checks the explicit six-site packet. At \(2<\mu\le8/3\) the forbidden isotropic differences form an 80-regular Cayley graph with eigenvalues \(80,8,-1,-10\), computed exactly by ternary characters. The Hoffman bound gives at most 27 sites. At \(\mu>10/3\) only a singleton is possible; at \(\mu\le2\) use the trivial cap 243. The largest density across these intervals is the stated six-coset value.

For \(D=192\) the quotas for \(\mu\) in the intervals \((10/3,4]\), \((3,10/3]\), \((7/3,3]\), \((2,7/3]\), and \((0,2]\) are respectively 1, 3, 9, 24, 192. The two small clique numbers are computed exactly. For the 24 bound split the group \(E_6^*/2E_6\) into the three sectors of \(E_6^*/E_6\). Within one sector only root differences (\(q=1\)) are allowed as \(\mu>2\). The resulting binary quadratic space has dimension six; a root-difference clique of nine would have eight differences from a common origin with alternating Gram \(I+\mathbf1\mathbf1^\mathsf T\), invertible over \(\mathbb F_2\), so each sector has at most eight sites. The resulting exact maximum center density is the singleton \(1/\sqrt{192}=0.0721687836487\ldots\), again below \(B_{18}\).

Replay: `python3 -B verify.py`. The normal-minimum census and small clique certificates do not use a numerical optimizer.

## Bounded constructive search beyond the two carriers

One-vector replacements of the two normal bases cover 931 sampled embeddings at determinants 192–700, with sign/basis symmetries used to avoid simple duplication. The exact ambient norm-four census found 3868 per-embedding class occurrences **without** a norm-four lift; the discrete lower metric was strengthened to \(6-q\) for each such class. For every tested packing-radius breakpoint with \(\mu\ge3\) we compared the graph's required motif size against \(B_{18}\) by the integer inequality \(m^2(\mu D)^{18}>D(3D)^{18}\). No witness out of 6129 clique tests, with zero node-limit stops. This tests the certified norm-four/norm-six lower metric for those sampled embeddings; it does not determine all the true distances of missing-lift classes, cover all replacement embeddings, or exhaust other normals and smaller radii. A separate hosted normal-diameter scout across all 32 published rank-six fixed-lattice Grams (Höhn–Mason nos. 33–64) yielded no positive candidate at \(\mu\ge3\); 12 graph budgets stopped that scout early, so it is not a theorem. Neither probe enters the two complete-coset proofs.
