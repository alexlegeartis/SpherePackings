# A three-layer packing in dimension 55

The packing has squared minimum 10 and center density **92,197.2234049774…**.
Its occupied density is at least **7.54599036285564e-11**, an increase of
**0.9452%** over the preceding verified packing. It maximizes density among
periodic full layers with offsets on the line through $Q$, allowing arbitrary
layer spacings and sphere radii. The new center density is exactly `15/19`
of the figure printed in Leterrier’s thesis. The thesis figure remains the
challenge target. [Previous packing and source audit](../cross_coset55_20261116/PROOF.md).

The packing is also a strict local maximum among three-translate 55D
packings with arbitrary period and motif perturbations. The proof counts
2,499,741 closest points in the parent coset and checks positive stresses
on the active contacts. [Exact contact count and local optimality](LOCAL_OPTIMUM.md).

## Construction and minimum

Let $L$ be Leterrier’s 54D lattice of squared minimum 10 and Gram determinant
$3^{25}$. The order-three coset $Q+L$ and its negative have squared minimum
$D=28/3$, attained by $Q$ and $-Q$. The verifier reconstructs the published
parent and binds $Q$ to its Gram. [Published 54D construction](https://doi.org/10.1007/s40993-022-00321-0) · [Height and coset inputs](../cross_coset55_20261116/PROOF.md#55d-interpolate-between-two-nontrivial-cosets).

Set $h=\sqrt{2/3}$. The period lattice is generated by $(L,0)$ and
$(Q/5,(19/5)h)$. Place translates at $(0,0)$, $(Q,h)$ and $(2Q,2h)$.
The repeating vertical gaps are $h,h,(9/5)h$. Adding $3Q\in L$ to the
period shift makes the horizontal offsets between consecutive layers
$Q,Q,(6/5)Q$.

For a horizontal offset $sQ$, reduce $s$ modulo 3. When $0\le s\le 1$,
the minimum of $sQ+L$ is $Ds^2$, since the origin remains nearest along the
segment from $0$ to $Q$. Reflection gives the corresponding formula for
$2\le s\le 3$. When $s=1+a$ with $0\le a\le 1$, apply
\[
\|w+aQ\|^2=(1-a)\|w\|^2+a\|w+Q\|^2-a(1-a)D\ \ge\ D\bigl(1-a(1-a)\bigr)
\]
to $w\in Q+L$. This uses the minima of both nonzero cosets.
The verifier supplies a vector $w$ with $\|w\|^2=\|w+Q\|^2=D$, so the
formula is attained throughout the middle segment.

| Number of consecutive gaps | Squared distances across the gaps are at least |
|---:|---|
| 1 | $10$, $10$, $10$ |
| 2 | $12$, $56/5$, $56/5$ |
| 3 | $10$, $10$, $10$ |

Four or more gaps contribute at least $16h^2>10$ from the vertical
coordinate alone. Within a layer the minimum is inherited from $L$.
The period vector has squared norm 10, which also gives attainment.

## Optimality for layers with offsets on the line through $Q$

Let $\mu$ be any proposed positive squared separation. Since each layer is
a full translate of $L$, $\mu\le10$. Let $H=(19/5)h$ be our period height.
An arbitrary layer at height $z$ and offset $cQ$ gives a point
$(\sqrt D\,c,z)$ on a cylinder of circumference $3\sqrt D$. The planar
packing obtained by unrolling and repeating the cylinder has squared
separation at least $\mu$. Indeed, the horizontal distance to the nearest
representative on the cylinder uses the displacement by some multiple of
$3Q\in L$, which is one of the admissible differences between the full layers.
The planar hexagonal bound caps the number of layers per unit height at
$6\sqrt D/(\sqrt 3\,\mu)$. For $\mu/10\le149/150$, the square of the ratio
of the resulting 55D density bound to our density is at most
$(149/150)^{53} D H^2/75<1$. [Planar bound](https://cohn.mit.edu/sphere-packing/#data).

For the remaining radii, write $d=15-3\mu/2\in[0,1/10]$ and measure
heights in units of $h$. Each gap $u$ is at least $u_0=\sqrt{1-d}$ by the
horizontal maximum $D$. Suppose three consecutive gaps have total
$U<U_0=19/5-3d$. Each then satisfies $u<U_0-2u_0\le9/5-d$.
Put $v=(u^2-u_0^2)/14\in[0,4/25]$. The exact horizontal distance formula
forces the offset across this gap, modulo 3, within
$b(v)=(1-\sqrt{1-4v})/2$ of $+1$ or $-1$.
On the outer intervals the tighter error bound is $1-\sqrt{1-v}\le v\le b(v)$.
In particular every error is at most $1/5$.

An adjacent pair with opposite signs has total height at most $14/5$ and
offset within $2/5$ of zero modulo 3. Its squared distance in units of
$h^2$ is at most $(14/5)^2+14(2/5)^2<149/10$. This violates the required
value $15-d$. Thus the three signs must agree. The error bound
$u\mapsto b((u^2-u_0^2)/14)$ is increasing and convex, and vanishes at
$u_0$. The sum of its values over the three gaps is at most its value at
$U-2u_0$, by convexity applied to their nonnegative excesses over $u_0$.
It is therefore at most $1/5$. The distance between the endpoints of all
three gaps is at most $U^2+14/25<U_0^2+14/25\le15-d$ in the same units.
This contradiction gives a mean gap of at least $U_0/3$.

Writing $x=d/15\le1/150$, the geometric sum identity gives
$1-(1-x)^{15}\ge15(149/150)^{14}x>(225/19)x$ for $x>0$.
It follows that $(1-x)^{55/2}\,(19/5)/U_0\le1$, which is the bound on the
55D density ratio from the mean gap. At $d=0$ the displayed packing attains
the gap bound. These arguments allow any number of layers per period.
