# Quaternary-code lattice packings in 29 and 30 dimensions

Two explicit lattices attain the same densities as the **unrestricted lower
bounds already listed** for 29D and 30D. They improve the lattice lower
bounds, not the unrestricted ones.

| Dimension | Exact center density | Occupied density at least | Gain over our shifted Bacher lattice | Gain over Cohn's listed lattice |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 29 | `1/sqrt(2)` | 3.4144646907422e-5 | 1.55048% | 22.47448% |
| 30 | `1` | 2.1915353447830e-5 | 28.08688% | 51.88782% |

The [preceding lattices](../bacher_slip_29_30_20261122/) have center
densities `4/sqrt(33)` and `8/sqrt(105)`. [Cohn’s lattice
table](https://cohn.mit.edu/sphere-packing/#lattice-data) instead lists
B29 and Q30, of center densities `1/sqrt(3)` and `3^(27/2)/2^22`.
The unrestricted comparisons are Vardy's B29* and T30
([Vardy, 1999](https://doi.org/10.1090/S0002-9947-99-02169-8)).
This is a density tie with those constructions, not a claim that these
packings are isometric, unrestricted optima, or exhaustive priority.

## Construction in 30D

Label the coordinates by the nonzero-index subset `{2,3,...,31}` of
the binary five-space, with bits ordered as in [witness.json](witness.json).
Let T be the binary code with parity-check columns `(1,j)` for those
labels. The columns span dimension six; they are distinct and each
has first entry one, so T has dimension 24 and minimum Hamming weight
at least four.

For each of the six rows specified by a pair of bit masks in the witness,
take the quaternary word `x + 2z`. Let B be the additive code modulo four
generated by these rows and all words of `2T`. The binary residues of the
six rows are independent and belong to T, so every word of B has a unique
expression as a binary choice of the lifted rows plus a word of `2T`.
In particular `|B| = 2^30`.

The lattice is `L = {u/2 : u in Z^30, u mod 4 in B}`. Its period
`2 Z^30` has covolume `2^30` and index `2^30` in L, so L has covolume
one. It is **not integral**: the first two lifted generators have scaled
dot product congruent to `1/2 mod 1`.

The minimum pre-scaling squared cost in a residue word is its Euclidean
weight: symbols 0, 1, 2, 3 have costs 0, 1, 4, 1 respectively. Every
nonzero binary residue has minimum Euclidean weight at least 16 over
the added words of `2T`. The exact 64-state syndrome dynamic program
shows 58 residue minima of 16, four of 20, and one of 28. The nonzero
even words have weight at least 16 by the minimum of T, and the period
vectors attain squared norm 16 before scaling. Thus the minimum squared
length of L is exactly 4, making its center density one.

## The 29D section

The binary residues at labels 2 and 3 agree in every codeword, so
`e_2-e_3` is a dual vector of L. It is primitive: the torsion word with
support `{2,4,8,14}` is in T and gives a vector in L that pairs to one.
The orthogonal section `y_2=y_3` therefore has covolume `sqrt(2)`.
It inherits minimum squared length at least 4 and contains the frame
vector `2e_4`, so equality holds. This gives center density
`1/sqrt(2)`.

Run `python3 verify.py`. The claims in [results.json](results.json)
