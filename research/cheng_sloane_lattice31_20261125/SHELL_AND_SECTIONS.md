# Further exact properties of the 31D lattice

These are properties of the explicit lattice in this directory, not new density or unrestricted kissing-number bounds. Run `node shell_and_dual.js` to reproduce the finite counts.

There are exactly **156,612** norm-32 vectors. Two exact counts agree:
- A coordinate DP over all 2^17 published coset representatives. For each coordinate v and parity bit b of the multiple of four being added, take the nearest representative in v+4b modulo 8, with multiplicity 2 precisely at residue 4, otherwise 1. Carry forward each total parity’s minimum norm and sum of counts on tied minima. Even/odd nonperiod contributions were 87,168 and 67,584. The period coset contributes 1,860 roots 4(±e_i±e_j), separately.
- Enumerate the 2^16 codewords by generator-row XOR. There are 681 weight-eight words, each giving 128 sign choices at energy 32. For the 24-position odd support T, its seven-position complement has code restriction histogram j=0,2,4,6 of 1,024, 21,504, 35,840, 7,168. On odd cosets, j=0 gives 24 shortest vectors (a single closest ±1 changes to the next signed representative of absolute value 3 to fix the carry), j=2 gives 2 sign choices on the two ±2 coordinates after parity, and j≥4 cannot reach energy 32. Total: 1,860 + 681·128 + 1,024·24 + 21,504·2 = 156,612.

## Two cheap exclusions for next-round search
1. **Continuous deformation is a dead end locally.** The 1,860 D31 roots at the same minimum suffice to make this lattice a strict local density maximum among lattices, up to scale and isometry. Elementary proof: normalize minimum to 32 and express a nearby metric as I+H. The roots require H_ii+H_jj ≥ 2|H_ij| for every i≠j. Summing implies tr H ≥ 0, with equality forcing H=0 (all pair sums zero in n≥3, then all off-diagonals zero). Compactness on the unit sphere of this cone gives tr H ≥ c||H|| for c>0, so det(I+H)>1 for all small nonzero H allowed by the roots. Search for different discrete code/glue rather than a small Gram perturbation.
2. **The same-minimum linear-section route cannot improve 28–30D.** Exhaustive XOR checks of all support sizes 1–7 among the 31 generator columns gave null-syndrome counts 0, 0, 0, 0, 0, 0, 31. Thus the binary dual distance is 7. Write u=x/4 for u∈L*. Because 4D31⊂L, x is integral or all half-integral. The half-integral norm² is at least 31/4. If x is integral, x mod 2 lies in C^⊥ and t·x≡0 modulo 4, where t is the odd support indicator. Hence the only x with 0<||x||²<7 are ±2e_i for the seven positions outside T. For a rank-r dual normal lattice Γ in x coordinates (r=1–3), suppose it contains k of these orthogonal coordinate axes, counted without signs. Project off those axes. Any nonzero projected vector can have a lift reduced to absolute coordinate ≤1 along all k axes, and that lift has norm²≥7, so the projected minimum squared is ≥7−k. The squared covolume of Γ is at least 4^k times the squared covolume of that projection. In ranks 1, 2, 3 use the exact Hermite constants 1, 2/√3, and 2^(1/3), respectively. This gives squared covolume at least 4^r, attained by taking r of the axes in the primitive dual section lattice. Therefore the best possible center densities among *linear lattice sections* at the parent minimum are 1, 1/√2, and 1/2 in 30D, 29D, and 28D. The first two tie the existing repo rows, and 28D is below 2/3. These bounds only cover sections retaining norm² 32. The coordinate sections attain the bounds and do retain that minimum. A higher-minimum section is not excluded by this argument. Higher-minimum sections and alternative discrete glues remain open.
