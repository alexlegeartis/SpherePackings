# A continuous chamber and a contact transition in a 19-dimensional antipode packing

This is a constructive result at the existing 19D center density, not an improved density lower bound. Use the six-coset construction and normal Gram matrix in Chen, Hu, Li, Wang and Wu, *New Sphere Packings from the Antipode Construction*, [dimension 19 in §3](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.02394v1#S3). We use an explicit Golay-coordinate embedding with their Gram matrix, without needing uniqueness of that embedding. The exact standalone verification is in [`verify.js`](verify.js) (Node.js, no imports or packages).

**Result.** There is a one-parameter family of pairwise nonisometric periodic 19D packings, all of squared minimum 52/15 and center density
`6 (13/15)^(19/2)/sqrt(300) = 13^(19/2)/(3^9 5^(21/2))`. Before its explicit endpoint the infinite contact graph has precisely two connected components. At the endpoint it is connected, with 436 extra unoriented contacts per original period cell. The mean number of contacts at a center rises from 4022 to 4167 1/3. No global maximality or priority claim is made.

**Coordinates.** Use the ordinary extended binary Golay model of the Leech lattice, with the integer 24-vectors in the verifier divided by sqrt(8). Let W be the five root rows in the verifier, K their span over the integers, and L the orthogonal Leech section. The script checks that the Gram matrix G of W has determinant 300, with inverse B/60, where
```
21  9  9 12 12
 9 29 13 20 16
 9 13 29 16 20
12 20 16 32 16
12 16 20 16 32
```
is B. It finds a Leech vector of each of the five unit integral pairing profiles with W, so the pairing map to Z^5 is onto. This proves primitivity: an element of the saturated K must pair integrally with the five dual-basis profiles and hence has integral coordinates in W. By unimodularity of the Leech lattice the covolume of L is sqrt(300) (equivalently, the projection onto span(K) has covolume 1/sqrt(300)). Let y_0=0 and y_1,…,y_5 be standard basis vectors indexing the five pairing profiles. The horizontal projection of all Leech points with profile y_i is one L-coset F_i. Write A=F_0 ∪ F_4 ∪ F_5, B'=F_1 ∪ F_2 ∪ F_3.

Let v be the following root of L, in integer coordinates divided by sqrt(8):
```
0 0 0 0 0 2 2 0 0 0 0 0 2 0 -2 2 0 2 0 -2 0 2 0 0
```
It has squared norm 4, hence v/2 is a unit vector. For 0 ≤ s ≤ s*=1−sqrt(19/20), define P_s=A ∪ (B'−s v/2).

**Minimum across every lattice translate.** For a pair of different cosets with oriented profile q=y_j−y_i (j in B', i in A for cross pairs), every horizontal difference before sliding is the projection of an actual Leech vector x of exactly that profile; conversely each such x realizes one difference modulo the period. Write b=qᵀBq. The squared norm before translation is |x|²−b/60. For the six intratriple edges b=32, and there are 2011 norm-four x for each edge. Distinct points in one coset have squared distance at least 4. Thus the internal minimum is exactly 4−32/60=52/15 throughout.

Among the nine cross pairs, b=29 for six and b=21 for three. After the translation the squared norm of the difference is |x|²−b/60−s(x,v)+s². When |x|²=4 the integral inner product (x,v) is at most 2: x has nonzero profile, so x≠v and the Leech minimum applied to x−v gives the bound. The six b=29 classes contain respectively 78,62,78,78,62,78 norm-four vectors with (x,v)=2, in the order (0,2),(0,3),(4,1),(4,3),(5,1),(5,2). For |x|²≥6 the distance after a slide is at least sqrt(6−29/60)−s by the triangle inequality, uniformly strictly greater than sqrt(211/60) on this interval. Consequently the minimum **over all cross differences** is exactly sqrt(211/60−2s+s²); at s* this is sqrt(208/60). Precisely the listed 436 cross differences become contacts. Each enumerated vector gives one unoriented contact per period cell for its fixed distinct pair of colors. There are always 6×2011=12066 internal contacts per cell.

**Infinite connected components and nonisometry.** For one contact class, differences between two norm-four lifts with the identical profile are in L. Two-step walks across that edge and back realize their differences as translations. All of L is generated already for edge (0,4), and also for edge (1,2). In fact, relative to a fixed first lift, the script exhibits three 19-tuples of lift differences with lattice indices 14,6,11 on (0,4), and one tuple with index 1 on (1,2). For 19 such differences F, their index is |det[W;F]|/(300·8^12) using the unscaled integer rows: sqrt(det G)·covol(L)=300, and the ambient determinant rescales by (sqrt(8))^24=8^12. The index of the subgroup generated by all differences divides the gcd, hence is one. This establishes transitivity under the entire period lattice by contact paths, not just connectivity in the six-vertex quotient. The remaining vertex in each triple has contacts to the first two, giving exactly two components for s<s*. At s* a cross contact joins the two, so the graph is connected. For s<s* the distance between the vertex sets of the two components is the strictly decreasing quantity computed above. It is preserved by any Euclidean congruence, even one exchanging the two components. The endpoint is distinguished by connectivity. This proves pairwise nonisometry of the entire interval.

**Next constructive use, not a solved improvement.** The endpoint supplies an exactly certified off-dual 19D source whose relative shifts have a geometric observable rather than a discrete label. Applying an 18D section to different members can align two independent stocks at different levels. Such a proposal requires a fresh density check: a primitive dual normal u, k retained classes and section squared minimum μ give center density k(μ/4)^9/(sqrt(300) |u|). For all six retained at μ=52/15, the required inequality is |u| < 6(13/15)^9/(sqrt(300)(3/4)^9). A first hosted pass over all norm-four normal donors and the original six colors, folding their integer levels onto one plane using the basic antipode distance only, did not beat Bierbrauer–Edel. This does not exclude other section angles, class-dependent closest-shell gains, or transverse motions outside one donor coordinate.
