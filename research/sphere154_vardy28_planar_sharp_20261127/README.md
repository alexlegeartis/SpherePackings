# Sharp 30D planar extension of the fixed Vardy 28D two-bank carrier

**No new density record.** This gives a literal four-phase square extension attaining the **unrestricted \(T_{30}\) comparison**, and an exact obstruction to improving it with the intact \(28\)-dimensional Vardy two-bank carrier. It is a family theorem, not a claim of 30D optimality or of an isometrically new equality construction.

The comparator is [Vardy, *Density doubling, double-circulants, and new sphere packings* (1999)](https://doi.org/10.1090/S0002-9947-99-02169-8), \(T_{30}\) of center density \(1\), occupied density \(2.19153534478302\ldots\times10^{-5}\). See the [current project table](../../LOWER_BOUNDS_1_96.md) and our [matching 30D quaternary lattice](../quaternary_29_30_20261122/). The carrier below is the same as the [verified Vardy-type 29D stack](../vardy28_stack29_20261122/), not the 30D quaternary lattice.

## Stock and exact syndrome geometry

Split 28 coordinates into two blocks of 14. Let \(A\) be the cyclic binary map with columns the cyclic left shifts of the 14-bit word 471, and \(C=\{(x,Ax)\}\), \(D=\{(A^{\mathsf T}y,y)\}\). Both are even \([28,14,8]\) codes and they are dual. With \(D_{28}\) the integer even-sum lattice, the stock is
\[
P=\bigcup_{c\in C}(-2c+4D_{28})\
 \cup\ \bigcup_{d\in D}(\mathbf1-2d+4e_1+4D_{28}).
\]
Its period has volume \(2\cdot4^{28}\), with \(2^{15}\) sites. Its exact minimum squared distance is 32 by a shortest period. Within the same bank, unequal mod-four codewords differ on at least eight coordinates with squared cost four. The two banks have all coordinates odd in their difference. Achieving only \(\pm1\) in all coordinates would require carries whose parity is \(\mathrm{wt}(c)-c\cdot d=0\bmod2\), whereas the \(4e_1\) offset forces odd parity. Thus the opposite banks are at squared distance at least 36 within a layer.

For an even integral horizontal shift \(2b+4k\), encode \(b\bmod2\) as a binary word, put \(d_C(b)=\min_{c\in C}\mathrm{wt}(b+c)\), and similarly \(d_D(b)\). The exact syndrome trellis gives, for **each** code, counts by depth \(0,\ldots,6\) of
\[
(1,28,378,3276,7735,4888,78).
\]
Every depth-six syndrome is even, and the 78 depth-six syndromes are **sum-free** (the sum of any two is not depth six). These are finite statements about the literal maps \(y+Ax\) and \(x+A^{\mathsf T}y\), checked by [verify.js](verify.js). The trellis starts at syndrome zero and includes or excludes each of the 28 parity-check columns; it computes minimum Hamming weights without a coset-representative guess.

For a pair of full stock layers with relative even shift \(2b+4k\), the squared horizontal separation is at most 24. More precisely, if \(d_C(b)>0\), choose a same-\(C\)-bank difference attaining \(4d_C(b)\): every nonzero mismatched even coordinate has two equally cheap signs \(\pm2\), and switching one sign fixes the parity of the \(4D_{28}\) carry. If the Hamming distance is zero, then the carry can cost at most 16 (add one \(\pm4\)); the same conclusions hold for the \(D\) bank. Thus for odd \(b\), at most 20 is available. For even \(b\), clearance greater than 16 requires both syndromes to have depth six, irrespective of \(k\).

## Arbitrary planar normal geometry with even integral phases

Put a full translated copy of \(P\) at each chosen point of the normal plane. There is no assumed normal lattice or period, and the even integral shifts can vary nonlinearly between sites. Normalize the squared packing minimum to \(32\) first. There is at most one phase fiber per normal site, since the horizontal separation of two is at most 24. Give each site a color given by the parity of \(b\) in its phase \(2b+4k\). From the syndrome facts just proved:
1. All normal pair distances are at least \(\sqrt8\).
2. Different colors have normal squared distance at least 12.
3. Any two same-color neighbors of a site, each closer than squared distance 16 to that site, are mutually at squared distance at least 16. Otherwise the three horizontal differences would give a depth-six syndrome triangle in \(C\), contrary to sum-freeness.

Here is a planar area argument that uses these three properties, not a preselected lattice. Clip each normal Voronoi cell by the radius-2 disk centered at its site. A point at distance \(u<4\) from the site removes a cap of angular halfwidth \(\alpha=\arccos(u/4)\) and area \(a(\alpha)=4(\alpha-\sin\alpha\cos\alpha)\). Around the site reserve a sector of angular halfwidth \(\alpha\) for a same-color cap, and only \(\alpha/2\) for a different-color cap. We prove these reserved sectors are pairwise disjoint in their interiors.

* For two same-color caps their neighbor distance is at least 4 by property 3. At angular separation \(\alpha+\beta\) their distance would be \(4\sin(\alpha+\beta)\le4\): represent the two positions at that angle as \(2+2e^{2i\alpha}\), \(2+2e^{-2i\beta}\). Distance increases with the center angle up to \(\pi\), so the angle must be at least \(\alpha+\beta\).
* For two different-color caps their total reserved halfwidth is at most \(\pi/6\). Among any two neighbors of the site inside radius 4, the center angle is at least \(\arccos(3/4)>\pi/6\), by their separation \(\sqrt8\). Indeed the bound \((u^2+v^2-8)/(2uv)\) on the cosine is increasing in both \(u,v\in[\sqrt8,4]\), so it is at most \(3/4\).
* For a mixed pair let \(\alpha\le\pi/4\) be the same-color halfwidth and \(\beta\le\pi/6\) the different-color halfwidth. If \(\alpha<\beta/2\), the reserved sum is \(<\pi/6\) as above. Otherwise consider trial angular separation \(\theta=\alpha+\beta/2\). With distances \(4\cos\alpha,4\cos(\beta/2)\) the chord has length \(4\sin\theta\le\sqrt{12}\). Shortening the latter distance to \(4\cos\beta\) cannot increase this chord: along that ray the distance to the first point increases with radius above \(4\cos\alpha\cos\theta\), and \(4\cos\beta\) is above this threshold because \(\theta\ge\beta\). The two actual neighbors are of different colors, so their mutual distance at least \(\sqrt{12}\) forces angular separation at least \(\theta\).

For \(\alpha\le\pi/4\), \(a(\alpha)/(2\alpha)\le 2-4/\pi\), since \(\sin x/x\) decreases on this range. For a different-color cap, \(\alpha\le\pi/6\), and even \(a(\alpha)/\alpha\le4-6\sqrt3/\pi<2-4/\pi\); the strict comparison follows from \(\pi<22/7\), \(2\pi+4<6\sqrt3\). Thus each actual cap costs no more than \(2-4/\pi\) times its **reserved** angular width. Summing the disjoint reserved widths, the total removed area is at most \(4\pi-8\). Each clipped cell has area at least 8. Since they are disjoint and contained in fixed-radius disks around their centers, the count in any large normal region is at most its area divided by 8 up to a bounded-width boundary. Normal intensity is at most \(1/8\), yielding center density at most \(2^{45}\,2^{-42}/8=1\).

### Lower radii also cannot rescue the even-shift stock

Keep the stock fixed and let the whole packing have squared minimum \(\mu\le32\), rather than presume the radius is inherited. Relative center density equals \(8(\mu/32)^{15}\) times normal site intensity, counting multiple phases at a normal location separately when present.

* On \(26\le\mu\le32\), scale squared normal lengths by \(8/(\mu-24)\). Property 1 holds since the raw normal minimum is \(\mu-24\); property 2 follows from the raw lower bound \(\mu-20\); and property 3 follows because scaled squared distance below 16 implies raw distance squared below \(2(\mu-24)\le\mu-16\), forcing deep-six same-color differences. Rescaling the proved planar bound gives center density at most \(8(\mu/32)^{15}/(\mu-24)\). This is increasing on \([26,32]\) and equals 1 only at 32.
* On \(18\le\mu\le26\), in an open normal disk of diameter \(\sqrt{\mu-16}\) every phase pair needs horizontal clearance \(>16\). Each of the two parity colors can occur at most twice there by the sum-free deep syndromes. Thus intensity is at most \(16/[\pi(\mu-16)]\).
* On \(9\le\mu\le18\), in an open normal disk of diameter \(\sqrt{\mu-8}\) the phase pairs need horizontal clearance \(>8\). Distinct \(C\)-syndromes must be at vector-quotient Hamming distance at least three, so their radius-one balls (size 29) are disjoint and there can be at most 564 syndromes. Each syndrome occurs at most twice: the mod-four residue support of its \(C\)-bank is the same, and in any one shared residue there are just two \(4D_{28}\) carry cosets; a repeated coset would make horizontal separation zero. The disk therefore has at most 1,128 sites.
* For \(0<\mu\le9\), in an open disk of diameter \(\sqrt\mu\) the same at-most-two per \(C\)-syndrome holds without requiring disjoint radius-one balls, hence at most \(2\cdot 2^{14}\) sites.

Each bounded local count gives an upper normal intensity by averaging the disk center over a large region; it works with multiplicities and without periodicity. In the last three ranges the resulting center density bounds are strictly below one: we may use \(\pi>3\) and check the two endpoints of \(m^{15}/(m-c)\) on the bounded ranges above \(c\) (its only interior critical point can be a minimum). The smallest-radius range is monotone in \(\mu^{14}\). Exact endpoint checks are in the replay. Uniform horizontal counts follow because every sheet has the same \(4D_{28}\) period and fixed cardinality, regardless of shift. Using expanding boxes and their bounded-width boundary, or tiling a large ball with large fixed boxes, gives the stated packing density bounds.

## A literal square equality construction

At positions \((m,n)\) of a square normal lattice of squared basis length 8, assign \(P+2(ma+nb)\), with two-half bit masks
\[
        a=(715,15538),\qquad b=(12300,6534).
\]
Both shifts have even Hamming weight, so the phase repeats after two units along either normal basis vector. The two syndrome depths in \(C,D\) are respectively \((6,6),(6,6)\) and \((4,4)\) for \(a,b,a+b\), exactly. On a basis edge normal square 8, same-bank differences are at least 24 horizontally; on a diagonal normal square 16, they are at least 16. Opposite-bank differences remain all odd and have horizontal squared length at least 28. All other normal differences have squared length at least 32, and within any layer the original stock is unchanged. A shortest horizontal period attains the bound. Normal area per layer is 8, giving exactly \(\delta_{30}=1\). The set is not a lattice, since its height-zero slice is the known non-additive 28D stock; no nonisometry to earlier periodic constructions is claimed.

## Allow arbitrary integer optical shifts on any Bravais normal support

Here the lower horizontal parity mask may be nonzero. For a relative shift \(u=s+2b+4k\) with \(s,b\) binary, put \(S=\operatorname{supp}s\) and \(w=|S|\). **If \(w>0\), the squared clearance between full copies of \(P\) is at most 22.** Derive this as follows.

* **Same bank.** Let \(d\) be the distance from \(b\) to the punctured code \(C|_{\bar S}\), computed outside \(S\). The squared gap is at most \(w+4d\) for \(d\ge1\), using a \(\pm2\) even mismatch to correct the carry parity at no cost. If \(d=0\), it is at most \(w+8\), using one odd coordinate to correct the carry by changing a residue representative from absolute value 1 to 3. Any single puncture has radius at most five: applied to an even depth-six syndrome, toggling the selected parity-check column gives an odd syndrome of depth at most five. **Any three punctures have radius at most four.** Puncturing means passing to the quotient by the three removed check columns; the projected distance is the minimum of the original distances in the corresponding affine cube. A cube entirely at depth at least five contains an even (hence depth-six) syndrome. The replay checks all \(3{,}276\) triples of columns and all 78 depth-six origins, and finds none. Thus for \(1\le w\le2\), \(w+4d\le22\), and for \(3\le w\le5\), \(w+4d\le21\).
* **Opposite banks.** Write \(v=c+d\in C+D\) for the sum of the chosen stock codewords. Want zeros on \(S\) and only \(\pm1\) outside \(S\). For the difference \(u+\mathbf1+2c-2d+4e_1+4D_{28}\), the condition on \(S\) is \(v|_S=(1+b)|_S\). Before the given \(4k+4e_1\) offset, the parity of required carries is \(\mathrm{wt}(b)+b\cdot v\bmod2\), as one sees by taking the parity of \(\lfloor(b_i+c_i-d_i+1)/2\rfloor\) and using \(c\cdot d=0\) and evenness of \(c\). If the required projection is unattainable, one can correct it by at most two bit flips, because \(C+D\) has codimension two. A nonzero even mismatch then allows an equally cheap \(\pm2\) choice to fix any carry. If attainable but the carry parity is wrong, changing one odd coordinate to absolute value 3 costs eight. Thus for \(w<28\) the unconditional opposite gap is at most \(28-w+8\), and for \(w=28\) it is at most 16 (one zero to \(\pm4\) if necessary). This gives the bound for \(w\ge14\).
* For the remaining \(6\le w\le13\), the projection of \(C+D\) onto \(S\) is surjective: its dual \(C\cap D\) has just three nonzero words of weights \(14,14,28\), directly checked. If the carry parity varies on the solutions with fixed projection, the exact baseline squared gap \(28-w\le22\) is attainable. If it does not vary, \(b\) lies in the annihilator of the subspace \(\{v\in C+D:v|_S=0\}\), which is \((C\cap D)+\mathbb F_2^S\). In this case \(b|_{\bar S}\) agrees with a punctured word of \(C\), so the same-bank bound \(w+8\le21\) applies. This completes the claimed cap.

Now at \(\mu=32\) all normal distances are at least \(\sqrt8\), and any normal edge of squared length below 10 must have even relative shift with depth-six syndrome in both codes. Two such edges incident on one point force the third squared edge length at least 16, by sum-freeness. This argument is valid with arbitrary nonlinear phase assignments on the chosen **full Bravais normal support**. Choose a Gauss-reduced normal basis of squared lengths \(8\le a\le b\) and dot product \(0\le e\le a/2\). If \(b<10\), the two short edges from the origin give \(a+b-2e\ge16\), so
\[
 a b-e^2\ge ab-(a+b-16)^2/4\ge64
\]
by monotonicity in \(a,b\) on this range. If \(a<10\le b\), then \(ab-e^2\ge10a-a^2/4\ge64\). If \(a\ge10\), the squared area is at least \(3a^2/4\ge75\). All cases give normal area at least 8 and density no greater than \(T_{30}\). The same rescaling as above works for \(26\le\mu\le32\) because \(\mu-22\), multiplied by \(8/(\mu-24)\), is at least 10.

This last claim deliberately does not say that arbitrary integer phases on arbitrary non-Bravais normal supports are excluded. Real shifts, changes to the carrier and horizontal metrics, and incomplete bank fibers are also not covered. A genuine improvement must escape one of the corresponding proved hypotheses.
