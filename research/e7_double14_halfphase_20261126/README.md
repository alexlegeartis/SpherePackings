# A sharp half-period motif bound for two scaled E7 blocks in dimension 14

Research objective: construct a packing exceeding the laminated 14D center density 1/(16√3), not a kissing number or an upper bound on unrestricted 14D packing. Full scope: periods √(2a) E7 and √(2b) E7 horizontally orthogonal, arbitrary finite motif in their full half-period quotient, with squared minimum normalized to at least 4. a,b≥1. Covolume 256(ab)^(7/2); center density M/[256(ab)^(7/2)]. No winner. Sharp new motif theorem at a=b=4/3: optimum M=64 (not merely optimizer output or a bound on invariant codes). Uniform upper throughout the family 25/(512√2), at most 0.9568319308 times the laminated incumbent. The bound at the critical scale is 64·3^7/2^22; do not advertise as record.

## Phase model, basis, finite duals

E=E7/2E7. There are 128 elements. Weights m=0,1,2,3 of a phase are squared distance to the period after scaling √2; populations [1,63,63,1]. m=3 is the anisotropic radical r, r has mask 82 in research/e7_hermitian35_20261123/certificate.json. m=1 iff q=x^TGx/2 mod 2 is 1 away from r. m=2 otherwise away from 0. E/<r>=V is symplectic of dimension 6, and m(z+r)=3−m(z). M accepts any pair with a m(z1)+b m(z2)≥4. At 4/3 in both blocks this is m1+m2≥3.

Normalized Fourier spherical functions on m=0,1,2,3 are rows (1,1,1,1), (1,−1/63,−1/63,1), f=(1,1/7,−1/7,−1), (1,−1/9,1/9,−1). f is average of 28 real ±1 characters, all χ(r)=−1. Decompose characters on E by parity on r; for odd χ, the rank-6 quadratic Gauss sum on a selected six-dimensional complement is ±8, giving Fourier eigenvalues ±16 for (−1)^q on E. Even nontrivial chars give the second row. Verify rows by finite Walsh transform on all 128 inputs if desired.

At critical scale h=(1+7f1)(1+7f2), g=1+25(f1+f2)+49 f1 f2 are positive definite with constant coefficient 1. h(0)=64, g(0)=100. In the order of allowed unordered types [03,12,13,22,23,33], h=[−48,0,−12,0,0,36], g=[−48,0,−192/7,−36/7,−144/7,0]. Thus the g loss is ≥36/7 for every qΣ=0 off-diagonal singleton pair, where qΣ=q1+q2 mod2.

The weight table can be checked without assuming any closest-vector search is exhaustive. On the integral E7 Gram basis from the linked certificate, enumerate representatives with coefficients in {−4,…,4}; the smallest squared norms seen by parity class are 0 once, 2 in all 63 nonradical odd classes, 4 in all 63 nonzero even classes, and 6 in the radical. These *upper* witnesses suffice. Integrality and quadratic parity give the matching lower bounds in all nonradical classes, since norm zero occurs only in the zero class. A norm-2 representative of the radical would pair to an even integer with each squared-norm-2 basis vector; by Cauchy–Schwarz the pairings have absolute value at most 2. A nonzero pairing therefore forces that representative to be ±one basis vector, none of which lies in the radical. So no shorter radical representative exists. Since the half-period representative is v/2 and the scale is √2, minimum squared displacement is half of the verified squared norm.

## Sharp upper: exclude M=65 by heredity

Let R=(r,r). Let k be the number of full R-pairs; n=M−2k are singletons. For an R-pair and an outside point both distance weights are ≥3 and sum to 6. Each relative total weight equals 3, so qΣ=1. Two distinct R-pairs have mutual qΣ=1. After translating one double anchor to 0, the projected records p=(πx,πy) of all other doubles and singletons have qΣ=1 as lifted labels. A clique for qΣ(difference)=1 has ≤14 vertices: project after translating one origin; the off-diagonal-one Gram of the remaining vectors has rank ≥t−1 on t vectors in the symplectic 12-space. In particular k≤14. Singleton projections are distinct because equal projection and qΣ parity leaves exactly two phase choices, differing by R, and those would have been removed as a double.

Pick l double orbits including the translated zero anchor. For other l−1 projected doubles y_j the alternating Gram has off-diagonal one; projected singletons satisfy B(z,y_j)=1. Therefore y_j independent: if l−1 even, the Gram is invertible; if odd the only possible relation is the sum of all, contradicted by pairing with one singleton. The singleton projections lie in an affine copy of their common perpendicular of dimension 13−l. For l=3 its form has rank 10 (dimension 10, two y form a nondegenerate plane). For l=6 the common perpendicular has dimension 7 and rank 6 (the five y’s span rank four). For l=8 its size is at most 32. In each affine space W, the sign matrix ((−1)^B(u,v)) is sign-conjugate to its restriction on the underlying space (multiply row/column signs for affine shift). A space of dimension d and polar rank t has sign-matrix operator norm 2^(d−t/2): square the matrix and pair characters; row inner products are 2^d inside the same radical coset, zero outside. Thus l=3 gives norm32, l=6 norm16. Among n distinct singleton projections, ordered qΣ=0 nonself pairs are ≥n(n−norm)/2−n. g allows only 65(100−65)7/36=442.3612... such pairs. With k=3..6, l=3 gives n≥53, hence ≥503.5; k=7, l=6 gives n=51, hence ≥841.5; k≥8, l=8 gives n≥37>32 available points. These are contradictions. k≥3 is impossible.

Weight each singleton 25 and each double member 16, sum W=25n+32k. Positive h gives E_h≥W² while its only positive nonzero weights are 36 on internal double pairs. Therefore E_h≤1600 W (since 64·25²=1600·25 and (64+36)·16²=1600·16). M=65, W=1625−18k implies k≥2. Only k=2 remains.

If k=2 then n=61, W=1589. The Fourier energy above the constant W² is ≤W(1600−W)=17,479. The two individual f terms have total Fourier weight 14, consisting of odd characters on r in ONE coordinate. Both double orbits cancel, and the 61 singleton terms have weight 25, so each such squared character sum is ≥625. For the cross 49 f1 f2 characters (uniform over 28×28), R never changes the sign. The fraction of cross characters taking opposite signs on the two doubles is [1−f(Δ1)f(Δ2)]/2≥1/2: their mutual sum of distances is exactly 3, so Δ is 03 or 12 or a swap, in either case f-product <0. There too the two opposite double orbits cancel, leaving odd times 25. Thus nonconstant energy ≥(14+49/2)·625=24,062.5>17,479, contradiction. This excludes 65 and all larger sets.

Exact 64 witness: use g=x+128y in E², E basis from the repo's E7 Gram. The motif consists of all eight cosets of W=span_F2[4507,9153,4754] represented by [0,164,353,460,1063,1227,1301,1520]. Pairwise min checked directly with q; symmetric weight histogram about zero: (00:1, 12:28, 21:28, 22:7). The witness and all its pair constraints were independently replayed.
