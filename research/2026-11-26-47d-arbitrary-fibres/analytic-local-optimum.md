# A one-page analytic stress certificate for the two-translate 47D antipode

**Statement.** Let P be an extremal even unimodular lattice in dimension 48, let v∈P have squared norm 8, and write L=P∩v⊥. In v⊥ take the two complete L-cosets obtained by projecting the parent levels x·v=0 and x·v=1. This is the 47D norm-8 antipode of Chen, Hu, Li, Wang and Wu (Dimension 47 in https://arxiv.org/html/2505.02394v1 ). Its squared minimum is 47/8. (i) Fix its carrier metric and perturb the relative position of the two full cosets by a vector of length s. If 0<s<1/√2, the new squared minimum is at most 47/8−s/√2+s²<47/8. (ii) Even if the 47D period metric is allowed to vary freely simultaneously with this relative position, the packing is a **strict local maximum** of center density among two-translate packings, modulo similarities. This is not a density improvement or a global optimality statement; no claim for deformations with an enlarged supercell or more than two independently moving translates.

**The contact slice.** Let w range over the norm-6 shell of P, an 11-design from the vanishing of the positive-degree harmonic theta sums through degree 10 and antipodal symmetry (proof/source replayed in research/2026-11-26-46d-torus-normal/). Write t=w·v and p=w−(t/8)v. Since w±v are nonzero parent vectors of norm ≥6, t is integral with |t|≤4. The 11-design gives E[t²]=1 and E[t⁴]=72/25<4, so some t=1 (otherwise t⁴≥4t²). On t=1, the projected p have |p|²=47/8; these are actual cross-coset differences, and all cross differences have squared norm ≥6−1/8. Within each original coset the squared minimum is at least 6. On the nine possible integer t values isolate t=1 by its degree-eight Lagrange polynomial. Multiplying by any polynomial in p of degree ≤3 yields degree ≤11. Its shell average equals its round sphere average, invariant under all rotations fixing v. Therefore the equal-weight p at t=1 form a spherical 3-design in v⊥. In particular, E p=0, E ppᵀ=I/8, and all cubic moments vanish. The analogous t=−1 slice is its negative.

**Bound for arbitrary shift in a fixed metric.** For a unit e, X=p·e has E X=E X³=0 and E X²=σ²=1/8. Since E[(X+σ)(X−σ)²]=0, there is some X≤−σ: if all were >−σ every X would equal σ, violating E X=0. Thus some contact vector after translation by se has squared length ≤47/8−2σs+s² as claimed.

**Perfection, without a numerical stress certificate.** Let Q be any nonzero real homogeneous quadratic on v⊥. The average of Q(p)² t²(4−t²) over the root shell equals its spherical mean by degree eight. This mean is strictly positive: set U=t/√48 on the radius-√6 sphere and condition on U. The conditional mean of Q(p)² is a positive constant times (1−U²)² by homogeneity and uniform angular distribution on S⁴⁶. Weighting the normal coordinate U by this factor changes its marginal law from a coordinate on the unit sphere in dimension 48 to one in dimension 52. Thus the ratio of weighted moments E[t⁴]/E[t²] is 3·48/(52+2)=8/3<4. At the integer t levels the factor t²(4−t²) is positive only for t=±1. Hence Q cannot vanish on every p in the t=1 slice. Equivalently, the contact outer products ppᵀ span all real symmetric 47×47 matrices.

**Strict local optimality with two arbitrary period cosets.** Normalize squared minimum to 47/8 and remove common translations and rotations. Locally in a fixed lattice basis, a deformation has symmetric positive metric I+S and relative translate adjusted by a∈v⊥. If distinct nearby packings with density at least the original approached it, their determinant would be at most one. Divide each perturbation (S,a) by its positive norm and take a convergent subsequence. This gives a nonzero limiting (S₀,a₀) with tr S₀≤0 and pᵀ S₀p+2p·a₀≥0 for every projected contact (the term of order two or higher vanishes in the limit). Averaging and E ppᵀ=I/8 force trace zero and equality in all individual constraints. Multiply by p and average; vanishing cubic moments and E ppᵀ=I/8 imply a₀=0. Perfection then gives S₀=0, a contradiction. Thus no nontrivial sufficiently small such deformation even matches the original density.
