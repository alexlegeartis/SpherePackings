## Main construction, success condition, and what actually failed

Attempt to reconstruct the 43D six-sheet packing as independently movable layers over a 42D horizontal fiber. The five-dimensional normal is the DSW K₅. Its 5×5 Gram is a reduced Laplacian of K₆ with the three edges of a perfect matching doubled. The 6×6 Laplacian is 7I−J with matching entries −2; remove row and column 0. Determinant 3072. Normal classes A=K₅*/K₅. A primary class a has torus cost q(a)=min(|x|²:x represents a). Couple this to an orthogonal rank-one normal of squared norm 6, with axial colors b∈Z/6. Candidate sites have (a,b,z), with independent real z. The horizontal labels are cosets from the combined normal. In the guaranteed-distance model the inherited minimum-6 lower bound for distinct horizontal labels is 6−q(Δa)−d₆(Δb)²/6, where d₆ is least absolute residue. Same horizontal label means the identical coset, so distinct such sheets need |z₁−z₂|≥√μ for target squared minimum μ. Formal density/intensity statements on this normal-product model do not require an unverified actual normal embedding; an independent embedding and saturation witness are necessary to promote any positive candidate.

The unmodified diagonal reconstruction has six occupied sites per vertical increment 1/√6, hence 36 per √6 period; a 37th would give +2.78% at the same radius. Searches considered all 3072 primary classes, all 6 axial colors, and all candidate heights at integer, half, or third increments; any site not already present blocks at least 13 of the 36 old sites according to these pair constraints. This count only concerns the guarantee, not necessarily actual distances; it is superseded by the analytic result below. Additional integer-axis variants m=8,10,12 offered no positive insertion. The success criterion was a verified witness, not a numerical near gain.

## Exact certificate-model theorem: arbitrary heights and perturbed radius

Let β₀=7/24, μ₀=6−β₀=137/24, and set μ=μ₀−t where 0≤t≤1/16. For any arrangement satisfying the preceding guaranteed-distance rules, every half-open interval of width w=√(1/6−t) on the vertical axis contains at most six sites; hence its center density relative to the unmodified DSW construction is at most (1−t/μ₀)^(43/2)/√(1−6t)≤1. This is not a universal upper bound on sphere packings, not even on all packings over the same horizontal lattice: an improved actual horizontal coset-minimum estimate could invalidate this conservative model as a necessary condition for the real geometry.

Proof: for two sites in the interval, squared vertical separation <w². Distinct primary labels must then obey q(Δa)<β₀+t+w²=11/24<1/2. The only possible nonzero dual costs below 1/2 are 1/4 and 7/24. They are exactly the 30 oriented differences of a six-point affinely independent simplex P=(0,e₁,…,e₅) in integer dual coordinates, with short edges the three disjoint pairs of the distinguished matching. Each torus clique at threshold β₀ lifts coherently to the dual: choose lifts about one vertex, and a difference that disagreed with a canonical shortest edge would yield a nonzero K₅ vector of length at most 3√β₀<√6. This uses min(K₅)≥6, true immediately as an embedded P48 section, or from minimum cut 6 of its weighted K₆ Laplacian. For simplex differences to have a difference on that same list they must share a head or tail. A pairwise-compatible collection of nonzero differences therefore has common head or common tail (if it contains at least two); adding the anchor yields at most six primary labels and makes the short edges a matching. An exact 243-point check is appended: |cᵢ|²≤6q<3 bounds integer dual coefficients by 1 in each direction.

If there are at least three different primary labels, the edges of cost 7/24 connect them (complete graph minus a matching), and each vertex is incident to one. Every such edge forces equal axial color b: different axial colors cost ≥1/6, strictly exceeding the allowable vertical compensation after the t relaxation, since |Δz|²<w². This works for *all* sites with the given primary color, forcing one common axial color throughout. A duplicate primary then repeats its whole horizontal coset, impossible since √μ>w. There are at most six sites. If one or two primary colors occur, each primary can have at most two axial colors: distinct b-values differ by shortest cyclic step at most 1 because d₆(Δb)²/6<11/24; the cycle C₆ has clique number 2. No pair with both labels the same repeats. Thus at most four sites. Half-open intervals contain pairs of strictly smaller separation, avoiding endpoint ambiguities; tile the line by such intervals to deduce upper intensity.

Relative density against the six-per-native-width reference is bounded by the 43-volume scaling times the interval-intensity ratio stated above. The convex function (1−t/μ₀)^43 is below the chord 1−6t on [0,1/16]: at the left endpoint equality and at the right endpoint 8·271^43<5·274^43. This is a real exclusion of a continuous modulation and of small radius losses, not a heuristic optimizer timeout.

Related torus observation: when q edges ≤μ(K)/9, a first wrapped triangle at equality must be the three equally spaced points 0,a,−a on a shortest period 3a. Any fourth point x must, unless it equals a or −a by triangle equality, satisfy |x±a|≤|a| in a common lift. Adding the two squared inequalities forces x=0. Consequently any ≥4-site torus clique at that exact threshold also has coherent ordinary representatives. Winding improvements need a larger threshold.
