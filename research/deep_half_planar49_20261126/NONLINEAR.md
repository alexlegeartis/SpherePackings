# Any full-planar-lattice colouring of the explicit 47D carrier is bounded

Use the literal v and nine parent Gram witnesses from [the additive 49D note](README.md): now the previously dispensable positive cost E7=E9=17 is essential. This proof permits any colouring of any full Euclidean planar lattice by the 16 canonical fibres, even nonperiodic, with no assumption of an additive label or period. It does not cover an arbitrary nonlattice set of planar sites, noncanonical translates, incomplete horizontal fibres, or general 49D packings.

Write z=16(6−μ), scaled planar Gram G=[[s,t],[t,c]] in Gauss form 0≤2t≤s≤c, D=det G. For candidate at least incumbent D≤32(1−z/96)^49≤32, so s≤sqrt(128/3)<7. Same-colour sites require separation squared ≥16μ=96−z. For z≥1, Thue’s planar hexagonal packing theorem bounds the upper point density of EACH colour by 2/(sqrt(3)·16μ); summing the sixteen (no periodicity required; use identical expanding square windows) yields D≥3μ²/4. The published scalar comparison still rules out gain. For 0≤z≤1 the integer costs for all distinct sites are the literal E_label(next)-label(previous) minus z, with E0=96.

**Short horizontal steps, s<3.** Consecutive sites along the first reduced basis vector must change colour by ±1, since s+z<4. Two consecutive steps cannot reverse (same-colour separation 4s<12). Every row therefore walks around the cyclic colours with a fixed orientation. Six steps along it yield s≥1−z/36≥35/36. The competitor determinant gives c≤32/s+s/4<35. The two adjacent rows through the basis vectors must have the same orientation: if they walk in opposite directions their colour difference across horizontal displacement k=0 or 1 cycles through all residues of one parity. Choose k so the parity is even. Some such neighbouring pair then has identical colours while its squared distance is at most s+2t+c<41<95. Thus one pair of full adjacent rows has a fixed relative colour b, and, up to simultaneous sign reversal, is labelled by i and i+b. Only these two rows are now needed.

Scale G'=G/(1−z/16). On each individual row the costs at inherited radius hold after scaling (for ±1,±2,±3 use 6,3,2 repeated steps, all costing E6=36, for other positive retained costs use E≥16). Between the two rows keep ONLY the difference labels with E≥16. Their z-relaxed constraints imply the inherited constraint in G'. The previous scalar comparison gives det(G')≤32 for a candidate of at least incumbent; s'≤3/(15/16)<5 and c'≤32/s'+s'/4<40 since s'≥1. The following exact reduced two-row vertex certificate checks det G'≥32 using only horizontal moves through ±8 and inter-row moves (i,1), |i|≤8, and no inter-row costs below 16. It returns 119 feasible plane triples, equality only at G'=diag(1,32), b=8, and thus excludes improvement on the short branch.

**Medium horizontal steps, 3≤s<7.** An explicit finite 4×4 patch has no valid colouring at any such putative reduced metric. Place a grid of boxes of width 1/4 over s and t. For each corner (s,t) of the box use c=(32+t²)/s. For displacement (i,j) its squared length is ≤max over four corners of s i²+2t i j+(32+t²)j²/s by convexity of the latter in (s,t), i.e. convex quadratic-over-linear plus linear. Boxes may overhang the reduced region. If an actual feasible colouring existed, each colour difference on this local patch would satisfy E≤1+this maximum. Check with the following small *integer* exhaustive list-colouring verifier. Set the first colour to zero by translation and choose any smallest nonempty remaining list; branch over every possible value, remove forbidden differences at all other vertices. The physical finite coloring engine is exact even though the code only uses a necessary relaxation. No admissible colourings in any box; result: 176 boxes, 6428 recursion nodes. The arbitrary-colouring theorem at all radii follows, with the same additive incumbent supplying equality.
