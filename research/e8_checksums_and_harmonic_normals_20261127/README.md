# Five E8 periods in 40D: sharp checksum fibres, and small-normal harmonic obstructions in 46–47D

Sphere 145, 27 November 2026. **No new lower bound.** The live 40D entry matches [Dorofeev–Sun–Wang's 40D section](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.20359v4#S3), center density $3^{16}/2^{20}$, occupied fraction $1.47983399313426\cdot10^{-7}$. The 47D and 46D comparisons are credited to [Chen–Hu–Li–Wang–Wu](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.02394v1#S3.SS5) and [Conway–Sloane](https://neilsloane.com/doc/anti.pdf), respectively. All $\delta$ below are center densities.

## 1. The 40D construction and the first gain gate

Let $A=E_8/2E_8$ and use period $(2E_8)^5$ of covolume $2^{40}$ and squared minimum 8. Each phase is five classes of $A$ represented by $E_8$ vectors. Give class $x$ the quadratic value $q(x)=\|x\|^2/2\bmod2$. The torus squared distances at zero, anisotropic, and nonzero singular classes are respectively $0,2,4$. This small fact is replayed by enumerating the 240 roots and 2160 norm-four vectors in $E_8=D_8\cup(\boldsymbol1/2+D_8)$, taking their pairings modulo 2 with an explicit $E_8$ basis. The class counts are $1,120,135$, so $q$ is the plus-type nondegenerate form.

A motif with $M$ sites at squared minimum 8 yields $\delta=M/2^{20}$: the strict gain gate is **$M>3^{16}=43,046,721$**. Any gain within the whole half-phase alphabet must use squared minimum 8. At squared minimum 6, projection to four blocks must be injective, so $M\le2^{32}$ and $M(6/8)^{20}<3^{16}$. At squared minimum at most 4 use the total $2^{40}$ phases and $2^{40}(4/8)^{20}<3^{16}$.

Use the hyperbolic model $A=\mathbb F_{16}^2$, $q(a,b)=\operatorname{Tr}_{16/2}(ab)$. Fix any five distinct $\alpha_i\in\mathbb F_{16}$ and let
\[
 C=\{c\in\mathbb F_{16}^5:\sum_i c_i=\sum_i\alpha_i c_i=0\}.
\]
Then $C$ has dimension 3 and minimum Hamming distance 3. Use $(a_i,b_i)$ where $a,b\in C$. Differences supported in at least four slots give squared distance at least 8. For a support of three, both scalar patterns are multiples of one vector $r$ supported there, so $\sum_i a_i b_i=0$ by $\sum r_i^2=(\sum r_i)^2=0$. The three local quadratic values cannot all be one. Thus this is a literal packing of $2^{24}$ phases at minimum 8 and center density 16. Three such banks would give $3\cdot2^{24}$ phases, about **16.92%** above the comparator, if they could be made compatible.

## 2. Arbitrary nonlinear motifs in one fixed checksum fibre have at most $2^{24}$ phases

Work in the additive group of five labels with $\sum x_i=0$ (other fixed sums are translates), of size $2^{32}$. Below squared 8 the only nonzero forbidden differences are: two equal anisotropic labels in two slots, or three anisotropic labels summing to zero in three slots. Give those edges weights 12 and 1 respectively. This symmetric weighted Cayley matrix has row sum $12\cdot10\cdot120+10\cdot6720=81600$.

For a character represented by $t_1,\dots,t_5\in A$ modulo a common translate, the two-slot transform on a pair is $120$ if the two tags are equal, $+8$ if their nonzero difference is anisotropic, and $-8$ if singular. This follows from the Gauss sum of $q$. On a triple put $u=t_i+t_k$, $v=t_j+t_k$ and sum
\[
 \sum_{x,y:q(x)=q(y)=q(x+y)=1}(-1)^{b(u,x)+b(v,y)},
\]
where $b$ is the polar form. The exact Walsh transform in the verifier gives: $6720$ for all equal; $-448,+448$ for two equal with remaining difference singular or anisotropic; and $64,0,-64,128$ for three distinct tags with respectively $0,1,2,3$ anisotropic pair differences. Thus it suffices to sum over the 1895 possible five-tag equality partitions with a binary choice of quadratic value for each distinct-tag pair; allowing unrealizable profiles is safe. The smallest combined eigenvalue is $-320$. The weighted Hoffman bound is consequently
\[
  2^{32}\frac{320}{81600+320}=2^{24}.
\]
For completeness, the bound follows by writing the indicator of a motif as its mean plus an orthogonal vector, using zero internal adjacency weight and the least eigenvalue on the orthogonal part. Section 1 attains the bound. This applies to all nonlinear motifs within a fixed checksum fibre; two such fibres are also insufficient for a gain by summing the two individual bounds.

**A saturated product bank from Section 1 cannot take one external phase.** The two linear syndromes of an external phase in its $a$ component are $u=\sum a_i,v=\sum\alpha_i a_i$, and similarly in the $b$ component. Suppose the $a$ syndrome is nonzero (swap roles if necessary). Choose a triple $T$ with $\sigma_T u+v\ne0$, $\sigma_T=\sum_{i\in T}\alpha_i$. Such a triple exists because distinct $\alpha_i$ give distinct values for at least two triples. Prescribed syndromes can always be solved on $T$, since its two checks have rank two. Its shortening kernel is spanned by $r_i=\sigma_T+\alpha_i$ on $T$, all three nonzero and summing to zero. Representatives of the $a$ difference on this triple have coordinates $a_i^0+\lambda r_i$; the $b$ differences have $b_i^0+\mu r_i$. The three coefficient vectors of the linear trace equations on $\mu$ are $r_i a_i^0+\lambda r_i^2$. Each proper nonempty subset of them sums to zero at at most one $\lambda$, because the corresponding sum of the $r_i^2$ is nonzero. The sum of all three is $\sigma_Tu+v$ and is never zero. Select $\lambda$ outside at most six exceptions in $\mathbb F_{16}$; the three coefficients are linearly independent over $\mathbb F_2$. Nondegeneracy of the trace pairing then lets us solve all three equations to make $q(a_i,b_i)=1$. This gives a genuine short difference of squared length 6 between the external phase and some stock phase. There is no capacity to add even one outside phase while keeping this whole stock.

## 3. A second large architecture: a near numerical gate, blocked by Loomis–Whitney

Let every $a$ word with $\sum a_i=0$ occur above each $b$ in a nonlinear code $B$; there are $2^{16}$ such $a$ words per $b$. To reach the 40D target $B$ would need **657 words**. Distinct $b$ words must differ in at least three slots. A difference in just one slot permits a short difference with $a=0$; in two slots one can choose $a$ supported there to make one block anisotropic. For a three-slot difference $(b_i)$, choose $a$ supported on those three slots. The three trace functionals on $\sum a_i=0$ have an odd relation precisely if the three nonzero $b_i$ are identical: an odd relation with one term is impossible, and the all-three relation holds iff all coefficients coincide. Without an odd relation the equations with all three traces 1 are solvable. Conversely identical coefficients force trace-sum zero. Thus $B$ is exactly a code whose pair differences have support at least four, or support three with identical nonzero entries.

Fix the value in one coordinate of $B$ and call the remaining four coordinates $x$. Change variables to $y=(I+J)x$ in characteristic two, where $J$ is all-ones of order four and $(I+J)^2=I$. Within that fibre, two words agree in coordinate $x_k$ **if and only if** their $y$ words agree in the other three positions: by the distance rule the only possible nonzero difference with $x_k$ fixed is a repeated scalar on the other three. Therefore each of the four coordinate triple projections of the $y$ set has at most 16 elements. Discrete Loomis–Whitney gives $|\mathrm{fibre}|^3\le16^4$, hence at most 40 words. With 16 choices in the fixed coordinate, $|B|\le640$. The cap $640\cdot2^{16}=41,943,040$ is about 2.56% below the record gate. It is an upper bound, not an attained construction.

The uncovered 40D territory includes motifs crossing at least three checksum fibres with **partial and coupled** stocks, other real phases, and deformed periods. Neither of these two precise mechanisms rules it out.

## 4. An independent 46–47D obstruction from the first harmonic theta coefficients

**Ambient lemma.** Let $\Gamma$ be any positive definite rank-48 even unimodular lattice, $U$ a rational normal subspace, $K=\Gamma\cap U$, and $L=\Gamma\cap U^\perp$. Suppose there is a positive semidefinite quadratic form $B$ of positive trace on $U$ such that all nonzero $y\in K^*$ satisfy
\[
  y^T B y\ \ge\ \operatorname{tr}(B)/12 .
\]
If $K$ contains a norm-two vector or a norm-four vector $w$ with $w^TBw>\operatorname{tr}(B)/12$, then $L$ contains a nonzero vector of norm at most four.

Indeed the polynomial $f(x)=(\pi_Ux)^TB(\pi_Ux)-\operatorname{tr}(B)\|x\|^2/48$ is harmonic of degree two. Its weighted theta series is a weight-26 cusp form on $\operatorname{SL}_2(\mathbb Z)$, thus a multiple of $\Delta E_4^2E_6=q-48q^2+\cdots$ (dimension one; use the classical modular form ring). If $L$ had minimum at least 6, every ambient norm-two or norm-four vector would project nontrivially into $K^*$, making $f$ strictly positive on norm two and nonnegative on norm four. If roots exist this contradicts the negative coefficient ratio. If roots do not exist, the cusp form vanishes, so every norm-four vector must have $f=0$. But the norm-four vector $w\in K$ stipulated in this case has $f(w)>0$, again a contradiction.

* **Dimension 47:** an even integral rank-47 lattice with Gram determinant 2 has discriminant quadratic form opposite to that of $A_1$, by Milgram's signature formula (the two order-two forms have Gauss phases $e^{\pm\pi i/4}$ and the signature is 47). Gluing to $A_1$ by this anti-isometry gives a rank-48 even unimodular lattice with primitive normal of norm two and perpendicular lattice exactly the original. In rank one use $B=I$ with dual minimum $1/2>1/12$. Thus every such 47D lattice has minimum at most 4. In particular a norm-8 primitive section of a min-six 48D even unimodular lattice cannot give the tempting two-bank **integral** index-two lift with minimum 6, which would have determinant 2 and improve the published $\delta=47^{47/2}/2^{118}$. Every integral such lift is even: its added order-two class in the cyclic order-eight discriminant has quadratic value $-4^2/8=-2=0\bmod2$. The remaining half-shift search must use a **nondual** half shift, or change the carrier altogether.
* **A concrete alternative gate in 47D, not a witness:** on a norm-eight primitive section $L$ of a minimum-six rank-48 even unimodular lattice, three translates $L,L+w/3,L-w/3$ would suffice if $w+3L$ had squared minimum at least 52, with $w\in L$ nonzero mod $3L$. The packing would have squared minimum at least $52/9$ and volume per point $\sqrt8/3$. Its minimum possible squared density ratio to the cited 47D comparator is exactly $9\cdot416^{47}/(4\cdot423^{47})>1$. This tests an actual positive construction mechanism distinct from the impossible integral half shifts; no such residue is exhibited here.
* **Dimension 46:** if $\operatorname{rank}K=2$ and $D=\det K\le20$, then its perpendicular $L$ has minimum at most 4 in **any** even unimodular 48D ambient, not only extremal ones. For $\mu(K)\ge4$, its dual minimum is $\mu(K)/D$ by rotating the lattice in its plane (the adjugate description of a planar dual), at least $1/5>2/12$. Take $B=I$. The planar Hermite bound forces $\mu(K)<6$ since $D<27$, so here $\mu(K)=4$ and the strict proviso holds. If $K$ contains a primitive norm-two root $v$, take $B$ with weights 1 along $v$ and 5 transverse within the normal plane. For $y\in K^*$ with $\langle y,v\rangle\ne0$ the first contribution is at least $1/2$. Otherwise, extend $v$ to a lattice basis; the transverse component of its second basis vector has squared length $D/2$, so a nonzero $y$ has transverse squared length at least $2/D$ and weighted contribution at least $10/D\ge1/2$. This equals $\operatorname{tr}(B)/12$. The ambient lemma applies. As the 46D $\mu=6$ bare-section gain requires $D\le17$ (compare $ (3/2)^{23}/\sqrt D$ to $13^{23}/3^{93/2}$), one cannot rescue that gain by choosing a nonextremal root-bearing **even unimodular** ambient. This is not a classification of arbitrary 46D lattices or nonlattice motifs.
