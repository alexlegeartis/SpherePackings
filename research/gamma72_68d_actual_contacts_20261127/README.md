# Actual Leech contacts rule out densifying the benchmark 68D motif by adding canonical phases

**No improved lower bound.** Fix the [archived 68D Γ72 packing](../novel_routes_20261113/higher_parent/gamma72_verified.json), the current [project lower-bound entry](../../LOWER_BOUNDS_1_96.md). Its normal lattice `K` has Gram `10 I_4 − 2 J_4`, determinant 2000, and is embedded in the third Leech block of [Nebe’s extremal Γ72](https://arxiv.org/abs/1008.2862). Its five normal dual labels are `0,e_1,…,e_4`, all differences of squared norm `1/5`. The published Γ72 minimum 8 certifies horizontal squared minimum at least `39/5`; equality follows from a witness below. The comparator centre density is `5(39/20)^34/sqrt(2000) ≈ 812141667.47`, the occupied fraction `≈2.20071022066104e−13`. The record here is an explicit repository consequence of Nebe's construction, not an independently published 68D number in her paper.

**Theorem.** A union of full translates of the unchanged 68D orthogonal section `L`, using any canonical dual phases in `L*/L` and retaining the five specified benchmark phases, cannot improve this density at any radius. Translation of the whole motif is immaterial. The extra phases are arbitrary, and are not required to form a subgroup. This does **not** rule out a different motif discarding one of the five, real off-dual phases, changing the period, or global improvement.

## A low-dimensional witness for an actual Γ72 contact

Use Nebe's §2.1 basis, verified against the [catalogued Γ72 Gram](https://www.math.rwth-aachen.de/~Gabriele.Nebe/LATTICES/neb72.html): the lattice is generated by rows of

```
( I  I  A )
( 0  B  B ) / sqrt(2),     B=I−A,  A²−A+2I=0,
( 0  0 2I )
```

in three orthogonal copies of the Leech lattice with Gram `F`. Both `A` and `B` give norm-doubling maps. Modulo twice the Leech lattice, they are complementary projections to the isotropic spaces `V=im A` and `U=im B`, respectively. Thus in unscaled Leech coordinates the three blocks of a Γ72 vector carry a common `V` component `v` and their `U` components sum to zero. Conversely every such triple of residues is represented: set the first block arbitrarily, vary the second by the `B` rows, and use the last `2I` rows. All assertions are in the exact named scale; `data.json` records the catalogue Gram and the integral factorization.

Let `c` be a Leech vector chosen for the third coordinate. The following are certified bounds for a Γ72 vector with third unscaled block `c`:

| Leech squared norm of `c` | Γ72 squared norm at most |
| ---: | ---: |
| 4 or 6 | 8 |
| 8 or 10 | 10 |
| 16 when `c=2λ` and `||λ||²=4` | 8 |

To see this, use Nebe's Lemma 3.1 and Proposition 3.4 with the two polar spaces interchanged if necessary. Every nonzero common `V` fibre has a norm-4 vector; each anisotropic residue is represented at norm 6. The latter residues occur in every nonzero `V` fibre because the pairing `b(u,v)` is a nonzero linear functional of `u∈U`. Indeed the finite quadratic value of `u+v` is `b(u,v)`, so the three quadratic values sum to zero whenever the `U` components do. For nonzero `v`, a third block of norm 4 or 8 has quadratic value zero; choose the other two blocks of norm 6 (choose either first anisotropic residue and the second is forced and anisotropic). For norm 6 or 10 the quadratic value is one; choose one block of norm 4 and the forced other block of norm 6. Divide the sum of three squared norms by two for the Γ scale. If `v=0`, `c` is in the norm-doubled Leech sublattice `im B`, since `2I=AB` and the latter is the full inverse image of `U`. No such vector has norm 4, 6 or 10; for `c` of norm 8 choose `(0,c,c)`. The doubled norm-4 case is simply `(0,0,c)`. This proof is reusable for any normal section inside one Leech block. It dispenses with enumerating the enormous 72D shell even when a projection is far from the origin.

In the third block let the four normal coefficient rows be `r_i` in the Leech basis. The Γ sections use twice those rows before dividing by `sqrt(2)`, so the normal inner products for such a lift are the four integers `b_i=(r_i,c)_F`. Their normal squared projection is exactly

```
beta(b) = b^T (10 I_4−2 J_4)^−1 b = (sum b_i² + (sum b_i)²)/10.
```

A unit coordinate minor proves that the displayed normal basis is primitive in Γ72. The horizontal complement therefore has covolume `sqrt(2000)` and all its dual labels are reached by the normal quotient. For any given witness `b,c`, projecting an ambient vector from the table proves that the actual squared horizontal distance in that phase is at most `ambient_bound−beta(b)`. One need not assume that `b` is the shortest normal representative of its quotient class. In particular the stored witness with `b=e_1` is Leech norm 4 and gives the actual interphase contact `39/5`.

## Exact compatibility graph, at all radii

Two integer normal labels are equivalent modulo the primitive normal stock iff their difference divided by `10I−2J` is an integer coordinate vector. Thus use the key `(b_i + sum b mod 10)_i`. Enumerating `[0,9]^4` covers all labels since `10 Z^4` maps trivially, and the 2,000 distinct keys agree with the determinant. For each of 812 stored witness rows, the verifier checks the exact norm and normal inner products and assigns the certified horizontal upper clearance to that key and its negative. No approximate search result or candidate absence is used as proof. A class with no witness has no extra restriction; the packing always has squared minimum at most 8 because the section contains a Leech min-8 translate in the first block. All assigned upper clearances lie on the `1/5` grid.

Suppose a packing retaining the five old phases has squared minimum `mu`. Its new phases first have to satisfy every old contact. In the remaining finite graph, an explicit coloring partitions the addable labels into groups of pairwise **incompatible** phases, so each feasible packing can use at most one label per group. For the rows not needing coloring, use the number of addable labels alone. Put `d=ceil(5mu)`; `mu≤39/5` because of the known old contact. At `d≤32` the total of 2,000 phases is already insufficient. The remaining exact caps are:

| `d` | Addable labels before pairwise constraints | Maximum total bounded by |
| ---: | ---: | ---: |
|39|0|5|
|38|0|5|
|37|42|18|
|36|95|28|
|35|213|53|
|34|417|422|
|33|594|599|

The `d=37,36,35` verified group counts are respectively 13, 23 and 48. For each row the density ratio to the incumbent is at most `(total/5)(d/39)^34 ≤ 1`, compared by integers. At `d=32` the liberal cap 2,000 still gives ratio less than 0.480. Between grid levels this argument takes the larger radius and all edges permitted there by the witnessed upper clearances. This gives the theorem, and in particular certifies why a seemingly promising sixth point in a far normal label cannot be added. A genuine next candidate on this carrier must replace rather than extend the five-site motif, or alter the period/phase geometry.
