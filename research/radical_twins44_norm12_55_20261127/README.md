## 44D: two shortened Leech factors, enlarged before matching

Let \(A=\Lambda_{24}\) be the Leech lattice at squared minimum 4, let \(R=\sqrt 2 A_2\) be generated by \(4(e_0-e_1)/\sqrt8,4(e_1-e_2)/\sqrt8\), and let \(L=A\cap R^\perp=\Lambda_{22}\). See [Conway–Sloane on these Leech sections](https://neilsloane.com/doc/anti.pdf); we use the usual integer Golay coordinate model, divided by \(\sqrt8\). The normal is primitive: a Leech point in its real span has the last 21 coefficients zero, hence common parity even; the support of its binary Golay reduction has size at most three, so is empty. Thus its three remaining integer coefficients are multiples of four and sum to zero. Consequently \(\det L=\det R=12\).

Put \(V=A/2A\) with \(q(\bar x)=x^2/2\bmod2\), and write bars for images there. Both \(R\) and \(L\) are primitive, so their mod-2 maps inject. The image of \(L/2L\) is \(\bar R^\perp\) (dimension 22 by unimodularity); its radical is \(\bar R\), a totally singular two-space. The quotient \(W=\bar R^\perp/\bar R\) is a nonsingular quadratic space of dimension 20. Let \(S\subset W\) be the images of minimum-4 vectors of \(L\).

Every nonzero radical class, considered in \(L/2L\), consists only of vectors of norm at least 12. To see this, pick a norm-4 representative \(r\in R\) of the same class modulo \(2A\); then \((y+r)/2\in A\) for \(y\) in this class, so \((y^2+4)/4\ge4\). This is sharp. If, for example, \(r=4(e_0-e_1)/\sqrt8\), choose a Golay octad containing coordinates 0 and 1 but not 2. A shortest vector \(w\in A\) supported on that octad can have integer entries \(+2,-2,0\) at the three chosen coordinates (with parity of the other signs adjusted); then \(y=2w-r\in L\) has norm 12. The other root directions follow by the same argument. Thus the nonzero radical **half phase** has squared distance exactly 3 in the unscaled \(L\) torus, and exactly 6 after rescaling.

The 49,896 norm-4 vectors in \(L\) give **24,948 different** elements of \(S\), one per antipodal pair. For the count: the \((\pm4^2,0^{22})\) shape contributes \(4\binom{21}2=840\). The \((\pm2^8,0^{16})\) shape contributes \(128\cdot210+32\cdot21=27552\), using the Steiner octad intersection counts with the chosen triple (the octads avoiding all three, or containing all three, respectively). The \((\mp3,\pm1^{23})\) shape contributes \(21\cdot1024=21504\) since the abs-3 coordinate must be outside the triple and each Golay word has uniform three-bit restriction. Projection to \(W\) does not collide two different root lines: a colliding difference is either zero mod \(2L\) (then half the signed sum gives a nonzero vector of norm at most 2), or a nonzero radical (choose sign of the sum to give norm at most 8, contradicting the radical minimum 12).

Take the carrier \(\sqrt2 L\perp\sqrt2 L\) (covolume \(12\cdot2^{22}\)). Suppose \(F:C\to W\) is injective, for \(C\subset W\), and for any different \(a,a'\in C\),
\[
q(a+a')=q(F(a)+F(a')),\qquad
\{a+a',F(a)+F(a')\}\not\subset S.
\]
For each paired reduced label \((a,F(a))\), put **all 16** choices of the two radicals into the half-period motif. Within that paired label any nonzero radical difference gives physical squared distance at least 6. Between different paired labels, both reduced differences are nonzero. If their quadratic values are 1, each numerator has norm at least 6. If both are 0, each numerator has norm at least 4 and one has norm at least 8. Half-phase norm after rescaling is one half the numerator norm, so the total separation is at least 6. In a single translate the period supplies minimum 8. Thus this is a certified implication including all pairs, no assumed ability to lift the map into an ambient extremal lattice.

The center density at radius \(\sqrt6/2\) is \(16|C|(3/4)^{22}/12\). To exceed [Chen–Hu–Li–Wang–Wu’s 44D record](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.02394v1#S3.SS7), \(157^{22}/(2^{22}5^{43/2}11^{23})=509.61898264\ldots\) (matching the live catalog), one needs \(|C|\ge214269\). A rank-18 nonsingular quadratic matching with no shallow collision would give \(2^{18}\) labels and center density **623.48563689…**, +22.34%. No such matching recovered. Additive matching gives a lattice; a nonlinear matching need not. Checking one 18-dimensional additive proposal requires only checking its intersections with the two 24,948-element shallow sets, not a \(2^{44}\) pair sweep. The full-radical expansion is logically essential; simply thinning an inherited P48 graph loses the density headroom.

### An adjacent 46D diagnostic: no single-axis radical bank

The analogous rank-one shortening on each side can be applied to a binary Leech graph \(P=(1/\sqrt2)\{(x,y)\in A^2:\bar y=T\bar x\}\) which is 48D extremal even unimodular. Such a realization of P48n is documented in Nebe's [A fourth extremal even unimodular lattice](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1312.3780), Table 2 via the two \(\sqrt2 A\) eigenspaces. For a root \(a\) on the first side and \(b\) on the second let \(u=\sqrt2(a,0)\), \(v=\sqrt2(0,b)\). The bare complement has covolume 8: \(u,v\) are primitive, and no half sum lies in \(P\), since \(T\bar a\ne\bar b\) (otherwise a norm-4 parent vector results). A two-bank complement at squared minimum 6 would have center density \((3/2)^{23}/4=2805.685\ldots\), +3.15% over Conway–Sloane [\(T_{46}\)](https://neilsloane.com/doc/anti.pdf). A one-sided nonzero radical cannot be the added bank.

Here is a direct obstruction for every \(a,b\), independent of short-vector enumeration. The norm-6 shell of \(P\) has 52,416,000 points and is an 11-design (ordinary theta and positive-degree harmonic theta argument, or [Bannai–Koike–Shinohara–Tagami](https://doi.org/10.17323/1609-4514-2006-6-2-225-264)). Its integer pairings \(U=p\cdot u,V=p\cdot v\) have absolute values at most 4. The degree-8 filter \(F(V)=V^2(V^2-1)(V^2-4)(V^2-9)\) isolates \(|V|=4\). The spherical moments give exactly 4512 at \(|V|=4\). Applying the 11-design also to \(U^2F(V)\) shows that the conditional \(E(U^2\mid |V|=4)=(48-16)/47=32/47\), because the spherical transverse second moment at \(V\) is \((48-V^2)/47\). Since \(U\) is integral, at least \(4512(15/47)/2=720\) vectors have \(V=4\), \(U=0\) using joint negation symmetry. Writing \(p=(x,y)/\sqrt2\), these have \(x\perp a,\ y\cdot b=4\). Either \(y=b\), or \((y-b)^2=y^2-4\ge4\); and \(x\ne0\) by the graph and parent minimum. The exceptional case \(y=b\) has \(x^2=8\) in one fixed class, with at most 48 representatives (distinct nonopposite representatives are mutually orthogonal, by applying \(\mu(A)=4\) to both half sums). The other ≥672 contacts have \(x^2=(y-b)^2=4\), and \((x,y-b)/\sqrt2\) is a horizontal squared-4 collision into the second-side radical bank. The other axis is symmetric. This says nothing against an off-canonical finite relation in the 44D proposal, and is not a universal 46D bound. Canonical combinations of both radical banks are already treated by the earlier verified rank-two-normal theorem [Sphere89](../2026-11-26-46d-torus-normal/).

## 55D: a second-slip generator, tested beyond norm 10

Use the [verified Leterrier 54D lattice and coset](../cross_coset55_20261116/PROOF.md), \(L\) of squared minimum 10, \(\det L=3^{25}\), \(Q\in L^*\) of order 3 modulo \(L\), and coset depth \(D=28/3\). The [live 55D three-layer construction](../uneven_layers55_20261122/) has \(\delta=92,197.2234\ldots\), \(H_0=(19/5)h,\ h^2=2/3\). Leterrier's thesis prints 116,783.1496, whose displayed construction instead contains a [norm-six vector](../tilted55_20261116/PROOF.md#external-source-correction); neither this nor our conditional comparator exceeds that printed figure.

If a primal norm-12 vector \(v\in L\) has no closest coset point \(w\in Q+L\) with \(\langle w,v\rangle=-6\), then \(w\cdot v\ge-5\) follows from integrality and \(\|w+v\|^2\ge D\). The three increments \(Q,Q,Q+(19/100)v\) with gaps \(h,h,(179/100)h\) now define a valid periodic three-layer packing. Across the long gap the squared horizontal distance of closest points is at least \(D-10(19/100)+12(19/100)^2\). All nonclosest points use interpolation of \(w,w+v\): with \(a=19/100\) their bound is \(D+2(1-a)-12a(1-a)\), since the even parent and dual \(Q\) give a norm gap of two. Adding the prescribed vertical square gives \(>10\) on both. At two gaps, the two-short horizontal part has depth \(D\); a short and long gap use the \(-Q\) coset interpolation \(D-12a(1-a)\). The three-gap period has horizontal phase \(av\) and vertical height \((379/100)h\); the phase's minimum is \(12a^2\), since \(\|av\|^2<10/4\). Farther gaps have vertical square at least \(16h^2>10\). The two short gaps attain 10. Hence the conditional gain is **380/379** at the same radius and with the same three sites per cell.

Conversely, when a witness \(\langle w,v\rangle=-6\) exists, exact interpolation gives \(\operatorname{dist}(Q+av,L)^2=D-12a(1-a)\) for \(0\le a\le1\). It covers the negative slip as well, since \(w+v\) gives the opposite extreme. This blocks the entire one-long-slip family with the first two increments exactly \(Q,Q\), at minimum 10, regardless of unequal gaps: take \(|a|\le1/2\). If \(|a|\le7/40\), period separation gives \(H^2\ge10-12(7/40)^2>H_0^2\) using the explicit horizontal representative \(av\). Otherwise the long gap squared is at least \(10-D+12(7/40)(33/40)>((9/5)h)^2\), and the two short gaps are each at least \(h\). This no-gain claim is for this sampled direction family at squared minimum 10, not other radii or arbitrary mixed shifts.

### Literal scope and the rational-coordinate contact test

We checked all \(\pm\)-pair sums of the [124 archived polynomial generators](../cm58_20261116/frontier/polynomial_points54.json), taking a representative of a simultaneous sign flip. Among the reduced sums with x numerator degree 12, there are **21 distinct polynomial directions** and **56 distinct rational directions with one finite denominator root**. Each of the 77 has a literal exact obstruction, including both signs as above. The list is not a classification of all norm-12 directions of \(L\).

For the rational case write \(v=(X/B^2,Y/B^3)\), where the x/y numerator degrees are 12/18 and the reduced monic \(B\) has degree 0 or 1. This point lies in the narrow lattice because it is a sum/difference of two given narrow points. Its height is exactly 12 by Leterrier's formula \(\max(\deg X,D+2\deg B)\). For a closest polynomial point \(w=(x,y)\in Q+L\), \(\deg x\le9,\deg y=14\), set \(d=X-xB^2,e=Y-yB^3\). Since \(\deg d=12\) and \(\gcd(d,B)=1\), group law and the curve equation give
\[
x(v+w)=\frac{XB^2+xB^4+2t^{28}B^4+YyB+X^2x+Xx^2B^2}{d^2}
\]
over characteristic 3; the numerator has degree at most 33. Its cancellation against \(d^2\) is exactly \(2\deg\gcd(d,e)\), since modulo \(d^2\) it is \(e^2/B^2\). Thus \(v\cdot w=6-\deg\gcd(d,e)\); the necessary blocker is simply \(d\mid e\). The literal witnesses are from the complete normalized coset-point parametrization in [LOCAL_OPTIMUM.md](../uneven_layers55_20261122/LOCAL_OPTIMUM.md). Each is applied to a translated generator sum using the same verified curve isometries \(t\mapsto t+a\) and three options for the additive correction's constant, then pulled back by the inverse isometry; all these preserve \(Q+L\).
