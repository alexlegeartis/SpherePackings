# Nonlinear two-block phase subfamily at depth five

A one-third-grid \(D_4^3\) motif of depth at least five that is invariant under a common order-nine translation subgroup supported in two blocks cannot reach 645 points if both projections of that subgroup are injective totally isotropic planes. This is an exact restriction on this architecture, not a reason that arbitrary near-dense motifs must have such symmetry.

Let \(G\) be the depth-one graph on the local 81-element \(D_4/3D_4\) quotient. It is 24-regular, with nontrivial eigenvalues 6 and \(-3\) from the Fourier table. A first-block fiber projects injectively onto each other block and each image is independent in \(G\): otherwise both other blocks cannot contribute at least five. The least eigenvalue bound gives independence size at most \(81\cdot3/(24+3)=9\). Thus each nonempty fiber of a motif with the indicated translation invariance is one size-nine orbit, and an improving motif projects onto a set \(X\) of at least 72 first-block labels.

Use hyperbolic coordinates \((a,b)\in \mathbb F_3^2\times\mathbb F_3^2\) with form \(Q(a,b)=\det(a,b)\) on each phase block. A basis of each isotropic plane extends to a hyperbolic basis, so we can take the subgroup to translate \(a\) by the same \(\lambda\) in both blocks. Choose the gauge \(a_2=0\) for each orbit, recording \(b_2(x),a_3(x),b_3(x)\). For a pair of orbits denote their differences by \(b=\Delta b_2,\ e=\Delta a_3,\ c=\Delta b_3\). Their phase-block differences are \((\lambda,b),(\lambda+e,c)\) for arbitrary \(\lambda\).

If either \(b\) or \(c\) vanishes, we can zero one block. If they are independent, both determinants can be 1. If \(c=b\ne0\), the determinants differ by a constant and some pair gives total depth at most 3. If \(c=-b\ne0\), the determinants range over the pairs summing to \(k=-\det(e,b)\); the minimum phase depth is respectively \(3,4,2\) for \(k=0,1,2\). Therefore every depth-one edge of \(X\) forces both the sum \(b_2+b_3\) to agree and \(k=1\). The graph induced by \(X\) is connected: after at most nine vertex deletions its minimum degree is at least 15. Two disconnected groups \(A,B\) would have sizes at least 16 and total at least 72, so \(|A||B|\ge896\); the eigenvalue bound for zero edges between them gives \( (24/81)|A||B|\le6\sqrt{|A||B|}\), a contradiction. Thus \(b_2+b_3\) is constant across \(X\).

Now \(f(x)=(-a_3(x),b_2(x))\) is an injective map from \(X\) to the local graph: equal images make \(b=c=e=0\) and the two phase blocks can both vanish. It preserves every depth-one edge. A depth-two pair cannot map to depth two: that would require \(b\ne0,\ c=-b,\ k=2\), allowing phase depth just two. Denote \(r=81-|X|\le9\). The two induced subsets \(X\) and \(f(X)\) have depth-one edge counts differing by at most \(\binom r2\), since each count equals \(972-24r\) plus the corresponding complement's edge count. Thus at most \(\binom r2\) depth-two pairs can become depth-one edges.

For a depth-two pair the six common depth-one neighbors induce \(K_{2,2,2}\); its three opposite pairs are also depth-two pairs. For a depth-three pair the common-neighbor graph is the triangle-free \(K_{3,3}\). [Exact local replay](verify_d4_isotropic_diagonal.js) checks these statements. If a depth-two pair maps to depth three, one whole opposite common-neighbor pair must be missing from \(X\); otherwise a triangle survives and is mapped injectively into a triangle-free graph. Each omitted opposite pair can account for at most three retained pairs, applying the same common-neighbor rule to the omitted pair. Therefore at most \(3\binom r2\) depth-two pairs map to depth three. The domain has at least \(972-24r\ge756\) depth-two pairs, while \(4\binom r2\le144\). This contradiction excludes the architecture.

## A correlated three-block phase architecture

There is also a short obstruction for a different, not necessarily translation-invariant ansatz. Use determinant coordinates \((x_j,y_j)\) in the three factors. Impose \(x_1+x_2+x_3=0\), and in each fiber \(X=(x_1,x_2)\) put \(y_j=g_j(X)+v\) with a common \(v\) ranging over an arbitrary subset of \(\mathbb F_3^2\). There are at most 81 fibers of size at most nine. Call a fiber heavy if it has size at least five.

Take two such heavy fibers with \(\Delta x_1,\Delta x_2\) independent. Their two phase sets have sizes summing to more than nine, so every phase difference occurs. The three determinants range over the affine plane \(q_1+q_2+q_3=b\), where \(b=\sum_j\det(\Delta x_j,\Delta g_j)\); this is because the three linear phase contributions sum to zero and the first two are independent. To avoid depths \((1,1,1)\) or \((1,1,2)\), both below five, we need \(b=2\) in \(\mathbb F_3\).

Identify \(X\in\mathbb F_3^4\) with a two-by-two matrix of columns \(x_1,x_2\). Let \(U\) be the two-dimensional subspace of multiplication matrices for \(\mathbb F_9/\mathbb F_3\). Each nonzero difference in a coset of \(U\) is invertible. If a coset has five heavy fibers, it contains an affine parallelogram of four distinct heavy fibers: among the ten unordered pairs of five points, two have the same sum in the nine-element affine plane; those two pairs cannot share a point. For the four corners, assign coefficients \(a=(1,-1,-1,1)\) in parallelogram order. Then \(\sum_i a_i=0\) and \(\sum_i a_i x_{ij}=0\) for each block \(j\). Consequently
\[
\sum_{i<k}a_i a_k\sum_j \det(x_{ij}-x_{kj},g_j(X_i)-g_j(X_k))=0;
\]
expanding the determinant bilinearly shows that the coefficient of each \(g_j(X_i)\) is zero by these two balances. But all six edge values must equal 2, and \(\sum_{i<k}a_i a_k=-2=1\) in \(\mathbb F_3\). This is a contradiction. Each of the nine cosets therefore has at most four heavy fibers. The total motif size is at most \(36\cdot9+45\cdot4=504\). No assumption of linear offsets or complete phase sets was used; the result still concerns only the stated architecture.
