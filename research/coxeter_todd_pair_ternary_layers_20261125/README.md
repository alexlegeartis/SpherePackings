# Two Coxeter–Todd blocks with ternary transverse layers

Pinned repository da4a938e533ddd77ad304d43d4ce83e7516e897c. New geometry: two independent 12D Coxeter–Todd blocks together with a 1D–3D transverse Euclidean root lattice, not the preceding 28D Barnes–Wall mixed torus. Let W = F₃⁶ with Q(x) = Σ xᵢ²; the Coxeter–Todd K/θK torsion alphabet for θ of norm 3 has real squared costs 0, 4/3, 2, 8/3 for zero, Q=1, nonzero Q=0, Q=−1. Each K-period has minimum squared 4 and covolume 27. Source: Conway and Sloane (1983), Sections 3, 7, https://neilsloane.com/doc/Me107.pdf. The two copies can be glued along an anti-isometry of Q (three blocks of the 2×2 matrix with rows (1,1), (1,−1) give an explicit one) to attain the full 24D Leech density. A nonzero quotient translation with Q=0 has squared depth 2, since neither coordinate-only representative is shallow and two nonzero costs exceed 2. Using a totally singular plane of quotient translations allows natural-scale 25D and 26D laminations of exact center densities 1/√2 and 1/√3. The 27D benchmarks are Vardy's unrestricted 1/√2 and Bacher's lattice 1/√3; the pinned general table and historical catalogue https://www.math.rwth-aachen.de/~Gabriele.Nebe/LATTICES/density.html agree. No record at opening.

The search allows fragmented and nonisometric transverse layers instead of retaining a Leech period. For k=1,2,3 use the full ternary torus of a root lattice A_k with arbitrary positive independent relative squared scale, as well as independent scales for each K. In minimum-four normalization with scales x,y,z ≥1, the covolume of the period is 729 (xy)^6 2^(k/2) sqrt(k+1) z^(k/2); the strict site threshold is this number times the preceding center density. S is any nonlinear subset of F₃^(12+k), including asymmetric layers. An optimizing fixed finite support can be taken at one of finitely many exact scale triples: the support inequalities are affine halfspaces, and the logarithm of the covolume is concave and increases coordinatewise. The minimum occurs at a vertex of this orthant-constrained polyhedron. Suitable Fourier bounds factor into two copies of the K radial character table and the permutation/negation orbits of the little A_k group. Candidate translation lattices have site counts powers of three. 
## Verified finite optimization

The three independent scale variables were allowed to vary over all positive real values, subject only to the periods not violating minimum four. After identifying interchanged K blocks there were 87, 136 and 215 distinct feasible plane intersections in 25D, 26D and 27D, and respectively 69, 122 and 192 distinct allowed-difference patterns. For each, a linear-programming dual was scaled by 10^14, clipped and rounded to nonnegative integer Fourier coefficients, then repaired by adding a multiple of the sum of all nonconstant characters (equal to −1 on every nonzero difference). Bounds and record comparisons were checked by integer arithmetic. Replay: [`replay.py`](replay.py).

**Additive motifs cannot improve any of the three lattice records at any independent block scales.** A lattice in this fixed torsion model has a translated subgroup as motif, so its cardinality is a power of three. The largest certified lattice density is exactly 1/√2 in 25D and 1/√3 in 26D, attained by the anti-isometry horizontal graph at transverse squared scale 9/2. In 27D every lattice in this model has center density at most 3√2/8 = 0.530330…, below Bacher's 1/√3; this last upper value is not asserted to be attained.

For arbitrary nonlinear motifs only four scale vertices can improve the unrestricted records: in 25D (1,1,3) with M≤1995; in 26D (1,1,1) with M≤1995 or (1,1,3) with M≤5987; in 27D (1,1,3) with M≤17619. All three triples list squared scales of the two horizontal and one transverse period; they describe possible optimizers, not the only parameters at which any improvement could exist. The following reduction sharpens the 26D upper cap at the second vertex to 5985.

**The two 26D vertices are exactly the same extremal problem, not just similar LP bounds.** Let H₃ be the maximum total number of sites in three colored horizontal layers, requiring within-color squared horizontal distance at least 4 and between-color at least 8/3. The 25D critical maximum is H₃; it takes at least 1786 sites to beat the existing 25D packing (M>729√6). The 26D maxima at the two vertices are H₃ and 3H₃; a 26D record needs H₃≥1459. In A₂/3A₂ there is a central line of three and three cosets of it. At z=1 distinct labels in the same coset supply vertical cost 4/3, all cross-coset labels only 4/9, which never enables a horizontal distance strictly below 4 (the next available horizontal cost is 10/3). A linear map to F₃ injective on the central line therefore compresses every such motif to three colors; conversely support a three-color motif on that line. At z=3, points in the same coset contribute 4 when their vertical labels differ, while different cosets contribute 4/3. Selecting one label from each coset gives an H₃ configuration; average over the independent selections to bound M≤3H₃, with equality by triplication within each coset. Thus 1459–1995 is the unresolved interval for a 26D record. This also gives the sharper unrestricted 26D family cap 1995/(1458√3)≈0.7900.

Every single horizontal layer is a partial matching between two copies of F₃⁶. A complete affine anti-isometry graph y=g(x) is extremal. If n members of such a graph form a layer, any external site in a second color must conflict with at least one of its two reference graph positions (at the same input and at the same output): the relevant two quadratic types are opposites, and cross-color horizontal distance 8/3 would require both to be of deep type −1. Every missing reference position is associated with at most two outsider sites in a given other color, by partial matching. Hence each other color has at most 2(729−n) sites. In an H₃ record the largest layer has at least 487 sites and cannot be contained in any affine anti-isometry graph. For 27D the unit-root tetrahedron in A₃/3A₃ has four labels at pairwise transverse cost 4/3 when z=3. Averaging all translates gives a four-color configuration of size at least 4M/27; a 27D record requires M>8748√3, i.e. at least 15,152 sites. One tetrahedron would contain at least 2245, with one layer of at least 562 sites. If that layer extended to an affine anti-isometry graph, its tetrahedron could contain at most n+6(729−n)≤1564, a contradiction. Thus a 27D record also requires a large nonextendible layer.

## Sharp nonlinear rigidity in two different classes

A 4-separated horizontal layer of more than 486 sites extends to a full affine anti-isometry graph if it has even a nine-point horizontal translation subgroup. This allows each layer to use a different subgroup. A period subgroup of rank two has injective projections A,B to both field copies. Pull back Q along those maps to forms q_A,q_B on F₃². Enumerating the 27 ternary quadratic forms and all GL₂(3) domain maps leaves 15 compatible ordered pairs up to change of basis and independent orthogonal maps on the two ambient copies (Witt extension). In the 59049-element quotient by the phase subgroup, define D(s) as the minimum of the nine lifted costs in thirds, and call s defective if any lift fails q_A+q_B=0, now denoting the ambient forms. A normalized layer contains quotient differences only when D≥12. If it contains the sites 0,s, its remaining quotient sites project injectively to the 81 first- and 81 second-coordinate cosets. Count how many first rows can supply a common D≥12 neighbor of 0,s, and likewise how many second rows; add two to cover the pinned sites. This is checked exactly via a six-coordinate ternary Fourier transform modulo 1009: true convolution coefficients are integers in [0,729]. A layer of at least 55 quotient points would need at least 53 viable rows of each kind. The row test eliminates all defective differences except 144 each in three of the 15 types. All other differences forbidden by this test can be removed globally. For each of those 432 residual defects, form the common-neighbor graph in the pruned allowed set. An exact coloring-based branch-and-bound found no 53-clique in any of them, using 792, 144 and 29088 recursive calls by type. Thus every actual pair of points in a >486-site layer has opposite quadratic distances, including inside its symmetry subgroup (the incompatible cases have no qualifying pairs). Since either coordinate support has more than 243 points, it spans its entire six-space affinely. Polarization with respect to an affine basis then forces one common affine anti-isometry on the whole layer. This proof is independent of a second, simpler classification of three-dimensional horizontal stabilizers: of 16 compatible pairs of pulled-back forms only six admit even a second 27-site coset, and all allowed cosets then preserve all quadratic distances. A genuine improvement in the surviving vertex therefore needs at least one large layer with at most three translational symmetries.
The second rigidity class has **no required translation symmetry**. Suppose one individual 4-separated layer lies in the union of three parallel complete anti-isometry graphs with displacement labels 0,d,−d, for arbitrary nonzero d. Its sites may independently select any of the three according to the individual input; no symmetry, regularity or balanced populations are imposed. If it has more than 486 sites, it is contained in a single full affine anti-isometry graph, which may be different from the three given. The threshold 486 is sharp even for two graphs.

**Proof.** Use first-block coordinates u after applying a fixed anti-isometry, so the second location is u+λd for λ∈F₃ and Q on the original first block is −Q(u). Divide sites into a 3 by 3 grid with indices λ and t=2u·d+λQ(d). Each tile has at most 243 sites. A fixed row lies in a complete graph with constant offset. If Q(d)≠0 a fixed column also lies in a complete anti-isometry graph, by the affine orthogonal reflection u↦u+(t−2u·d)d/Q(d); changing reference to that graph exchanges the two types of axes. For Q(d)=0, all first symbols of a column lie in one affine hyperplane, so the whole column has at most 243 members. If two tiles differ in both row and column they conflict via a bipartite Cayley graph on two copies of a 243-element hyperplane. Its degree and maximum nonconstant squared character magnitude (d₀,s) are (81,81) and (162,81) for Q(d)=0; (153,81) and (91,100) for Q(d)=±1. For a fixed outside tile, there is precisely one of each pair of types toward the two nonaligned tiles in a chosen row. The replay obtains these numbers by a literal sum of all 729 characters restricted to the appropriate difference hyperplane, discarding the three characters constant on that hyperplane.

For compatible populations a,b in two nonaligned tiles, the expander inequality gives d₀²ab≤s(243−a)(243−b). Thus any two tiles of size at least 25 are aligned. If at most one tile is that large, the total is at most 243+8·24=435. Otherwise all such tiles lie on one axis. In the singular-column case its 243-site maximum and the ≤24 in six outer tiles give at most 387. In every other case take this axis as the reference row (changing by the reflection if needed). Let m be the largest of its six outer tile sizes, so 0≤m≤24. With m≥2 the total is at most 243 + 6m + Σ min(243−d₀, floor(243s(243−m)/(d₀²m+s(243−m)))), the sum over the two degree types above. The largest of these bounds is 470, over all integers 2≤m≤24 and all three Q types. With m=0 the layer lies in the reference graph. With m=1 there are at most six outsiders. One outsider conflicts with exactly 244 or 245 reference sites. Any two compatible distinct outsiders conflict with at least 353 different reference sites, as detailed below, so two to six outsiders leave at most 729−353+6=382 sites. The one-outsider case gives at most 729−244+1=486, and this is attained for either nonsingular displacement by taking that outsider and every remaining compatible reference site. The resulting 486 sites really do not extend to another affine anti-isometry graph: it contains 485 points of the original graph, whereas two distinct affine maps on F₃⁶ agree on at most a hyperplane of 243 points.

**Universal stability of a large layer, without any symmetry or bank assumption.** For an arbitrary reference affine anti-isometry graph g, any two mutually compatible outsiders exclude at least 353 distinct sites of g, not merely outsiders taken from the same displaced graph. More precisely each outsider excludes 244 or 245 sites and any compatible pair's excluded sets have intersection at most 135. In first coordinates and with g normalized to linear T, put E_s={x:w(x)+w(T(x−s))<4}, with s nonzero. If the first outsider has coordinates (0,Ts), another is (a,T(a+t)), t≠0. Impose w(a)+w(T(a+t−s))≥4 and correlate E_s with a+E_t. The exact ternary convolution modulo 1009 checks every a,t and representatives of all three nonzero Q(s)-types. Compatible pairs with t not proportional to s have intersections at most 95. If k outsiders coexist and a sites remain on g, Cauchy applied to incidence multiplicities gives (729−a)[245+135(k−1)]≥244² k. For layer size a+k≥487, one outsider is impossible, two force a≤376, and substituting k≥487−a gives a≤289 by exact integer comparison. Hence any actual large nonextendible layer needed for a record agrees with **no** affine anti-isometry on more than 289 sites. It needs at least 198 genuine departures already at size 487, cannot arise by one-point or a handful of trades, and cannot be any nonlinear selection on the rank-one displacement family above.

**Operationally useful next mechanism.** In the single six-dimensional ternary quadratic space target the concretely specified H₃=1459 problem, not another scale sweep. A record needs a 487-site-or-larger nonextendible layer which (i) has at most three translational symmetries, (ii) spans more than one affine displacement line relative to every full anti-isometry, and (iii) has at least 198 departures from each full graph. A construction that switches entire large row banks is now excluded; a genuinely two-dimensional displacement plane with overlapping parity checks, or an affine-rank-high nonlinear matching, is the next model to try. The structural restrictions apply to the effective three-color layers after compression at the first 26D vertex and the randomly selected layers at the second. None of the results supplies a new repository or worldwide lower bound.
