# 95D: merging unequal-radius balls across MW92 phases

The construction yields a **95-dimensional lattice** (existential real shears), with attained squared minimum 16 and occupied fraction at least **2.13554696184854e−21**. This is **more than 0.099%** above the live nine-site simplex construction, whose stronger branch was already exact. Both the lattice-only and unrestricted lower-bound rows improve. There is no assertion of an explicit 95-dimensional Gram matrix, theoretical optimality, or independently exhausted worldwide priority.

The comparison is against [the now-live nine-cluster bound](../mw92_nine_cluster95_20261128/README.md), also in the repository catalogs, with squared tail covolume \(D_{\rm old}=14260143213204819/200000000000000\) and occupied fraction at least \(2.133434801047687\cdot10^{-21}\). The parent is [Shioda's characteristic-47 Mordell–Weil lattice](https://doi.org/10.2307/2374791), with squared minimum 16 and covolume \(47^7\); use the same [published normalization, horizontal automorphism and inherited equal-ball lemma](../mw92_nine_cluster95_20261128/README.md) as the preceding construction. These results are not assumed for unequal-ball covering. The improvement uses a new cover construction, not a Taylor rounding: **cover an intermediate forbidden ball and a smaller ball from a different torus phase by a single large ball**. Continuous choice of the shear gives the upper distance needed to do that.

## Inputs and exhaustive first two shears

Let \(d(x)=\operatorname{dist}(x,L)\), \(J^2+J+I=0\) be the prior integral isometry and \(T=I-J\). Set
\[
 A=11.00098526,\quad \varepsilon=10^{-5},\quad s=16-A,\quad t_0=16-3s.
\]
The [prior exact Coxeter–Few–Rogers input](../mw92_cfr_taylor13_20261123/README.md) forbids any ordinary periodic radius-\(\sqrt D\) covering with average at most \(N\) balls per \(L\)-cell when \(ND^{46}<A^{46}\). In particular there is a point at distance squared greater than \(A\). Along a Euclidean line from 0 to that point the distance is continuous, so **choose** \(a\) with \(A<d(a)^2<A+\varepsilon\).

- If \(d(Ta)^2\ge t_0\), retain the earlier generic shears \(a,-Ja\) with triangular squared norm \(s(i^2+ij+j^2)\). The horizontal depths for Eisenstein norm 1 and 3 are respectively \(d(a)^2>A\) and \(d(Ta)^2\ge t_0\). All larger Eisenstein norms are at least 4, for which \(4s>16\).
- Otherwise take \(h=T^{-1}v\), where \(v\) is a shortest representative of \(Ta\bmod L\), and \(u=a-h\). Then \(Tu\in L\) and \(\|h\|^2<t_0/3\). The earlier affine order-three orbit proves \(d(u)^2>A-t_0/3=32/3\) by averaging three displacements summing to zero. Since \(3\|u-x\|^2=\|T(u-x)\|^2\) is an even integer for each \(x\in L\), \(d(u)^2\ge34/3\); its class has exact order 3, as \((I-J^2)T=3I\). We additionally have the **upper** bound
\[
 d(u)\le d(a)+\|h\|<\sqrt{A+\varepsilon}+\sqrt{t_0/3}.
\]
Put \(p=14/3, B=10.78402070, q=16-B\). The established [two-strong-one-weak paired covering argument](../mw92_paired_cover_9495_20261126/README.md) gives a point \(b\) satisfying \(d(b)^2,d(b-u)^2>B\) and \(d(b+u)^2>B-2p\). Choose this point further as follows. Travel continuously from any such point to 0; the component of the open feasible portion containing the start has an endpoint since 0 is infeasible. At that endpoint at least one of the three scalar inequalities is equal, so some point inside the component has all three strict and, in addition, that one value within \(\varepsilon\) above its threshold. Thus **at least one of** \(b,b-u,b+u\) has squared torus distance below \(B+\varepsilon\). This choice preserves the earlier two-shear vertical Gram \(\left(\begin{smallmatrix}p&p/2\\p/2&q\end{smallmatrix}\right)\) and every one of its original packing constraints. The first two fixed-order shears are \(u,b\).

## New third shears and exact finite geometry

For a chosen third shear \(c\), take the full lattice generated by \(L\times\{0\}\) and the three shear–vertical pairs, using the positive definite metric
\[
G_g=\begin{pmatrix}s&s/2&-s/2\\s/2&s&-s/2\\-s/2&-s/2&16-D_g\end{pmatrix},
\quad D_g=10.55366;\qquad
G_f=\begin{pmatrix}p&p/2&p/2\\p/2&q&q/2\\p/2&q/2&16-D_f\end{pmatrix},
\quad D_f=10.55460 .
\]
The replay uses \(|z_j|^2\le (z^\mathsf TGz)(G^{-1})_{jj}\) and \(16(G^{-1})_{jj}<9\) for all coordinates. With the last coordinate positive, complete finite enumeration gives only last coordinate 1 for vectors of vertical squared norm below 16. Last coordinate 0 is checked against the first-two-shear depth cases above. In third level 1, the horizontal forbidden balls for \(c\) have centers minus the phase \(ia-jJa\) (generic), or \(iu+jb\) (fixed), and squared radii \(16-(i,j,1)G(i,j,1)^\mathsf T\). Negation covers level −1. In the fixed branch reduce \(i\bmod 3\) and retain the maximum demand per phase label. Additional coincident phase labels can only lower the count. All cases with vertical squared norm ≥16 are safe, and the zero-vertical fiber uses the attained horizontal minimum 16.

**Merging lemma.** Two torus balls of radii \(r_1,r_2\le R\) whose center distance is at most \(2R-r_1-r_2\) can be replaced by one torus ball of radius \(R\) that contains both. Take closest lifts of their center difference and a point on the connecting segment at distances at most \(R-r_1,R-r_2\) from the respective centers. This replacement pairs the full Euclidean cosets bijectively by one fixed period translation; the centers of the new balls form one full coset. It therefore also gives an ordinary periodic equal-ball covering on lifting, not only a pointwise density estimate.

### Generic: at most \(27/4\) equal balls per cell

The finite tail demands, grouped by squared radius, are:

| Squared radius | Phase labels \((i,j)\) |
|---|---|
| \(D_g\) | \((0,0),(0,1),(1,0)\) |
| \(D_g-s\) (mid) | \((-1,1),(1,-1),(1,1)\) |
| \(D_g-2s\) (weak) | \((-1,0),(-1,2),(0,-1),(0,2),(2,-1),(2,0)\) |

Match the three mid labels to \((-1,0),(0,-1),(0,2)\), respectively. Each matched difference has Eisenstein norm 1, so its center separation is \(d(a)<\sqrt{A+\varepsilon}\). Exact rational square checks give
\[
\sqrt{A+\varepsilon}+\sqrt{D_g-s}+\sqrt{D_g-2s}<2\sqrt{D_g}.
\]
The three unmatched weak phases use the parent four-site simplex faces of radius \(\sqrt6\), whose index-four translated partition is [proved in the live predecessor](../mw92_nine_cluster95_20261128/README.md). They fit since \(\sqrt6+\sqrt{D_g-2s}<\sqrt{D_g}\). The count is three strong plus three merged mid–weak pairs plus \(3/4\), hence \(27/4\).

First merge \((2,1)\) with \((0,1)\); their center distance is \(d(u)\), with the upper bound proved above. Next choose one pair from the following table, according to which almost-tight boundary condition was obtained for \(b\):

| Bounded center distance | Mid | Tiny |
|---|---|---|
| \(d(b)\) | \((1,-1)\) | \((1,-2)\) |

The matched differences agree up to sign in the torus; all these distances are \(<\sqrt{B+\varepsilon}\) in their chosen case. The exact verifier checks both types of inequality for merging, as well as \(2+\sqrt{D_f-2p}<\sqrt{D_f}\) for the one dimer phase and \(8/3+\sqrt{D_f-2q}<\sqrt{D_f}\) for the two remaining tiny phases. The dimers use any primitive min-vector partition modulo 2. The nine-site simplex of radius \(8/3\) with its translated index-nine partition is [literal and proved in the predecessor](../mw92_nine_cluster95_20261128/README.md). The count is now \(6+1/2+2/9=121/18\).

In either case, if no suitable \(c\) exists, the forbidden balls cover the torus. The demonstrated periodic replacements yield a Euclidean ordinary equal-ball cover; intersect the periods of the distinct partitions. The rational inequalities \((27/4)D_g^{46}<A^{46}\) and \((121/18)D_f^{46}<A^{46}\) contradict the published equal-ball theorem in the two cases. Thus there exists \(c\) in the applicable branch, and its lattice attains squared minimum 16 through the unchanged horizontal sublattice.
