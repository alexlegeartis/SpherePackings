# Quantitative rounding of Coxeter–Todd deep shifts, with applications in 22D and 16D

**No new density record.** The positive construction target here was to put complete Coxeter–Todd fibers over a contracted nonlattice Best packing \(P_{10c}\). The ideal 22D density had 5.72% headroom, but continuous horizontal translations do not rescue it in the range described below. A separate 16D normal architecture using \(D_4\) has 6.79% ideal headroom and encounters a different consequence of the same rounding mechanism.

## 1. Quantitative continuous-to-finite reduction

Let \(K=K_{12}\) have minimum squared norm 4. Put \(\ell(x)=\operatorname{dist}(x,K)\), a well-defined function of a phase (a class modulo \(K\)). The covolume is 27 and \(K^*\) is \(K\) scaled and rotated by multiplication by \(\theta^{-1}\), where \(\omega=e^{2\pi i/3}\) and \(\theta=1+2\omega\). We use the [Conway–Sloane Coxeter–Todd article (1983)](https://neilsloane.com/doc/Me107.pdf), Sections 2–3 and 8, specifically Corollary 2, the congruence description around (7)–(10), and Theorem 6 and Corollary 7. In this scale their covering radius squared is \(8/3\), and the \(K^*/K\) classes have depths squared \(0,4/3,2,8/3\). The classes of depth \(2\) have nearest-displacement shells containing the unit-\(\omega\) rotations of a complex orthogonal basis of six length-\(\sqrt2\) vectors. All deepest classes are equivalent by unitary lattice automorphisms and translations. In particular \(K^*\) has covering radius squared \(8/9\) and minimum squared norm \(4/3\).

**Rounding theorem.** If \(\ell(x)^2\ge5/2\), the nearest point \(c\) in \(K^*\) has deepest class \(c+K\) and \(\|x-c\|<1/5\). If instead \(\ell(x)^2\ge21/8\), the error is \(<1/25\). Consequently any set of real phases with pairwise squared torus distances at least \(5/2\) has at most seven elements.

Here is the geometric inequality. The contact polytope at a deep class contains a 12D ball of radius \(1/\sqrt3\). To see this directly, use the article's \(A^{(3)}\) model with raw vectors in \(\mathbb Z[\omega]^6\), common coordinate residue modulo \(\theta\), and coordinate sum zero modulo 3; multiply raw lengths by \(\sqrt{2/3}\). A deepest dual class contains the vectors \(\theta^{-1}a_i,\theta^{-1}b_i,\theta^{-1}c_{ij}\), and their products by \(\omega,\omega^2\). Here \(a_i=3e_i+\omega{\bf1}\), \(b_i=3e_i+\bar\omega{\bf1}\), \(c_{ij}={\bf1}-3e_i-3e_j\). They all have raw squared length 12 before division and are congruent modulo \(\theta A^{(3)}\) before division. Thus their scaled squared lengths are \(8/3\) and they are all nearest displacements of this class, up to a common sign.

Write \(u_i=e_i-{\bf1}/6\) and let \(t\) be a unit imaginary multiple of \({\bf1}\). Up to a common unitary rotation, the 27 unrotated vectors above are
\[
\sqrt2 u_i+t,\quad \sqrt2 u_i-t,\quad-\sqrt2(u_i+u_j),\qquad 1\le i<j\le6
\]
(with the first two defined for all six indices). They lie in a real six-space \(W\) orthogonal to \(iW\). Their convex hull contains the real \(W\)-ball of radius \(\sqrt{2/3}\): in its polar put \(w_i=\sqrt2 z_i\) for the component \(z\perp{\bf1}\), and let \(a\ge0\) be the absolute last coordinate. Permute \(w_1\le\cdots\le w_6\); the constraints are \(w_6+a\le1\), \(-w_1-w_2\le1\), \(\sum w_i=0\). In the five nonnegative sorted gaps and \(a\), the two rows of coefficients are \((1,2,3,4,5,6)/6\) and \((2,4,3,2,1,0)/3\). Every vertex has at most two nonzero variables; maximizing the convex quantity \(\sum w_i^2/2+a^2\) on these vertices gives \(3/2\). The short exact checker replays all vertices, not a directional sample.

For any complex direction, at least one of its three projections onto \(W,\omega W,\omega^2W\) has norm at least \(1/\sqrt2\) times its norm: the average squared projection is one half of the squared norm. The rotated real balls give the stated 12D inball by the support-function characterization of containment. Now write \(x=c+u\) for the nearest dual point and \(r=\|u\|\le\sqrt{8/9}\). If \(c+K\) is deepest, some nearest displacement has inner product with \(u\) at most \(-r/\sqrt3\), so
\[
\ell(x)^2\le8/3+r^2-2r/\sqrt3 .
\]
For a depth-two class, a component of \(u\) along an orthonormal complex axis has absolute value at least \(r/\sqrt6\). The three rotations of the corresponding length-\(\sqrt2\) vector supply a real inner product at most \(-r/(2\sqrt3)\), hence \(\ell(x)^2\le2+r^2-r/\sqrt3\). This is \(<5/2\) throughout \(0\le r\le\sqrt{8/9}\), by convexity and the endpoints. In the depth-\(4/3\) class the rotated triple has mean zero, so \(\ell(x)^2\le4/3+r^2\le20/9\); the zero class is simpler. Finally the deep quadratic lies strictly below \(5/2\) on \([1/5,\sqrt{8/9}]\) and strictly below \(21/8\) on \([1/25,\sqrt{8/9}]\), again by the endpoints. This proves both rounding estimates.

For the clique statement choose one anchor \(x_0\) and round all pair differences to deepest dual vectors \(c_{ij}\). The rounding defects in \(c_{i0}-c_{j0}-c_{ij}\) have norm \(<3/5\), smaller than the dual minimum, so this vector in \(K^*\) is zero. Thus the rounded phases form a deepest-difference clique in \(K^*/K\). This finite group is a six-dimensional vector space over \(\mathbb F_3\), since \(3K^*= \pm\theta K\subset K\). The well-defined quadratic form \(q(v)=3\|v\|^2/2\bmod3\) distinguishes depths: \(q=1\) exactly for deepest classes. Write \(B\) for its symmetric polar form with \(B(v,v)=q(v)\). If an eight-point clique existed, translate one vertex to zero. The seven remaining vertices have \(q=1\), and \(q(v_i-v_j)=1\) gives \(B(v_i,v_j)=2\) off the diagonal. Thus their Gram is \(2(I_7+J_7)\), invertible in characteristic 3, impossible in dimension six.

## 2. The scaled Best packing in 22D

The comparison is [Conway–Sloane's \(R_{22}\) or \(V_{22}\), §2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0207182), center density \(\delta_R=3(11/12)^{11}/\sqrt{12}=0.33254962459\ldots\), occupied density \(0.00245103404412\ldots\) in the [project table](../../LOWER_BOUNDS_1_96.md). The [Best \(P_{10c}\) packing described by Sloane](https://ems.press/content/book-chapter-files/27252?nt=1) has center set \(P=C+2\mathbb Z^{10}\), where \(C\) is the Gray image of the 40 words obtained from \((c-d,b,c,d,b+c)\) with \(b,c,d\in\{1,-1\}\) and their five cyclic positions modulo four. At each chosen site \(x\in P\) put a complete Coxeter–Todd fiber at any real phase \(\phi(x)\). The normal squared distance between sites is \((x-y)^{\mathsf T}G(x-y)\). Fix the full packing minimum squared at \(4\). No imposed periodicity on the selected sites or horizontal shifts. There is at most one fiber at each site, since the horizontal covering radius squared is \(8/3<4\).

In the ideal geometry \(G=I/3\), the nominal full-occupancy value is
\[
\delta_0=\frac{40/2^{10}}{27(1/3)^5}=\frac{45}{128}=0.3515625,
\]
5.72% above \(\delta_R\). But \(P\) contains a literal equilateral 10-point window of squared side 4, with the anchor \(0000011010\) and offsets checked in [verify.js](verify.js). The cyclic pair rotation and the quaternary symmetry \((w_0,\ldots,w_4)\mapsto(-w_0,w_1+2,w_2,w_3,w_4+2)\) preserve the code and are transitive on its 40 words. Their action on binary residues consists of signed coordinate permutations and integer translations, so their lifts preserve \(P\) and the raw Euclidean length of the window's edges. Averaging over these actions and all translations by \(2\mathbb Z^{10}\) bounds point occupancy by \(m/10\) if at most \(m\) sites occur in each window. For a periodic support this is finite averaging. For arbitrary supports, apply it in a large normal box or ellipsoid, with only a fixed-width boundary error; transitivity ensures a common coefficient for all 40 residue types, and the boundary error is smaller order. Since every horizontal fiber is a shift of the same lattice, its point count in a horizontal ball of radius \(R\) is its volume divided by 27 up to \(O(1+R^{11})\), uniformly over shifts. Integrating the normal bound over horizontal slices gives the same occupied-fraction cap on center density if it is measured in balls in 22D.

**All stretching metrics:** If \(G\succeq I/3\) and \(\lambda_{\max}(G)<3/8\), every pair in an occupied 10-window has horizontal squared torus distance \(>5/2\). At most seven can occur by the rounding theorem; the center density is at most \((7/10)\delta_0=63/256\). If \(\lambda_{\max}(G)\ge3/8\), then \(\det(3G)\ge9/8\) since every other eigenvalue of \(3G\) is at least 1. Even full occupancy has density at most \(15\sqrt2/64<\delta_R\). This proves the uniform upper bound \(15\sqrt2/64\) for **all** these metrics, including arbitrary off-palette real shifts. The window action need not preserve \(G\), since only its raw edge lengths and the global eigenvalue bound are used.

**Moderate scalar compression:** If \(G=sI\), \(2/9\le s<1/3\), no two points of one raw 10-window coexist because their normal squared distance is less than \(4/3\), while horizontal squared distance is at most \(8/3\). Center density is therefore \(\le 5/(34560(2/9)^5)=0.26696777343\ldots<\delta_R\). The theorem says nothing about \(s<2/9\), metrics with directions contracting below \(I/3\) outside this scalar band, lowered full minimum, or other normal supports.


### Deleting individual points cannot rescue the near-threshold 22D construction

The stretching-metric theorem still holds if at each normal site one takes an **arbitrary subset of one translated Coxeter–Todd fiber**, with different deletions at different sites and no horizontal period requirement. In the \(\lambda_{\max}(G)<3/8\) branch the new bound is \((17/20)\delta_0=153/512=0.298828125\), still below the bound in the other eigenvalue branch. This extension does not allow multiple different horizontal phases at one normal site.

For a window of ten sites put an edge \(ij\) between phases that would conflict if both translated fibers were complete. Its independence number is at most seven by the rounding theorem. A conflict furnishes one fixed difference vector of squared length below 4 in the joint metric; translating by this vector is a bijection between the two horizontal lattices, so the retained endpoints cannot each occupy the same matched slot. In a common large horizontal cube, let \(w_i\) be its retained count divided by the complete-fiber count to leading order. For a conflict, \(w_i+w_j\le1+O(R^{-1})\) for cubes of side \(R\), with a uniform error, since a shortest horizontal difference has length \(\le\sqrt{8/3}\). Similarly \(0\le w_i\le1+O(R^{-1})\). Rescaling all weights by \(1+O(R^{-1})\) gives \(0\le w_i\le1\), \(w_i+w_j\le1\) exactly. The vertices with \(w_i\le1/2\) cover every conflict edge and therefore number at least three. So \(\sum w_i\le17/2\) before restoring the vanishing error. Averaging the windows as before proves the bound in large cubes, uniformly over translation, and then in large balls (tile by large fixed cubes and let their size grow after taking the ball limit).

For scalar \(5/17\le s<1/3\), the same argument gives the smaller bound \(\frac12\frac{5}{3456(5/17)^5}=0.32867060185\ldots\), since all window pairs are conflicts and the total weight is at most five. The wider lower endpoint \(s=2/9\) above is for complete fibers only.

## 3. The compact four-dimensional normal architecture

The dimension-16 comparison is the [Barnes–Wall packing (1959)](https://doi.org/10.1017/S1446788700025064), center density \(1/16\), occupied density \(0.01470816439743\ldots\) in the project table. Take full \(K\) fibers at chosen sites of \(\sqrt{s}D_4\) with arbitrary continuous phases. Assume the **entire packing** has pure normal period \(3\sqrt{s}D_4\), i.e. this period induces no horizontal shift. Let its squared minimum be \(8/3<\mu\le4\). Then its center density is strictly below \(1/16\). This pure-period condition matters; it is not deduced merely from having the same vertical support pattern.

There is at most one phase per normal site. Let \(2rs\) be the smallest squared distance between occupied distinct normal sites, for an integer \(r\ge1\). Since every horizontal torus clearance is at most \(8/3\) and each occupied fiber repeats under a pure normal translation of squared length \(18s\),
\[
s\ge\mu/18,\qquad rs\ge(\mu-8/3)/2.
\]
Write \(F(\mu,u)=(\mu/4)^8/(54u^2)\), so \(F(\mu,s)\) is the full-occupancy density. If \(r\ge2\), the general four-dimensional normal sphere packing bound gives density at most \((4/3)F(\mu,rs)\). In detail, the 4D packing center density for sphere radius 1 is less than \(1/6\), by the elementary Blichfeldt gauge: for centers of separation 2 and \(g(x)=\max(0,1-\|x\|^2/2)\), if \(m\) translates contribute at one point, the sum of the squared distances to it is at least \(2(m-1)\), by the all-pair sum identity. Hence the sum of the \(g\)'s is at most 1, and integrating gives \(1/\int g=3/(2\pi^2)<1/6\). Rescaling this to normal separation \(\sqrt{2rs}\) and multiplying by \((\mu/4)^8/27\) gives the claimed bound. Since \(rs\ge\max(\mu/9,(\mu-8/3)/2)\), its maximum occurs at \(\mu=24/7\) or \(4\), strictly below \(1/16\): the branch with denominator a multiple of \(\mu^2\) is increasing, while \(\mu^8/(\mu-8/3)^2\) has only a minimum in the positive domain away from its left pole. The same general normal packing argument with only \(rs\ge(\mu-8/3)/2\) shows that for **arbitrary normal sites**, without lattice or period, no 16D full-\(K\)-fiber packing beats \(1/16\) if \(\mu\ge10/3\). In this separate claim interpret \(2rs\) as a lower bound on the squared normal separation, not necessarily an integral shell.

For \(r=1\) the raw bound \(F(\mu,\max(\mu/18,(\mu-8/3)/2))\) peaks at \(\mu=3\), with value \(2187/32768=0.06674194335\ldots\), 6.79% above the benchmark, and has value \(1/24\) at \(\mu=4\). To compete with \(1/16\), necessarily \(59/20<\mu<121/40\) by the same endpoint check. It also requires
\[
s\le s_*(\mu)=\sqrt{8/27}\,(\mu/4)^4 .
\]
On that interval \(\mu-2s_*(\mu)>21/8\) and \(\mu-4s_*(\mu)>23/10\): each is concave in \(\mu\), and both endpoints pass by rational squaring.

The eight-point translate window \(\{e_1\pm e_i:1\le i\le4\}\subset D_4\) has squared side 2 except four antipodal pairs of squared distance 4. Suppose all its sites are occupied. Each pair on a side has horizontal squared torus clearance \(>21/8\), so it rounds to a deepest difference with error \(<1/25\). For a fixed anchor, obtain rounded labels for each vertex by a direct side from the anchor, except its antipode uses a path of two sides. For a side between any two vertices, these labels' difference agrees with the direct rounded side, since their difference is a dual vector of norm \(<4/25\). Each of the four antipodal pairs then has horizontal difference within \(2/25\) of its rounded label difference: either both vertices were reached in one step or they are the anchor and its antipode. If an antipodal pair rounds to a shallow class, its horizontal separation is at most \(\sqrt2+2/25<\sqrt{23/10}\), impossible. The eight labels thus form a deepest clique, also impossible. Averaging the raw eight-window over translates of \(D_4\) yields occupancy at most \(7/8\). Once applied at the only otherwise competitive scales, the density is at most \((7/8)F(3,1/6)<1/16\). This closes \(\mu>8/3\) for the stated pure period, but not longer or glided periods in the low-radius range, not multiple fibers allowed at smaller minima, and not arbitrary 16D packings.
