# Composite-order depth bound by a product of theta twists

The [product argument](README.md#averaged-depth-for-every-odd-prime-without-isotropy)
also applies to every integer m>1 coprime to 6. For a phase a=v/m,
average the squared depths d(s a) over the units s mod m, identifying
opposite signs. In rank 24k with D=2k their average is at most

    D · product_{p|m}(1+1/p).

In particular, for each rank 48, 72 and conditional 96 the previous
general real covering endpoint R0 cannot be attained by any phase
whose exact order has odd part a power of 7 or 13. For 72 and
conditional 96 this also applies to all powers of 5 (for rank 48
only the prime 5 case was proved by the sharper eta argument).
An analogous exclusion holds for any m coprime to 6 whose unit
group is generated by 2 and −1 and for which the displayed
ceiling is strictly less than R0. These are exact denominator
restrictions on a possible extremizer, not a lower bound on the
covering radius and not a uniform improvement of R0.

Proof: the same Jacobi translation formula holds replacing p by m.
The scalar twists are even in s. The sum of the squares of one
representative per sign pair is zero mod m: for each prime p|m,
take a unit t with t²≠1 mod p (there is one since p≥5, and it
lifts to a unit mod m). The permutation by t of the sign pairs
implies (t²−1)Σ s²=0 mod m, hence Σs²=0 mod the entire p-power
dividing m. This gives cancellation of every automorphy phase
for γ∈Γ0(m) and the product is an ordinary scalar modular form.
It is holomorphic also at the other cusps: for a general integral
matrix, a torsion twist transforms to a constant times a theta
sum for one shifted lattice, with unit complex coefficients.
This is the same Jacobi substitution, completing the square
for the shift csv/m instead of reducing it into L. All exponents
at that cusp are nonnegative.

There are b=φ(m)/2 twists and the product has weight 12kb. At zero
the Fricke parameter counts (m/2)Σ_s d(sa); this is the
ordinary integer-exponent cusp parameter of width m. Since the
index of Γ0(m) is m·product_{p|m}(1+1/p), the valence formula
proves the mean bound. When 2 and −1 generate the units,
some doubled iterate of the odd part realizes the predicted
saving; the doubling estimate propagates it to the original
phase even in the presence of a 2-power denominator.

For p=5,7,13 the order of 2 modulo p is respectively 4,3,12.
The corresponding values of 2^order−1 are 15,7,4095,
each divisible by p but not p². Binomial lifting shows
that this order multiplies by p at each prime-power lift.
Including −1 where needed shows generation of the entire
unit group at every p-power. Compare the rational factors
1+1/p with R0/D (respectively 7/6, 11/9, 5/4); this gives
the exact exclusions above.
