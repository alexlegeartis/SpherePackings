# Exact ternary phase ceilings, and a prime-order cyclic extension

Let L be an extremal even unimodular lattice of rank n=24k, k=1,2,3,4,
with squared minimum 2k+2, and write D=2k. Rank 96 is conditional on
existence of such a lattice. The depth of a phase means squared distance
to L. This is a result on specified finite-order phases, not a new general
covering radius or density record.

* For every v in L and r=v²/2 mod 3, the depth of v/3 is at most
  D, D−4/9, or D−2/9 for r=0,1,2 respectively. If the cap is attained,
  the number of nearest points is the corresponding entry below.
* At the isotropic ceiling D the contact vectors, after normalization,
  are a spherical 2-design, including the centroid condition. At the
  r=2 ceiling their centroid is zero. No point symmetry is assumed.
* The 48D r=1 bound 32/9 is attained in **every** extremal parent,
  by more than 12.7% of its r=1 classes. This gives an actual full-period
  ternary 49D lattice lift, although not a numerical improvement.

| Parent dimension | r=0 count | r=1 count | r=2 count |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 24 | 36 | 2 | 9 |
| 48 | 1836 | 105 | 450 |
| 72 | 105084 | 6060 | 25632 |
| 96, conditional | 6334524 | 366750 | 1541727 |

## Order three: two cusps and just the needed coefficients

Use q=exp(2πiτ), a=v/3 and ζ=exp(2πi/3). For a homogeneous harmonic P of
degree j set F(P)=Σ_(x∈L) ζ^((v,x)) P(x) q^(x²/2) of weight w=12k+j.
For P=1 its first k+1 coefficients are (1,0,...,0); for j>0 they
all vanish. By harmonic Poisson summation its weight-w Fricke transform
at W=[0,-1;3,0]/√3 is
3^(w/2) Σ_(x∈L+a) P(x) q^(3x²/2), up to a fixed sign if the Fourier
convention sends a to −a. For P=1 the factor is positive. The exponents
of this transformed series all lie in Z+r/3. In particular each of the
two cusps is holomorphic, and the ordinary T multiplier at infinity
is 1.

Let A=Θ_(A2) (weight 1), B=η(3τ)^9/η(τ)^3, C=η(τ)^9/η(3τ)^3
(weight 3); the three have character χ_(−3). Their values at infinity
start A=1+..., B=q+..., C=1+...; the Fricke transforms are respectively
−iA, iC/27, i27B. The transformations of A also follow from
sqrt(3) A2* being isometric to A2. Write C1=η(τ)^3/η(3τ)
(weight 1, C1^3=C); then C1 is T-invariant, zero-free on the upper
half-plane and C1|W=−3i B^(1/3), using the holomorphic eta quotient.
It has order 0 at infinity and 1/3 at zero. The two parabolics T and
WTW^(-1), together with −I, generate Γ0(3): on the first column
(a,3b) one may first reduce |b| to ≤|a|/2 by the latter parabolic,
then reduce |a| to ≤3|b|/2 by T; iterate. (If b=0 the matrix is ±T^s.)
The T multiplier of F(P)|W is ζ^r, exactly canceled by that of
(C1^r)|W. Thus F(P)/C1^r is an ordinary holomorphic form of weight
w−r on Γ0(3) with character χ_(−3)^(w−r); at −I this is its weight
parity, and the character is trivial on the two parabolics. Holomorphy
at zero follows because the exponents, before removal of the smallest
possible exponent r/3, are nonnegative.

A basis at any nonnegative integral weight u with this character is
A^(u mod 3) B^i C^(floor(u/3)−i), 0≤i≤floor(u/3). Distinct orders
give independence at infinity, and the valence bound with index 4
gives the dimension upper bound floor(u/3)+1. Their orders at zero
are floor(u/3)−i. The coefficients c_i in this basis for P=1 are
triangularly determined up to c_k by the first k+1 coefficients at
infinity, since C1 has constant 1. The exact c_k is positive in all
12 cases. Consequently the zero-cusp order cannot exceed
floor((12k−r)/3)−k+r/3. Multiplying by 2/3 gives the depth bounds.
At equality c_i=0 for i>k, and transforming the term i=k gives
contact count c_k in type 0 or c_k/3^(3−r) otherwise. If j=1,2 and
r=0 at depth D, the normalized weighted form would have order ≥3k
at zero, while its first k+1 coefficients at infinity are zero.
This would leave only i≤k and simultaneously c_i=0 for i≤k, so
the form vanishes. This proves the two design conditions. When r=2
and j=1 the same reasoning applies with weight 12k−1 in the quotient
and order at least 3k−1 at zero.

For the 48D attainment, the rank-48 unsigned theta is determined from
E4^6, E4^3 Δ, Δ² and the initial coefficients (1,0,0). There are
23,203,775,703,480,751,884,960 vectors with v²/2 ≡1 mod 3 and
v²/2<16. There are 3^47−3^23 = 26,588,814,358,863,360,108,960
classes of type 1: the finite quadratic form over F3 is split by its
rank and determinant, and each nonzero value of the split form x·y
has 3^23(3^24−1) preimages. Each vector can cover just one class
below the ceiling; at least the difference, 3,385,038,655,382,608,224,000
classes, have depth 32/9. The lattice generated by L and (a,√(22/9))
has covolume √(22/9) and squared minimum 6. Indeed the two first
nonzero vertical levels ±1 have clearance 32/9+22/9=6 and levels
of magnitude ≥2 have vertical norm alone >6. The known half lift
uses height √2 and remains denser. For higher-dimensional vertical
extensions, the packing-family ceiling with all third-torsion levels
is the same as the old half/quarter ceiling over μ∈[D+s/12,D+2]
for s vertical dimensions: each distinct pair of vertical sites
requires squared separation ≥μ−D. The half/torsion examples attaining
that upper value need not be ternary.

## Isotropic prime cyclic subgroups

For p in {2,3,5,7,13}, suppose a=v/p has exact order p and v²/2≡0
mod p. Then **at least one** nonzero multiple of a has depth at most D.
If the shallowest nonzero multiple has depth D, its contact count,
averaged across all p−1 nonzero multiples (taking zero for ones
whose depth is larger), is exactly as follows:

| p | 24D | 48D | 72D | 96D, conditional |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 2 | 48 | 2400 | 136704 | 8222400 |
| 3 | 36 | 1836 | 105084 | 6334524 |
| 5 | 30 | 1530 | 87600 | 5281650 |
| 7 | 28 | 1428 | 81760 | 4929540 |
| 13 | 26 | 1326 | 75920 | 4577430 |

Proof: M={x∈L:(v,x)=0 mod p} has index p, Gram determinant p²
and level dividing p. Indeed M*=L+Z(v/p) and p x²/2 is integral for
x∈M* by isotropy. The classical theta transformation on Γ0(p) for
even lattices gives weight 12k and trivial character for Θ_M: the
determinant is a square. Thus F=(pΘ_M−Θ_L)/(p−1) is a holomorphic
form of that weight, with the familiar first k+1 coefficients at
infinity. Poisson, or averaging the p−1 twisted theta series,
identifies F|W_p with p^(6k)/(p−1) times the sum of the nonzero shifted
series at pτ. This step is crucial: the result for p≥5 concerns the
whole cyclic subgroup, not a single chosen generator.

Put h=24/(p−1), Ck=η(τ)^(hpk)/η(pτ)^(hk),
H=(η(pτ)/η(τ))^h. The eta quotient criterion shows Ck is a
trivial-character form of weight 12k with order zero at infinity and
(p+1)k at zero; H is a modular function with a single simple zero at
infinity and a single simple pole at zero, and no zero or pole on
the upper half-plane. For the criterion, the two eta congruences
are (0,24(p+1)k) for Ck and (24,−24) for H, and the determinant
characters are trivial since h is even. The index of Γ0(p) is p+1
and the two cusps have width 1 and p. Hence the forms Ck H^i,
i=0,...,(p+1)k are a basis, by distinct orders at infinity and the
valence dimension bound as above. The expansion of F determines c_k
exactly; this coefficient is positive in the table. This bounds the
order at zero by pk, and therefore the smallest shifted squared
norm by 2k. Fricke sends H to p^(−h/2)/H and sends η(τ) and η(pτ)
to the other eta factor with the usual square-root factors. The
leading coefficient of (Ck H^k)|W_p is p^(6k), so if equality holds,
the required mean count is c_k. In particular the p=2 isotropic
computation recovers the previous half-theorem counts independently.

For k<p one can also read c_k without triangular arithmetic:
c_k = p/(p−1) [q^k] ∏_(j≥1)(1−q^j)^(−24k).
Indeed, up to the needed order, H=q/E(q)^h and Ck=E(q)^(hpk)
with E(q)=∏(1−q^j); Lagrange inversion gives the formula.

## Averaged depth for every odd prime, without isotropy

For each odd prime p≥5 and each phase a=v/p, write d_s for the squared
depth of s a, 1≤s≤b=(p−1)/2. (Depth is invariant under sign.) For **every**
type of v, the mean depth is at most D(1+1/p). For p=5,7,13 this
improves to D(1+(1-1/b)/p).
In particular some multiple always lies at depth strictly below the
previous all-real bound R0 in 48, 72 and conditional 96, even for an
anisotropic class, when p=5,7,13.

For a fixed s define F_s by the scalar twist at s a. The product
G=∏_(s=1)^b F_s has weight 12K, K=kb, and is a holomorphic form
on Γ0(p) with trivial character, also in the anisotropic case. In
fact the unimodular Jacobi theta transformation followed by its
elliptic translation law gives, for a matrix γ=[a0,b0;c,d],

    F_s|γ = exp(−2πi cd s² v²/(2p²)) F_(ds).

Here c is a multiple of p. To see the translation directly, transform
the Jacobi variable z=(cτ+d)sv/p, then shift z by −csvτ/p, an integral
lattice period times τ. The two exponential factors combine to the
displayed one. As ds permutes residues up to sign and Σ_(s=1)^b s²=0
mod p for p≥5, the product has no multiplier. At zero the Fricke
transform is a product of shifted theta functions, so its order is
(p/2)Σ_s d_s; at infinity G starts 1+O(q^(k+1)). The valence
inequality gives the first bound from (p+1)K.

For p=5,7,13, expand G in the same eta basis C_K H^i as above.
The first k+1 coefficients determine c_k, strictly positive: since
k≤4<p, the Lagrange formula here is

    c_k = (pK/(pK−k)) [q^k] product_(j≥1)(1−q^j)^(−h(pK−k)),
    h = 24/(p−1).

Thus order at zero is at most (p+1)K−k and gives the stronger mean
bound. If powers of 2 generate the nonzero scalars modulo p up to sign,
one can combine the smaller depth in the orbit with the next subsection.
In particular **no** phase of denominator 2^j times 3, 5, 7 or 13 can
attain R0, irrespective of quadratic type. This also excludes each
prime p≥7 with the same generation property, by the first bound.
It does not address arbitrary odd denominators or arbitrary p-orbits.

## Consequence for real covering at selected denominators

For 48D, 72D and conditional 96D the preceding real covering
argument bounds a phase with dist(2x,L)^2≤B, B≥D, by
D−1/2+B/4. Indeed the maximal-depth integral half coset has a
2-design, so its competing convex endpoint is
D+B/4−sqrt(B/12)≤D−1/2+B/4; the remaining endpoint is D.
Thus if some doubled iterate 2^j x has depth ≤B, B≥D, then x has depth
at most R0−(R0−B)4^(−j), R0=4(D−1/2)/3. With B=D this applies
to all phases with denominator dividing 3·2^j; in particular for denominator
6 the bounds are 9/2 and 7 (and conditional 19/2) rather than the
larger full-real bounds. It also gives a strict saving at every
phase with isotropic odd component of exact order 5, 7 or 13 and
2-power remaining denominator: the powers of two modulo p cover all
nonzero scalars up to sign, so one iterate hits the cyclic bound.
These consequences neither claim a uniform improvement of the
real covering radius nor exclude all of its potential equality cases.
