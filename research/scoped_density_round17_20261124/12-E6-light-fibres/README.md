# Light fibres without symmetry in the 12D anisotropic ternary mask

Consider the fixed \((9/5,18/5,0)\) metric and ternary period of the earlier radical-invariant scope, with required squared separation 2. An arbitrary motif has at least 21 of its 729 actual vertical fibres of size at most one, hence size at most \(3\cdot708+21=2145\). This does not reach the 1935 needed to beat the [Coxeter–Todd](https://doi.org/10.4153/CJM-1953-043-4) density. The stronger bound 1893 still applies under radical invariance; no lower bound changes.

Let \(V=E_6/3E_6\), \(R\) its norm-form radical, and \(W=V/R\). The required distance gives \(d(u)+2d(v)\ge10\) for any nonzero motif difference \((u,v)\), using least squared lift norms. Every horizontal fibre is in a single \(R\)-coset and so has size at most three. If two fibres are heavy (size at least two), their horizontal differences cover the three lifts of their difference label in \(W\). Thus vertical depth 2 forces the labels to differ by a nonzero isotropic vector, and depth 4 forces the labels to be unequal. A literal depth-2 six-clique of heavy vertices forces its distinct labels to one affine totally isotropic plane. Four heavy depth-2 neighbors in that plane, with pairwise vertical depths 2 or 4, force another heavy vertex's label into the plane, because the neighbors' four distinct labels affinely span it. Subtracting their four isotropy relations puts the new difference in the orthogonal complement of the plane direction; its isotropy gives membership in the plane, which is maximal isotropic in nondegenerate dimension five.

From this seed, 30 verified vertices can be added by the four-neighbor rule, then 204 more. If there are at most 20 light vertices, a translate of the first 36 positions misses all light vertices because \(36\cdot20<729\). Translate back to the canonical sector. The first 36 labels lie in one plane; within the 240-position second shell, all occupied heavy vertices are then forced. For 477 additional targets, store disjoint four-neighbor groups within the second shell. To lose a target via loss of witnesses, the number of light vertices used in its witness groups must be at least the number of groups for that target. The replay finds a total upper budget of 941 witness incidences on any twenty light vertices, so at most 233 targets can have all groups hit (take the smallest group counts first). With actual light vertices also removed, \(240+477-233-20=464\) heavy vertices are forced into the plane.

A single label can be used on at most 48 heavy vertices: their vertical differences avoid depths 2 and 4 and an explicit 15-point clique for these depths gives \(\lfloor729/15\rfloor\) by averaging over translates. Nine labels of a plane give at most 432 heavy vertices, contradiction. The target 1935 permits up to 126 light fibres, so the gap remains substantial.

Replay with Python 3: python replay.py. It checks all short lift norms needed, every witness, and the incidence budget using exact arithmetic. No optimizer or random generation is needed.
