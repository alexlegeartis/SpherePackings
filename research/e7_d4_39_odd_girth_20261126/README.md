# 39D E7–D4 extension: two exact odd-cycle gates

November 26, 2026. No change to the 39D bound. The pinned [project comparison](https://github.com/alexlegeartis/SpherePackings/blob/662af9ed90d774a37f0e38d9ef471f8b38ab3277/LOWER_BOUNDS_1_96.md) is Dorofeev–Sun–Wang's [v4 rank-nine complement](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.20359v4), center density \(3^{16}/(2^{20}\sqrt5)\approx18.35925948\), occupied density about \(1.68028177365432\cdot10^{-7}\). The result here concerns an entirely different period, the project's five-\(E_7\) \(L_{35}\), and is not an upper bound on arbitrary 39D packings.

## The proposed construction and exact gate

Take the 35D [five-\(E_7\) period](../e7_hermitian35_20261123/) of minimum squared distance 8 and covolume \(2^{31/2}\). It is the kernel in \(E_7^5\) of the binary syndrome \((\sum x_i,\sum\alpha_i x_i,\sum e_i)\), where a block's coefficient parity is \((x_i,e_i)\in\mathbb F_{64}\oplus\mathbb F_2\), with slopes \((0,1,14,15,22)\) in \(\mathbb F_8\). The two field checks force nonzero field support at least three. On exactly three blocks \(x_i=zc_i\), \(c_i\in\mathbb F_8\), \(\sum c_i=0\), so the three quadratic trace values sum to zero and three norm-two choices violate the last check. This proves the stated minimum; \(2E_7^5\) provides equality. The syndrome is onto, giving the stated covolume.

Put one complete translate of \(L_{35}\) over each site of \(D_4\) with normal quadratic form \(\lambda D_4\) (multiply squared norms by \(\lambda\)). At squared minimum 8 the proposed 39D center density is \(8/\lambda^2\). A record trial \(\lambda=21/32\) would give \(8192/441=18.5759637\ldots\). Every \(D_4\) root edge has normal squared distance \(2\lambda\); hence its phase difference must have horizontal squared torus depth \(h\ge107/16\) for this trial. There are root triangles, for example \(0,e_1-e_2,e_1-e_3\); the three edges of such a triangle must simultaneously clear the same gate. In the half-grid \(h\) is a half-integer, so this requires \(h\ge7\). In the quarter-grid it requires \(h\ge27/4\).

## Exact fractional-phase laws

Write \(r\) for the \(E_7/2E_7\) radical (mask 82), and \(R=r/2\bmod E_7\) for its nonidentity class. For a half-grid shift, a block's fractional type is its doubled residue modulo \(2E_7\). It is zero, root, even nonzero or radical, with local least squared length (in units of \(1/4\)) respectively 0, 2, 4 and 6. The appendix verifier regenerates the complete exact \(E_7/4E_7\) norm table, hence checks all the local supports used below, not just example shortest vectors.

**Four radial blocks.** At their individual minima the four radial components absorb every one of the 4096 pairs of field syndromes and either value of the parity syndrome. Consequently, if the projected fractional part equals \(R\) in any four blocks, then for *any real shift \(u\)* in the fifth block
\[
h=6+\operatorname{dist}(u,E_7)^2.
\]
Additional integral shifts in any of the four radial blocks only translate the syndrome target. In particular, five radial blocks give \(15/2\). A fifth half-grid block of zero, root or even nonzero type gives \(6,13/2,7\), respectively.

**Exactly three radial blocks and two arbitrary half-grid blocks.** Here \(h\le13/2\), sharp when both other blocks are even nonzero. Here is the proof in integer quarter-units. At minimum cost 6 each, three radial blocks cover all but 36 of the 4096 field labels; parity remains free. The two unused slopes yield two independent field lines (their intersection is zero). For every choice of three radial positions, the 36 missing labels either hit every affine line of each unused slope at most once, or all lie in a single affine line of one unused slope, while still hitting lines of the other slope at most once. These properties also follow from the quadratic argument below. [verify.py](verify.py) independently checks their exact finite versions by set addition.

A root block offers 2 field labels at cost 2 and 34 at cost at most 6; an even nonzero block offers 12 at cost 4 and 44 at cost at most 8. With two even blocks, the \(12\cdot12\) different labels from their minima defeat 36 holes, for total cost 26 (also the trivial lower bound). With two roots, use \(2\cdot34>36\), total at most 26. With one root and one even, if the missing labels lie on one affine line, varying along the other slope gives at least two different parallel lines, so at least one avoids it; if every line has at most one hole the conclusion is immediate. The minimum costs 2 and 4 then suffice. With an even and a zero, \(44>36\); with a root and a zero, the 34 cheap root labels contain an affine binary hyperplane of size 32 (the local \(E_7/4E_7\) table checks this for every root mask). Even if the omitted slope is parallel to the missing-label line, its 36 missing labels form a nondegenerate quadratic level. Such a level cannot contain an affine hyperplane: constancy would force its polar form to vanish on a 5D subspace, which can have an orthogonal complement of only dimension 1. With both zero, vary the three radial field options along the one free \(\mathbb F_{64}\) parameter solving the two checks. Each block has its cheapest option at 28 parameters; \(3\cdot28>64\) guarantees two cheapest simultaneously. The third costs at most 14, again total at most 26. This proves the bound without any search over exterior geometries.

### Why exactly 36 field labels are missing

The radial minimum support from the local table is \(b+Q\), where \(b=60\) in the stated basis, \(Q=\{x:q(x)=0\}\), and \(q(x)=\operatorname{Tr}_{8/2}(x^9)\). The norm map has 9 preimages over each nonzero element of \(\mathbb F_8\), so \(|Q|=28\). Translate the three local variables by \(b\) and write the prescribed field label as \((S,T)\) in the translated coordinates. Set \(s=\alpha_1+\alpha_2+\alpha_3\), \(c_i=s+\alpha_i\). All solutions to the two field checks then have the form \(w_i+c_i z\) for one \(z\in\mathbb F_{64}\); \(c_i\) are nonzero and \(\sum c_i=0\).

Expand the three conditions \(q(w_i+c_i z)=0\) via the binary indicators \((1+(-1)^q)/2\). Every proper nonempty subset of the three polynomials has quadratic part \(\operatorname{Tr}(a z^9)\) for \(a=\sum c_i^2\ne0\) over that subset. Its polar form is nondegenerate (for any nonzero second argument, \(z^8w+zw^8\) runs over \(\mathbb F_8\)), so each corresponding Walsh sum is \(\pm8\). For the full subset the quadratic part cancels; its linear part is zero exactly when \(\sum c_i w_i=sS+T=0\). If the linear part does not vanish, the full Walsh sum is 0, giving at least \((64-6\cdot8)/8=2\) common zeros. If it vanishes and the constant is zero, the sum is \(64\), giving at least 10; if the constant is one there are none. On \(T=sS\) we may choose \(w_3=0,w_1=(c_2/c_3)S,w_2=(c_1/c_3)S\), so the constant is \(\sum q(w_i)=q(S)\). Thus the 36 missing labels are precisely \(T=sS,\ q(S)=1\), up to the initial affine translation, proving the line property.

A fourth radial minimum support absorbs all 36 holes. If its slope is not \(s\), at most one of the 28 translates can lie in the missing-label line. If its slope is \(s\) and the target lies on that line, use \(Q+Q=\mathbb F_{64}\): for nonzero \(a\) there are \(12\) solutions to \(q(x)=q(x+a)=0\), by expanding the two indicators (individual sums \(-8\); the joint sum is zero by nondegeneracy). For \(a=0\) there are \(28\). This proves the real four-radial depth formula.

## Two exact palette constructions and graph obstruction

**Half-grid, clearance at least 7 and at least three radial blocks on each edge: odd girth 15.** By the fractional-phase laws, every allowed edge has all five radial blocks or four radial blocks and an even nonzero fifth. Modulo \(2E_7\), an edge differs from the all-\(r\) word in at most one coordinate; that deviation is then root type, since adding \(r\) toggles the quadratic bit. In an odd closed walk, the deviations at each of the five coordinates have XOR \(r\). One root cannot equal \(r\), and two roots cannot sum to \(r\), since adding \(r\) changes their quadratic bit. Each coordinate needs at least three deviations, so there must be at least 15 edges. Equality is constructive: the three root masks \(1,7,84\) XOR to \(r\). Apply these three deviations in each block in turn, giving 15 edges of exact clearance 7 via the four-radial law. Their fractional sum is zero; replace the final edge's integral part so the sum is actually zero modulo \(L_{35}\), without affecting clearance. The vertices are distinct; any repetition would split off a shorter odd closed walk. The verifier checks the explicit cycle.

**Real phase torus, threshold \(6<T\le55/8\) and four radial blocks on each edge: odd girth 11.** Now project phase differences componentwise to the real \(E_7\) torus. In an odd closed walk, the deviations from the all-\(R\) word must sum to \(R\) in every block. A single deviation of \(R\) gives exceptional shift zero, forbidden by \(T>6\) and the four-radial law. There must be two deviations in every block and the length is odd, so the length is at least 11. To attain it let \(w=(7,-1,-1,-1,-1,-1,-1,-1)/8\) in the sum-zero realization of \(E_7\) as the union of integer and half-integer sum-zero vectors. Its distance squared from the integer part is \(7/8\) and from the half-integer part is \(15/8\), by rounding and correcting the sum. Moreover \(2w=R\bmod E_7\): in the checker’s verified simple-root basis \(4w=(4,1,4,4,3,2,1)\) and \(r=(0,1,0,0,1,0,1)\). Thus \(w\) is quarter-grid. Use two edges with exceptional phase \(w\) for each block, then an all-radial edge; this closes up to an integral phase, removable from the last edge at no cost, and its least squared clearance is \(6+7/8=55/8\). As above the vertices are distinct. The lower-bound part (no shorter odd closed walk) works for **every** \(T>6\); only attainment uses the indicated range.

**The real-torus threshold jumps at \(55/8\).** For \(55/8<T\le7\) the same four-radial real-edge graph has odd girth 15. Indeed two exceptions in one coordinate could only be \(u,v\) with \(u+v=R\). Both would have distance squared from \(E_7\) greater than \(7/8\), so \(u\) would have distance squared greater than \(7/8\) from the union \(E_7\cup(R+E_7)=E_7^*\). This contradicts the exact covering radius of \(E_7^*\), squared \(7/8\), established in [Worley's Voronoi-region paper](https://doi.org/10.1137/0401015) (value also stated in [*On the Covering Multiplicity of Lattices*, §8](https://citeseerx.ist.psu.edu/document?doi=34b6067bef1d2d82e89e8788174add8f1dcd636c&repid=rep1&type=pdf)). Therefore at least three exceptions per block are needed. The explicit half-grid 15-cycle uses four radial blocks on every edge and has clearance exactly 7, proving attainment. This last improvement uses the cited radius theorem; the rest of the note and the explicit cycles do not.

## Consequence for the 39D target

For squared minimum 8 and half-grid labels with at least three radial blocks in every root-edge difference, a root triangle forces \(\lambda\ge3/4\); its density is at most \(128/9\), below the pinned \(18.35925948\). For arbitrary real labels with four radial blocks on every root-edge difference, a root triangle forces \(\lambda\ge1\); its density is at most 8. Both allow arbitrary nonlinear assignments of the horizontal labels. In particular any attempt to beat the record by filling the entire \(D_4\) root shell with these very deep half-grid holes must find a low-radical deep edge on every root triangle. Neither statement excludes labels with fewer radial blocks, altered normal geometries, intermittent or multiple fibres, nor other carrier lattices.

This differs from the [earlier half-radical 39D result](../e7_half_radical39_20261123/): it does not bound all exterior Bravais lattices, but it genuinely allows nonradical and, in one block of each four-radial difference, arbitrary real torus phases. Its graph laws are sharp by the stated explicit cycles.

## Bounded constructive probe, not a theorem

Before moving to other exterior geometries, we tried the necessary root-triangle gate at \(\lambda=21/32\). One pivot was required to lie in the exact four-radial family, with arbitrary movement in the fifth quarter-grid block and arbitrary integral syndrome changes. The third vertex was free on the whole quarter-grid. The oracle regenerated every local \(E_7/8E_7\) norm, then solved the two field checks for two blocks, branching only on the other three and minimizing the parity split exactly; the candidate search itself was heuristic. In 68,124 restarts (60,006,175 candidate evaluations), the best observed minimum of the three horizontal edge costs was 6, below the necessary quarter-grid target \(27/4\). The triple \((15/2,15/2,6)\) is exactly realizable: take two shifts with radial fractional part in all five blocks, differing by the integral pure radical of one block. The difference has depth 6 since a norm-two or norm-four choice cannot put all field syndromes at zero and give odd radical parity. There was neither a complete 39D motif nor a density change. This sample does not rule out other quarter-grid triangles, and is not used in the proofs.
