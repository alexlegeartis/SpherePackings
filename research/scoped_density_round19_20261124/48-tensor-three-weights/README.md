# 48D E₈ zero-sum half-period motifs: three exceptional weights

Let the carrier be the sum-zero sublattice of seven copies of $E_8$,
scaled by $1/\sqrt2$, with squared slot weights $(a,b,c,1,1,1,1)$
and $a\le b\le c$. Allow an arbitrary motif of cosets modulo twice
the carrier, with no assumption of additivity. In the positive-definite
range the center density is at most **16,800** in each of these cases:

* all three exceptional weights are nonnegative, with no upper bound;
* all three exceptional weights are at most 1, including the full
  positive-definite range with a negative exceptional weight.

This excludes improvement over the P48 level $(3/2)^{24}=16834.11\ldots$
in these families. The case $a<0$ with $c>1$ is not covered.
This is not a global 48-dimensional density claim.

Replay: run `python3 verify.py` in this folder. Only the Python standard
library and a C++20 compiler on a little-endian host are required. The
compressed JSON files are exact rational certificates; there is no
network call or optimization solver. The relay of the earlier
two-exception Fourier verifier lives in `base.py`. We include the
standalone character/witness enumeration and its finite input
rather than depending on previously compiled tables.

## Splicing the old character bounds into the three-weight family

On the six independent scalar slots the determinant is
$$
 D=ab+ac+bc+4abc.
$$
Indeed, eliminate slot $a$ and use a rank-one update of
$\operatorname{diag}(b,c,1,1,1,1)$; positive definiteness implies
$b,c>0$ by the opposite $(a,b)$ slot vector if $b\le0$.
The covolume of the doubled period is $2^{24}D^4$, and the center
density of $N$ phases at squared minimum $g$ is
$N(g/8)^{24}/D^4$. There are at most $2^{48}$ phases.
Period contacts give $g\le8$, $g\le4(a+b)$, and
$g\le4(1+a)$.

As in the preceding two-exception theorem, the finite phase labels
are the zero-sum tuples in $V^7$, where $V=E_8/2E_8$. Fix all
but $k+1$ slot labels to obtain at most $256^{6-k}$ fibres.
Each difference fibre is identified with $V^k$. We use the exact
literal short lifts and the diagonal $O_8^+(2)$ Fourier matrices
generated by the same code as in the two-weight calculation. It
rechecks the E₈ root dictionary and that the generating isometries
lift the entire binary orthogonal group. Within a fibre with at most
two exceptional changing slots, Fourier orbits and short lifts
are unchanged; the only question is how to choose changing slots.

* When $c\le1$, regard its slot as an ordinary slot at the upper
  weight 1 whenever needed, or fix it. Raising the coefficient
  of a squared-norm summand is safe even if $a<0$.
* When $0\le a\le b\le1\le c$, never change the $c$ slot in a
  witness. For a fibre with five nominally ordinary changing
  slots, use the four literal ordinary slots plus a light slot
  at upper weight 1. All the other used shapes can use just the
  actual ordinary slots and the relevant exceptional slots.
* When $0\le a\le1\le b\le c$, freeze $b,c$; use the four
  ordinary slots and optionally $a$. The all-ordinary shape
  with five changing slots again treats $a\le1$ as weight 1.
* When $1\le a\le b\le c$, freeze $b,c$; use shapes with
  four ordinary slots, or four or fewer ordinary slots plus $a$.

The exact nonnegative character multipliers are checked by division-free
integer signs and rational comparisons in the same way as before:
if $N_j$ is the size of an allowed difference orbit, $W_{ij}$ its
character sum, $v_i\ge0$, and $z=\min_j(-\sum_i v_i W_{ij}/N_j)>0$,
a fibre has size at most $1+\sum_i v_i/z$. This applies to nonlinear
motifs. Scoping to a fibre counts distinct changing labels, not any
unproved independence of their choices.

## Parameter coverage

**Light nonnegative weights.** For fixed $a,b$, the preceding fibre
bounds and period cap $4(a+b)$ are independent of $c\le1$.
The determinant increases with $c$, so it is enough to bound
$D\ge D(a,b,b)=b^2+2ab+4ab^2$. Set $t=a+b$; because
$b\ge t/2$ and $b\le t$, one has $b^2+2ab=b(2t-b)\ge3t^2/4$.
The trivial phase cap therefore gives
$$
 \rho\le 2^{24}(4/3)^4t^{16}<16800\quad(t\le3/5).
$$
For the remaining interval the lower determinant is at least $27/100$.
We verify the low-minimum window $g\le7$ by bisecting the closed
$(a,b)$ square. Every tile uses the determinant at the lower
corner or the analytic floor and a period/contact/light-slot bound
at the upper corner. The prior high-window theorem for arbitrary
diagonal slots applies to $g>7$, since the second-largest slot
weight is 1.

**Light weights with $a<0$.** Positive definiteness gives
$a> -1/(4+1/b+1/c)\ge-1/6$. At small positive $D$ use the
rank-three period sublattice in a single E₈ root direction:
its slot coefficients are
$(-(u+v+4r),u,v,r,r,r,r)$, with $u,v,r\in\mathbb Z$.
The effective 3×3 form before the period length factor 4 has
determinant (4D). The [classical rank-three Hermite bound](https://www.mathnet.ru/links/0557d289084eb89109496216d67c52cd/rm4080_eng.pdf)
$\gamma_3=2^{1/3}$ gives $g\le8D^{1/3}$, whence
$\rho\le 2^{48}D^4$. This directly closes $D\le11/4000$.
For the rest, every exact box lower-bounds $D$ by the
minimum of the eight corners or $11/4000$, because $D$
is affine separately in each variable. The displayed period
sublattice supplies individual contacts with
$|u|,|v|,|r|\le5$, evaluated at the upper corners. Their
positive slot-norm coefficients make every such evaluation an
upper bound on minimum throughout that box. Again $g>7$
is covered by the prior universal diagonal high-window theorem.

**Heavy but nonnegative weights.** When $c\ge1$ and $a,b\le1$,
lower $c$ to 1 in the determinant; the old positive
two-exception tree verifies $g\le7$ using its phase bounds with
the slot choices above. If the lowered determinant is at most
$389/2000$, the old tree bypasses the cell: here use $D\ge a+b$,
$g\le4(a+b)$, and $a+b\le389/2000$ to get the trivial bound
$2^{24}(389/2000)^{20}<16800$. This does not require any witness
changing the heavy slot. For $0\le a\le1\le b\le c$, lower both
$b,c$ to 1 in the determinant. The old mixed two-exception
tree verifies $g\le7$. The old period cutoff in these positive
charts is exactly $\min(7,4(a+b))$ or $\min(7,4(a+1))$, with
each witness using only the indicated light or ordinary pair,
so no heavy slot is needed for those cutoffs. For
$1\le a\le2$ with $a\le b\le c$, lower $b,c$ to $a$
in the determinant and use a one-dimensional certificate with
$g\lemin(8,7a)$. For $a\ge2$, use $D\ge44$; the trivial
phase bound suffices when $g\le5$, while the four-ordinary
fibre bound 1606 suffices when $g>5$.

To finish all remaining positive heavy windows, the earlier
[all-diagonal high-window theorem](ALL_DIAGONAL_HIGH_WINDOW.md)
covers $g>7b$ whenever $b\ge1$, because $b$ is the
second-largest weight. With $g\le7b$ and
$g>7\max(1,a)$, write $s=g/7>1$. Freeze $b,c$ and bound the
other five weights by $max(1,a)$. The exact five-ordinary
fibre bound above rescaled minimum 7 gives at most
$1243\cdot256^2$ phases. Also $b,c\ge s$ and
$a\ge g/4-1$ by the period contact. Positive monotonicity yields
$$
 D\ge D(g/4-1,s,s)=7s^3+s^2/2-2s.
$$
The density bound increases with $s\in[1,8/7]$, because the
logarithmic derivative has the sign of
$6D-sD'=21s^3+2s^2-10s>0$. The endpoint $s=8/7$ gives
$D\ge432/49$ and the exact cap
$1243\cdot256^2(49/432)^4<16800$.

All tiled verifications use the same interval convention: if endpoints
of two successive allowed minima here are $l<h$, work in
$l<g\le h$; a literal upper witness of squared length at most
$l$ excludes a difference in that interval. Thus equality on any
tile or spectral band boundary is covered without a floating tolerance.
