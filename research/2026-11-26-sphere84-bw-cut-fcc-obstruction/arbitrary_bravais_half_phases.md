# Sharp arbitrary-Bravais bound for 19D half-integral extensions of two binary fibers

For either the homogeneous or the new third length-16 fiber in README.md, put one entire H_U coset above every point of ANY 3D Bravais lattice L; the horizontal phase map must be additive into (1/2)Z^16/H_U. With squared minimum at least 8, covol(L)≥1, sharply; the center density is at most √2/16. The cubic fixture attains it. This strictly strengthens the regular-FCC exclusion and covers all six real Gram parameters with no stacked-layer or small-period assumption. Horizontal H_U is fixed with min²8, as before. Neither nonlinear maps nor arbitrary smaller target minima, horizontal zoom or real horizontal phases are covered. For the repeated U type the arbitrary-Bravais conclusion is NOT established.

**Short-vector gap.** Whenever x∈L has squared length t<3/2, its phase has depth ≥27/4 because 8−t>6.5 and depths are quarter-integers. The double phase has integer depth ≥3 because 8−4t>2. The mask analysis and exact 560+1820 evaluations in sphere84_fcc.cpp for each of the two U prove such a phase is integral, with odd sum (otherwise the double is zero). Thus all nonzero x have t≥1 (the double of an odd integral phase has depth four). An even integral phase forces t≥2 by doubling. In particular distinct short u,v give squared lengths ≥2 for u±v whenever nonzero.

For a three-dimensional lattice of minimum ≥1, covol ≥1/√2 by the FCC theorem. We also use its immediate consequence covol(L)≥λ₁λ₂λ₃/√2 for Euclidean successive minima λ₁≤λ₂≤λ₃. To verify it, choose an adapted flag spanned by independent vectors realizing these lengths and containing all vectors strictly shorter than the next minimum (choose a complete flag within each tie). Scale the successive orthogonal lines by 1/λ_i. A vector belonging first to the ith subspace had norm ≥λ_i, and the smallest applied factor on that subspace is 1/λ_i. The image lattice has minimum ≥1. This proves the displayed product bound without assuming those independent vectors were a lattice basis.

Case 1: λ₂²≥3/2. Then covol(L)≥(3/2)/√2>1.

Case 2: λ₂²<3/2≤λ₃². Choose two shortest independent u,v, squared lengths a,b∈[1,3/2), and reverse one sign to have c=u·v≥0. They generate the full section in their plane: for an arbitrary section vector write its real coefficients and round independently to neighboring integers with mean the original coefficient. The expected squared remainder is ≤(a+b)/4<3/4, so there is a remainder shorter than the minimum unless zero. The inequality ||u−v||²≥2 gives c≤(a+b−2)/2. Their parallelogram splits along u−v into two congruent triangles with sides √a,√b,√(a+b−2c), area √P/2, P=ab−c². Every point in the triangle is within its circumradius R of a vertex (barycentric squared-distance identity), so the plane has covering radius at most R with R²=ab(a+b−2c)/(4P). If h is the distance between neighboring planes of L parallel to this primitive section, take a site on the neighboring plane above a point within R of a section site: its lattice displacement has norm at least √(3/2) because it leaves the plane. Therefore covol(L)²=P h²≥(3/2)P−ab(a+b−2c)/4. This is concave in c. At c=0 the value ab(3/2−(a+b)/4) is increasing in both variables on [1,3/2]² and is ≥1. At the other endpoint it is ab−3(a+b−2)²/8≥1: put a=1+x,b=1+y, x,y≥0 and x+y≤1. Done.

Case 3: λ₃²<3/2. For each z∈L, decompose in a real basis of three independent short u_i and round its coefficients independently as above. Some remainder has squared norm at most ∑||u_i||²/4<9/8, so is either zero or has integral phase. Each u_i does too. This forces the phase of *every* z∈L to be integral. In the finite integral-phase image, doubling always lands in the same two-element sheet subgroup. Since there are at most three generators, the image has cardinality ≤16 (image/2image has size ≤8). The kernel in physical space has squared minimum ≥8, hence 3D covolume at least (√8)^3/√2=16. Its index ≤16 proves covol(L)≥1.
