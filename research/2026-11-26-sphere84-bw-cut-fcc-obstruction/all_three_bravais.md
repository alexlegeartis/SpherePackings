# All three self-orthogonal four-bit fibers: completing the repeated-type case
Same model and fixed squared minimum ≥8 as arbitrary_bravais_half_phases.md. For the remaining repeated type (the eight identical-coordinate pairs), the same arbitrary-Bravais upper bound √2/16 holds; unlike the other types this note provides no attaining example for this particular type. Thus this entire family of half-integral additive extensions cannot beat the 19D comparator.

If x∈L has squared norm t<3/2, the root gap argument in README.md gives either (i) an integral odd phase (depth≥7), or (ii) a half phase with support size 3 or 4, touching exactly two duplicate pairs and having pair XOR one on all other pairs. Hence every nonzero displacement has norm²≥1: integral short phases have double depth four; nonintegral short double phases have a binary mask of weight ≤4 with depth ≤4, so 4t+4≥8. Case (i) has high-bit XOR one on all but at most one pair: in the eight pairs put r_j equal the sum of the two component signs. For every character of F2³, |W|=|Σr_jχ(j)|≤2 by depth≥7 and the all-one word of the code. Parseval gives Σr_j²≤4; each pair with equal upper bits contributes four.

For two short vectors, the horizontal phase of either sum or difference has fractional mask of size m≤8. At least four complete pairs were untouched and had opposing upper bits in both inputs; on each of these, the resulting upper bits have XOR zero. This holds for ± signs, since on a common pair there are no fractional bits or borrows and integer signs are the same modulo 2. In the coordinates outside the resulting mask form the eight new signed pair sums r_j, allowing one or two entries absent. Thus Σr_j²≥4·4; max|Σr_jχ(j)|≥4. The minimum punctured Hamming distance is therefore at most (16−m−4)/2 because U contains all signs of each character. With at least one mismatch, horizontal depth ≤m/4+(16−m−4)/2≤6. With zero mismatches its sheet correction is ≤2 when m>0, or ≤4 if m=0; this also suffices. Consequently nonzero u±v from any two short displacements have squared norm ≥2. This establishes cases 1 and 2 from the previous note directly.

For case 3, take three independent short vectors generating L'. Since covol(L') <(3/2)^(3/2) and covol L≥1/√2, their index is at most two. At index two the nontrivial coset contains all signed half-sums of some nonempty subset of those basis vectors. The mean squared norm over these signs is strictly below 9/8. Select a coordinate in the subset and pair choices differing by its sign. Their difference is a short basis vector. They cannot both have norm²<3/2 since their difference would have norm²≥2. Every half-sum in the coset has norm²≥1, so the mean must be ≥(1+3/2)/2=5/4. Impossible.
 
Thus the three vectors form a genuine basis. Write their squared lengths as 1+δ_i, δ_i∈[0,1/2]. The short-sum conditions imply |g_ij|≤(δ_i+δ_j)/2. All matrices in this polytope are SPD by row diagonal dominance margin ≥1/2. Concavity of logdet permits minimization on the indicated 64 corners: for fixed δ, first select endpoint signs of each offdiagonal by concavity; at each fixed sign tuple the matrix is affine in δ, so again choose box endpoints. In units 1/64, the minimum determinants grouped by number of nonzero δ_i=0,1,2,3 are respectively 64,88,112,128, by det G=g11g22g33+2g12g13g23−g11g23²−g22g13²−g33g12². Therefore covol L≥1. For the repeated fiber the proof excludes a record but does not assert attainment; for the full class cubic constructions attain the bound.
