# Classifying the integral BW cuts, their regular FCC half-layers and all cubic equality lifts
19D, November 26, 2026. No global packing improvement.

Let U be a four-dimensional binary length-16 minimum-weight-at-least-8 self-orthogonal code and H_U=∪_{u∈U}(u+2D16). These assumptions guarantee it is an additive lattice, of minimum squared norm 8 and volume 8192. There are exactly three U up to coordinate permutations and changes of basis (not only the two hyperplanes of RM(1,4)). The sum of its 15 nonzero weights is eight times the number of nonzero columns in any generator, so its excess over 120 is 0 or 8. On a self-orthogonal code wt(c)/2 modulo 2 is linear; a nonzero such form would force eight words to have weight at least 10, impossible. Thus all weights are divisible by 4. Either all 15 weights equal 8, there is one weight 16, or there are two weights 12. Fourier inversion of the binary column-multiplicity function now fixes U: respectively one of every column j in F2^4; twice each column of an affine hyperplane not containing 0; and multiplicities 1−(χ_a(j)+χ_b(j))/2 for two different nonzero characters a,b. Example columns for the last are [1,2,3,3,5,6,7,7,9,10,11,11,13,14,15,15].

## Positive construction, with a global isometry caveat
For the third fiber, let g1,g2,g3 be binary integer masks (1367,1643,16761) and above each p∈Z³ put the entire translate H_U+∑p_i g_i. Individual, pair-XOR, and triple-XOR Hamming distance to U are respectively (7,7,7), (6,6,6), and 5. All three generators have odd weight, so doubled phases equal the nonzero common sheet of depth four. These conditions check all outer vectors of squared norm <8: units, signed pairs/triples, ±2e_i, and mixed 2e_i with one/two unit entries. In the latter two cases the binary mask is a generator or pair, so suffices. Its minimum is 8, volume 8192, center density √2/16. CHLWW is 0.088958102129..., higher. The same test is necessary for any integral homomorphic cubic assignment at unit scale: ≤3 unit steps, and doubling, impose exactly the seven Hamming distances and odd generator conditions. For any triple modulo U, sheet choices do not change these tests.
 
This equality construction is ISOMETRIC to the old one. Project modulo 2 to the [19,7,8] code generated by U padded by zeros and the three g_i padded by successive unit vectors. The full lattice contains 2D19: double a generator for each exterior axis and cancel its even horizontal displacement modulo 2D16, using odd weight. Its index over 2D19 is 128. Two lifts with the same parity code may differ by the sheet chosen above each codeword; since their multiplication cocycles agree, the difference is an F2-linear form on that code, hence dot product with a binary mask. Sign changes in the indicated coordinates implement it. It therefore suffices to compare codes. The standard-library sphere84_isometry.py enumerates all canonical odd deep masks and their unordered cubic triples and explicitly searches for an invertible binary 7×7 transformation taking one column multiset to the other. Results (deep classes, successful triples): repeated (128,0); homogeneous (44,448); third (32,448). All 896 triples map to the single homogeneous (254,855,1389) fixture. Relative marks within U and the three individual generator signs/sheets do not change the conclusion. There is no genuinely different 19D baseline here, although the cut-and-repacking neighborhoods differ.

## No 19D win for regular FCC + half-integral additive phases at any zoom
Exterior L is a similarity of D3, with shortest squared norm s; place one translate of H_U above each point, using a homomorphism from L into (1/2)Z16/H_U. At minimum at least 8 its advertised normalized density is 1/(8s^(3/2)). The true minimum cannot exceed 8 because H_U has pure norm eight. A win needs s<1.26 by the published comparator. Every nearest-root horizontal phase g must have squared depth at least 27/4 (depths are quarter-integers), and 2g at least 3 (integer). For two orthogonal shortest edges their difference has exterior norm 2s, requiring horizontal integer depth at least 6 whenever those phases are integral. These are necessary over the WHOLE winning range, not just at the candidate s=5/4.

Represent a half-phase by fractional support A (m=|A| coordinates at 1/2), a high bit b and an overall central sheet. Write d=min_{u∈U} wt((b XOR u) outside A). For m>0 its depth is exactly m/4+d when d≥1: an odd integral mismatch supplies either sign to repair D parity. If d=0 its depth is at most m/4+2, which cannot reach 27/4. For m=1 or 2 the double has nonzero binary mask A not in U, thus squared depth at most m<3. For m≥8 the arithmetic average d over u is at most (16−m+1)/2 since there is at most one all-zero column. Hence the cost is at most 8.5−m/4≤6.5. For m=6 use the integer bound floor(11/2)=5. For m=5 and 7 reaching 27/4 would require at least 6 or 5 binary mismatches respectively; even the mean is that large only for the homogeneous type, with coordinate zero outside A fixed to one, and equality for every u. Constant mismatch counts over u mean the Walsh transform of the signed indicator of the remaining coordinates is constant; inversion forces support only at the zero column, inconsistent with 11 or 9 remaining distinct columns. Thus besides integral roots only m=3,4 need checking.

Replay sphere84_fcc.cpp takes TYPE M as arguments. For each of 560 or 1820 supports A and all 4096 b modulo U, it checks the punctured distance formula exactly. For both homogeneous and third type no A whatsoever at m=3 or 4 can reach 27/4. All nearest roots must therefore be integral and odd (otherwise their doubles have zero depth). But three nearest edges in an FCC triangle cannot all be odd under an additive map.

For the repeated fiber, the replay instead finds exactly 112 and 28 viable A for m=3,4. Group into eight coordinate pairs with identical columns. For m=3 the signs 1−2*(b XOR u) outside A have length 13, and their sums for all Walsh characters of F2³ have absolute value ≤1 because the code includes the all-one word. Parseval forces the squared sums of the eight per-pair signed contributions to total ≤1. Thus one whole pair and one additional coordinate were removed, and the b bits on every completely untouched pair have opposite parity (their XOR is one). For m=4, the analogous 12-sign sums must all vanish; two pairs were removed and all untouched pairs likewise have XOR one. Consequently in an FCC tetrahedron no three vertices have mutually different fractional masks: from one vertex, the two other differences touch at most four pairs in all. Take an untouched pair. On that pair the three vertices have identical fractional coordinates; all three high-bit pair XORs would have to be mutually different, impossible. Thus the four vertices occupy at most two fractional classes; no three can share one class by the odd-integral triangle argument. Exactly two classes of two remain. The two opposite same-class edge phases u,v must each have odd weight and Hamming depth ≥7. These opposite edges of a regular tetrahedron are orthogonal. Thus u−v needs integer depth ≥6, but among all 128 eligible odd classes no pair has Hamming distance modulo U ≥6 (verified by the same replay; for coincident classes the distance is at most four). Contradiction. This argument does not cover different Bravais metrics, nonlinear assignment, smaller fibers, arbitrary real phases, or horizontal rescaling.
