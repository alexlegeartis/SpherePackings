# A different carrier in 17–19D: half-full packets force a bipartition

Take two horizontal copies of E8/sqrt(2), independently periodic modulo sqrt(2) E8. Horizontal period covolume is 16 times 16. Write V=E8/2E8. Its quadratic form q(x)=|x|²/2 modulo 2 has nondegenerate polar form by unimodularity. The scaled squared depth of a nonzero class is 2 for q=0, and 1 for q=1; the short independent e8_check.py verifies the three exact depth counts in the usual even-sum integer/half-integer model. Thus in any one block a set of symbols all of whose pairwise differences have depth two lies in a translate of a totally singular subspace. Indeed translate one point to zero and polarize. Its affine dimension is at most 4, since it is perpendicular to itself for a nondegenerate bilinear form on 8 dimensions.

Place a horizontal packet of exactly 8 or exactly 16 distinct pairs of symbols above each occupied exterior site in arbitrary Euclidean k-space (k=1,2,3). The individual packets can be nonlinear, mutually inequivalent, and the exterior sites need not form a lattice. Impose physical minimum distance two. If two sites have exterior distance <1, their two packets' union must have horizontal squared separation at least 4 because the horizontal squared depths are integers. Within one site the same holds. In the first E8 projection alone, every pair then has distinct symbols at depth two: the other projection contributes at most two. A single 16-packet consequently is isolated from every other exterior site at distance <1. Any two adjacent 8-packets project to complementary 8-subsets of a 16-point affine singular flat.

**Bipartition.** Draw an edge at exterior distance <1 between occupied 8-packets. If one packet spans affine dimension four, every neighbor is its unique complement in that same flat. This complement also spans four: the complement of an affine hyperplane of an affine binary four-space is another hyperplane. Hence the component alternates two complementary patterns. Otherwise an 8-packet is an affine three-flat with direction T. All its neighbors are parallel translates of T and nonidentical. From a fixed root with offset u their offsets all lie in u+T-perp: for any adjacent pair the offset difference is singular and perpendicular to T. In the two-dimensional nonsingular quadratic space T-perp/T the nonzero singular classes number either zero or two. If the component has an edge the latter holds and the two classes form a binary basis. A linear functional taking both to one toggles along every edge. Thus all components are bipartite, including the nonlinear case.

Color the 8-packets by that bipartition and place each isolated 16-packet in both colors, with multiplicity one in each. Each color of exterior positions has minimum distance at least one; normalized total horizontal packet multiplicity is eight times the total number in the two color lists. If lambda_k denotes the maximum intensity for separation one in exterior k-space, center density in dimension 16+k is therefore at most lambda_k/16. For k=1,2,3 the respective ceilings are 1/16, 1/(8 sqrt(3)), and sqrt(2)/16. This ties the 17D *numerical* comparison as an upper ceiling (no attainment claim here) and misses the 18D Bierbrauer–Edel and 19D Chen–Hu–Li–Wang–Wu comparisons. It covers arbitrary independent 8/16 packets, not intermediate packet sizes or arbitrary off-grid horizontal phases. The applicable 2D/3D intensity bounds and the 19D record are given in [Chen et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.02394v1#S3); [Bierbrauer–Edel](https://www.yvesedel.de/Papers/Sphere.pdf) gives the 18D comparison (3/4)^9.

In the one-dimensional exterior only, even unrestricted packet sizes cannot beat 1/16: every half-open interval of length one contains a single combined set requiring integer horizontal depth >=4; its first projection therefore contains at most 16 symbols. Averaging intervals bounds the exterior weighted intensity by 16, and horizontal covolume is 256.
