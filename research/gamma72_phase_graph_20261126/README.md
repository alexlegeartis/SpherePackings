# Integral Γ72 phases: a sharp obstruction in 77–78D and new equidense lattices

No higher density is claimed. At minimum squared distance 8 the new explicit lattices in ranks 77 and 78 have the incumbent Gram determinants 6 and 27/4. They are **not isometric** to the earlier determinant-matching [half-root lifts](../gamma72_root_tilt_20261122/). There is also an inequivalent lattice of determinant 27/4 in 79D; it remains below the unrestricted 79D packing. The main theorem is a sharp density ceiling for the whole D4-layer architecture below, not for arbitrary 77D or 78D packings.

## The complete short-distance compatibility graph

Use the three polarized Leech blocks of [Nebe, §§2–3, especially Lemma 3.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1008.2862), with the explicit [Γ72 certificate](../gamma72_depth6_20261122/). An **integral phase** is a whole translate of Γ72 by a point of the integral Leech-block superlattice. This is a 36-bit quotient, not the space of all horizontal translations. A phase has labels (a,b,t), each 12 bits: relative A-labels of its first two blocks against the third and the total B-label. Block A-labels are therefore (z+a,z+b,z) for arbitrary z, with addition mod 2. Horizontal squared lengths are half the sum of the three ordinary Leech squared lengths. Let M(u) be the 24 distinct B-labels of norm-4 classes for nonzero A-label u; M(0) is empty. The inherited verifier enumerates all 196,560 minimal Leech vectors. In dual binary coordinates the quadratic value of a Leech residue is the dot product of A and B mod 2.

Every nonzero Leech residue has a representative of squared length 4, 6 or 8, and the anisotropic ones have minimum 6. If a,b are dependent, a single occupied block realizes the phase at squared length at most 4. For independent a,b, the three choices of a zero block give horizontal representations using any two different A-labels in T={a,b,a+b}. Give one nonzero block an arbitrary norm-4 representative and the other the required B-label: its representative has norm at most 8. Thus the squared depth of every integral phase is at most 6.

For depth 6 it is necessary that for every ordered pair of distinct u,v in T, and every x in M(u), one has v·(t+x)=0: otherwise put a norm-4 and a norm-6 vector in those two blocks. This forces v to annihilate the differences of M(u). All three resulting affine dot conditions on t must also agree. Enumerating these necessary conditions gives 3,315 unordered triangles before the consistency check, 975 afterward, or 5,850 ordered projected labels R={(a,b)}. This is deliberately a **superset**: a 4+4 representation disqualifies some choices of t.

Join x,y in R when x+y also belongs to R. Each link consists of exactly 14 vertices in seven disjoint edges; for y in the link of x, its only neighbor inside the link is x+y. No two distinct vertices have more than four common neighbors. The respective numbers of other vertices with 0,1,2,3,4 common neighbors are 5713,94,40,0,2, independent of the vertex. Thus no five arbitrary integral phases are pairwise at squared distance 6: translate one to zero, and the four others would project to a K4 in this graph. Four are attainable by an existing D4 root tetrahedron. Every four mutually deep phases project onto an affine binary 2-plane.

## Consequences for carriers and layered packings

* At the sites of D5 (minimum squared spacing 2), the five points e_i−e_5 form a root clique. At most four sites of every translate can support full integral-phase fibers. Averaging their translates bounds the upper occupancy by 4/5, even for nonperiodic nonlinear colorings. A 77D gain needs occupancy greater than sqrt(2/3)>4/5: the D5 carrier has determinant 4 versus 6. The same averaging bounds D6 occupancy by 4/6, below its 78D gate. For D5 this is stable even if actual fibers deviate from integral phases by less than (sqrt(6)−sqrt(5))/2 in horizontal length after a shared translation: one base pair in any 5-clique has squared depth at most 5 and its distance still remains below sqrt(6).
* Consider a site set made of full unit D4 layers in parallel four-planes, with arbitrary independent rotations within the parallel planes, lateral shifts, colors and transverse locations. Every site supports one whole integral phase. A D4 Delaunay cell is an orthoplex with vertices ±e_i after an isometry. These orthoplexes fill space: their centers are the odd integral vectors and all half-odd integral vectors; at each center the eight nearest D4 points form four perpendicular antipodal pairs at radius 1. Their Delaunay interiors are disjoint and the three centers per D4 fundamental cell give total volume 3·(2/3)=2, exactly its covolume.
* For any lateral projection x inside such an orthoplex write m=max |x_i| and sum |x_i|≤1. If m≥1/2, a nearest vertex has squared distance at most 2(1−m)^2≤1/2. Otherwise ||x||²≤m≤1/2, and choosing the nearer sign on each of the four axes gives four mutually adjacent vertices, all within squared distance 3/2. In the first case a transversal gap h²<3/2 would require horizontal depth greater than 6. In the second case the original four vertices must have pairwise depth 6; the extra point would also have depth 6 from each (all four vertical distances are strictly below 3). The K5 obstruction forbids it. Thus every two distinct full layers have transverse separation **at least sqrt(3/2)**.
* In transverse dimension 1 or 2 the exact maximal center intensities at this spacing are the equally spaced points and the hexagonal packing. Combined with covolume 2 of the D4 layers, the squared effective covolumes are respectively **6 and 27/4**, both attained by the exhibited lattices. In transverse dimension 3 the Kepler bound similarly gives 27/4, attained here but below the separate unrestricted 79D result. This is a theorem at the stated normalization, not an optimality assertion allowing rescaled parents or arbitrary real horizontal phases. In fact each individual full D4 layer is forced to use a single affine plane of A-colors: its Delaunay facets are K4s; neighboring facets sharing three vertices have the same fourth projected color. Opposites in every orthoplex have equal projected label; the displacements generate 2Z^4+Z(1,1,1,1), of index four in D4.

A different 78D positive target, the scaled dual E6 with vertical Gram (3/2)C^−1 for the E6 Cartan matrix C, would give determinant 243/64 and density gain 4/3. This target cannot use any linear exponent-two integral phase map, not even after changing all the A-labels. Of the 63 nonzero coefficient parities, 27 attain vertical square 2, the other 36 attain 3: all require horizontal depth at least 5 and therefore nonzero projected label, forcing injectivity of a linear projected map. The 27 root parities make a graph under adjacency when the sum is again a root parity. A pair has five distinct common neighbors. Its image would contradict the maximum codegree four proved above. See the finite Cartan-matrix calculation in verify_extra.py. Arbitrary nonlinear and nonintegral E6-dual schemes are not excluded.

## The positive construction and a small determinant target

Use the same vertical Grams as the half-root lifts, but the seven explicit rows of [phases.json](phases.json). Set missing final coefficients to zero for ranks 5 and 6. For coefficient z define the following matrix over F2:
```
U(z) = [ z1+z3+z4    z2 ]
       [ z5+z7       z6 ]
```
For odd parity (meaning z is not entirely even), the exact horizontal depth is 6 if U has rank 1, 5 if it has rank 2 and 4 if U=0. Even parity has depth zero. Each of the 128 values is the greatest possible for the corresponding fixed A-projection (the zero coefficient parity always maps to zero under a binary linear map). The single set of seven actual phases attains all 128 simultaneously. Versus the old phases this raises 2,6,24 parities, respectively, from 4 to 6 and does not lower any. To compute depth exactly, for independent a,b check for a 4+4 representation, then a 4+6 representation; if neither exists, depth 6 is attained by the upper bound above. The analogous one-block test is exact in the dependent case: competing two-block representations have total raw norm at least 8.

For any new vertical Gram V there is now a complete low-dimensional test at minimum 8: all nonzero z must have V[z]≥2, rank-2 parities need V[z]≥3, and non-even parities with U(z)=0 need V[z]≥4. An even vector is twice an integral vector and automatically has V[z]≥8. The expensive 72-dimensional coset problem is gone. A particularly concrete target is an even integral Gram G=2V of minimum at least 4 with G[z]/2 mod 2 equal to det U(z) and no length-four vector in the kernel of the prescribed map U. In rank 5 this kernel need not be the whole radical of the restricted bilinear form. A determinant below 192 in rank 5 or 432 in rank 6 would beat the records. No such G is asserted.

The horizontal parent contacts are identical. Numbers of squared-norm-8 vectors *outside* Γ72, in the old/new lattices, are 3,837,532/3,837,340 (77D), 4,943,844/4,943,268 (78D), and 6,605,112/6,603,888 (79D). These differences in the total kissing number prove non-isometry to the respective old lifts. The count uses 2,2,48 minimal representatives per type-4,-6,-8 Leech residue (Nebe, Lemma 3.1); for a horizontal class of depth≤6 one counts one, two, or three occupied blocks. Three occupied blocks can contribute only at 4+4+4. All vertical coefficients of norm≤8 lie in the exact inverse-Gram coordinate box. This distinguishes the lattices without claiming historical uniqueness, and fewer contacts do not prove flexibility.
