# Sphere156: sharp 14D two-bank bound with arbitrary planar normals

**No new density record.** This proves that the triangular Hamming–Witt lower bound remains sharp when the normal sites move arbitrarily in the plane, not just on an equilateral lattice, for the bank specification below. Separately, the actual Hamming scaffold already forces the precise allowed locations of every potential new point. Compare the live [14D construction and exact center density](https://github.com/alexlegeartis/SpherePackings/tree/43df41742f55bceecca95081c4e50f874e143efd/constructions/dim14) and the original [Leech 1964 laminated benchmark](https://doi.org/10.4153/CJM-1964-065-1), which the construction exceeds by 25/24.

## 1. A geometric weighted packing theorem

Let Y be any set in the Euclidean plane of minimum distance at least 1, with a marked subset W of minimum distance at least √2. No lattice, periodicity, or triangulation hypothesis is imposed. Write N(Q), W(Q) for the numbers in a square Q of side L. Uniformly in the square,
```
 N(Q)                     ≤ (2/√3) L² + O(L+1),
 N(Q) + W(Q)/4            ≤ (2/√3)(13/12) L² + O(L+1).
```
Both bounds are attained asymptotically by the unit triangular lattice with one of its three colors marked. In particular
```
128 N(Q)+16 W(Q) ≤ (800/(3√3)) L² + O(L+1).
```
The stronger weighted estimate also gives a linear density penalty: at limiting unweighted intensity D, the last weighted intensity is at most 800/(3√3) − 64(2/√3−D).

**Saturation and triangulation.** Add unmarked sites, preserving minimum distance 1, until the set is maximal (the union of a chain of admissible sets is admissible). The enlarged set is locally finite by separation, covers the plane at radius ≤1 by maximality, and is still admissible for W. Its ordinary planar Delaunay tessellation has bounded cells: each empty circumcircle has radius ≤1, all its vertices lie within a disk of radius 1, and the Voronoi cell at each site is bounded by the covering radius. Take any triangulation of each polygonal Delaunay cell across cocircular sites. The resulting locally finite tessellation of the plane consists of triangles with sides ≥1, circumradius ≤1, and opposite angles across each common side summing to at most π (equality for a diagonal inside a cocircular cell). The last fact follows from the empty-circle condition or the inscribed-angle theorem. No genericity perturbation of distances or of marked sites is needed.

Write A₀=√3/4. Each angle is at least π/6 since its opposite edge is ≥1 and the circumradius ≤1. Thus the largest angle γ lies in [π/3,2π/3]. With p,q its adjacent sides, triangle area A=pq sinγ/2≥A₀. This proves the first count by angular shares. For the second count let Θ be the sum of its marked angles (zero if none), and require the area budget
```
T(Θ) = A₀ (12+3 Θ/π)/13.
```
Indeed the triangle accounts for the weight (π+Θ/4)/(2π) of the corners' full angles, and dividing this by the proposed intensity (2/√3)(13/12) gives T. Angles at any tessellation vertex sum to 2π.

**Triangle inequality and exceptional cases.**
- With no marked corners, T<A₀. With three, adjacent sides at γ are ≥√2, so A≥2A₀>T.
- With two marked corners, if their common edge is adjacent to γ then A≥√2 A₀>T. Otherwise take γ at the unmarked corner, so Θ=π−γ. On [π/2,2π/3], concavity of sin gives A≥A₀ plus a chord slope times (2π/3−γ). That slope is (3/π)(1−√3/2)>3√3/(52π), the slope of T in Θ. Hence A≥T. For γ≤5π/12, write m=pq. With m≤9/8 the long edge would have squared length p²+q²−2m cosγ ≤1+m²−m/2 ≤113/64<2, using p,q≥1 and cos(5π/12)>1/4. This contradicts the marked-marked separation. Thus A>(9/8)A₀>T(2π/3)≥T(Θ). On [5π/12,π/2], A≥sin(5π/12)/2>T(2π/3).
- With exactly one marked corner, ignore it if Θ≤π/3, since T≤A₀. For γ≤π/2 and Θ>π/3, the chord slope of sin between π/3 and π/2 again gives A≥T(γ)≥T(Θ). For γ in [π/2,3π/5], A≥sin(3π/5)/2>T(2π/3)≥T(Θ); the inequality follows, for example, from sin(3π/5)/2=√(10+2√5)/8 and T(2π/3)=7√3/26.

Therefore only a one-mark triangle with γ>3π/5 can have a deficit, and that deficit is at most T(2π/3)−A₀=√3/52. Send its charge across the edge opposite γ to its Delaunay neighbor. This shared edge has L²≥2(1−cosγ)>13/5. The opposite angle θ in the recipient is ≤π−γ<2π/5, so cosθ>3/10 and sinθ≥L/2>√(13/5)/2. Write m=pq for the product of the recipient's two incident sides. If m≤8/5 then L²=p²+q²−2m cosθ<1+m²−3m/5≤13/5, impossible. The receiver thus has A>(2/5)√(13/5)>9√3/26. This is more than its maximum possible own budget T(π)=15√3/52 plus three times √3/52. Consequently it is not itself deficient and it covers even three incoming charges.

For finite counting, take the triangles meeting Q, plus the receiving neighbor of each deficient triangle in that set, if not already present. Every deficient triangle in the enlarged set sends to an included recipient, because added receivers cannot be deficient. The number of charges to any recipient is ≤3. All triangles have diameter ≤2 and the enlarged set lies in the square expanded by 4 on each side. Sum the inequalities and use that for every vertex in Q all incident triangle angles are present, so its full weight is accounted for (other vertices give nonnegative terms). This proves the asserted bound for each square and hence for arbitrary aperiodic sets. The same square argument with A≥A₀ proves the first inequality.

**Application to 14D.** Horizontal structure is always the same unit-scale period 2 Z^12; for B the shift is integral, for W it is all-half modulo integral shifts. The binary code at each row can be arbitrary and unrelated to other rows. Assume B has 73–128 words, W has 129–144, each with d≥4, and horizontal plus normal squared packing separation is ≥4. (Full 128 and 144 is the advertised application.) Same-phase normal distance <1 would give a combined binary length-12 d≥4 code with >144 words, impossible. Opposite-phase rows have exact squared horizontal distance 3 for every pair modulo the horizontal period, so their normal distance is at least 1. Two W row sites at normal distance <√2 would give cross Hamming distance ≥3; append a bit distinguishing the two rows to obtain a binary length-13 d≥4 code with >256 words, impossible. Use A(12,4)=144 from [Östergård–Baicheva–Kolev](https://users.aalto.fi/~pat/72.html) by parity extension, and A(13,4)=256 by parity extension of the [Best–Brouwer triply shortened Hamming bound](https://ir.cwi.nl/pub/6915/6915D.pdf), also given in [Brouwer's table](https://aeb.win.tue.nl/codes/binary-1.html).

The normal sites and their W marks satisfy the theorem. Each horizontal periodic count has its row cardinality divided by 2^12 as intensity. The normal bound divided by 2^12 is 25/(384√3), the exact project comparator. Since radius is 1, this is the 14D center density; multiply by π^7/7! for occupied fraction. Box counts with uniform boundary estimates also bound Euclidean upper density (tile with large aligned boxes and take their side length to infinity after the observation window). The theorem does not cover a rich row in the other phase, banks of mass ≤72, arbitrary real row translations/rotations, a new horizontal period, or scaled/coupled horizontal and normal metrics.

## 2. The fixed Hamming scaffold determines every possible insertion

For the actual [14D packing](https://github.com/alexlegeartis/SpherePackings/tree/43df41742f55bceecca95081c4e50f874e143efd/constructions/dim14), keep only the two full Hamming color classes, with their exact horizontal labels. No assumption is made on the code or normal position of an **additional** point. If its squared distance to this Hamming scaffold is ≥4, its normal projection must be a Witt site of the original triangular grid and all its horizontal coordinates must be half-integers. Conversely all such points are at squared distance at least 4 from the scaffold. Thus one cannot improve by keeping the Hamming banks fixed and replacing the entirety of the Witt portion with any other packing at minimum squared distance 4, even a nonperiodic or nonlayered one: new positions are forced into the Witt rows and each has at most 144 binary residues per horizontal period on average. For literal nonperiodic choices in a Witt row, the same bound holds in every half-grid 2-cube, in which the positions themselves form a binary length-12 d≥4 set. The construction keeps exactly that many. A pair between rows can further restrict feasible code choices but cannot increase this cap.

**Horizontal covering by each Hamming coset.** For h∈R^12 round to a binary corner u modulo 2, let r_i∈[0,1/2] be the per-coordinate error and a_i=1−2r_i. For an error e taking u to the desired syndrome, the squared distance is
```
Σ_i (1−a_i)²/4 + Σ_{i in supp(e)} a_i .
```
The columns 16,…,27 are three tags of four odd columns each, with distinct low labels F₄ inside each tag; the fourth odd tag 28,…,31 is missing. For any desired syndrome there are four errors with pairwise disjoint supports (zero syndrome uses empty supports):
- An even nonzero syndrome of tag difference zero has disjoint pairs inside each tag, with a total of six across the three tags. An even nonzero tag difference pairs the two appropriate complete tags and gives four disjoint pairs, with the needed low difference.
- For an odd missing-tag syndrome, use one point from each tag; the four triples `(x, ξx,(1+ξ)x+w)` in low labels, with ξ primitive in F₄ and w the target low label, are disjoint with the desired XOR.
- For an odd present-tag syndrome, first use the singleton at that column. Use two of the other three entries in that same tag for separate triples, completing one with a pair in each of the other tags. Their required low differences are distinct nonzero d₁,d₂. The unused pair in each other tag has the same difference as the used pair (all four sum to zero). Join these two unused pairs to the last entry of the first tag for the last error, because its low discrepancy is d₁+d₂. These four errors are disjoint.
Averaging the four errors bounds squared horizontal distance by
```
Σ_i (1−a_i)²/4 + Σ_i a_i/4 = 3 − (1/4) Σ_i a_i(1−a_i).
```
The covering radius squared is exactly 3 because the all-half point attains it. Equality forces every coordinate to be an integer or a half-integer.

**Normal covering.** The Witt sublattice has triangular spacing √3 and covering radius 1. Choose a nearest Witt site W₀ to z, with t=||z−W₀||≤1. Its six Hamming neighbors lie in regular unit directions. One has direction within π/6 of z−W₀, with squared normal cost at most 1+t²−√3 t≤1−(√3−1)t. Thus outside Witt sites some Hamming bank is at normal squared distance strictly below 1. At a Witt site, admissibility requires horizontal distance squared 3 to **all six** neighboring Hamming cosets.

**Common horizontal maximizers.** The six neighboring syndromes, from the literal Hamming–Witt label formula, are always a translate of S={1,2,3,28,29,30}. Their affine span of differences is L=span(1,2,28). Let F be the coordinates where h is half-integral, of size m. The horizontal squared distance to each Hamming coset is m/4 plus the minimum number of errors on unmasked coordinates to match its syndrome modulo V_F, the span of the masked columns. Thus m∈{0,4,8,12} if equality 3 holds. If m=0, attaining 3 requires the syndrome after rounding to be odd and missing; a common maximizer is impossible since S contains both parities. If m=4, V_F has rank ≥3. At rank 4 the quotient has dimension 1, and every syndrome can be reached with at most one unmasked error. At rank 3 the quotient has dimension 2, and the projected columns span it so at most one nonzero quotient value is missing from the individual columns. Attaining projected distance 2 for all six thus requires all six to lie in that one quotient coset, implying L⊆V_F. The ranks then give equality, but all odd columns of L have the missing tag, so V_F cannot be generated by actual masked columns. If m=8, V_F has rank ≥4, and getting projected distance 1 for all six requires rank exactly 4 and again L⊆V_F (the six must all lie in its unique nontrivial coset). Quotient out the two low bits span(1,2); the image of V_F is two-dimensional and contains the missing odd tag from L. Such a subspace can contain at most two odd tags. Eight actual masked columns already require at least two distinct nonmissing odd tags, a contradiction. Finally m=12 makes horizontal distance exactly 3 to each bank, with no additional condition. This proves the exact admissible set.

**Strict full packing saturation with an explicit margin.** The Witt code covers its binary corners within Hamming distance 3. This follows from the [Leech–Sloane construction, §2.6](https://doi.org/10.4153/CJM-1971-081-3): it contains the hexads of S(5,6,12) and the six disjoint pairs, and is closed under complements. Reduce a given corner to weight ≤6 by complement; for weights 0–3 use a pair meeting it when nonzero, for weights 4–5 use a containing hexad, and for weight 6 use a hexad containing a chosen 5-subset. Thus no center can be added to the full packing.

More quantitatively, its squared covering radius is at most 4−1/40. Put ε=1/40. Suppose a point has squared distance >4−ε to P. For the nearest Hamming normal neighbor of W₀, the covering bounds give t<ε/(√3−1)<3ε/2, and `Σ_i a_i(1−a_i)<4ε`. Let d_i be the distance to the nearest half-grid on coordinate i. Since a_i(1−a_i)=4d_i(1/2−d_i)≥d_i, every d_i<4ε; then a_i(1−a_i)≥(2−16ε)d_i and Σ d_i<5ε/2. Round h to that half-grid and z to W₀. If not all horizontal coordinates become half-integral, the common maximum test and quarter-integrality give some Hamming neighbor with squared distance ≤1+11/4=15/4. Otherwise there is a Witt point at squared distance ≤3. In either case move back to the original point. Squared horizontal torus distance changes by at most 2Σd_i<5ε (one-dimensional squared distance on a length-two torus is 2-Lipschitz), and squared normal distance costs at most 2t+t²<3ε+9ε²/4, because the exact rounded normal cost used is at most 1. Thus the true squared distance is <15/4+8ε+9ε²/4<4−ε, contrary to supposition.

These statements are independent: the weighted theorem uses only extremal binary-code sizes and the planar normal geometry; the fixed-scaffold theorem applies to the particular Hamming labels irrespective of replacement geometry or row definition. Neither claims global 14D optimality, and relative deformation of the Hamming scaffold or additional real phase types remains open.
