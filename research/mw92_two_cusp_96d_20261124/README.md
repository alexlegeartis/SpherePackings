# Two-cusp full-fibre MW92 lifts in 96 dimensions

**A scoped obstruction, not a density record.** We test packings whose
horizontal parts are full affine cosets of the common kernel of
reduction at two distinct cusps of MW92, within the zero-reduction
lattice at the first cusp. Arbitrary vertical geometry and assignment
of labels to vertical centers are allowed; other horizontal metrics,
partial fibres, or the use of nonzero first-cusp residue are not covered.

## Exact vectors in the common kernel and in every difference colour

Use the normalized equation Y²−X³=v⁴⁷−v over F47[i] from
[the related derivative construction](../mw92_derivative_lines_96d_20261124/README.md).
Its 48 cusps are P¹(F47). Heights are squared norms, given by 16+2 times
the intersection count with the zero section. This uses the same
irreducible fibre normalization as the [published parent](../mw92_cusp_d4_20261122/README.md).

Take the degree-(18,27) polynomial section P=(A,B) from the line-2
entry of the derivative replay. Under v→(23v+38)/(v+23), use output
scales λ⁻² and λ⁻³ with λ=21+17i, λ⁶=21. Multiplying A and B by the
minimal Weierstrass factors of degrees 16 and 24 yields a section Q
on the same equation. The reduced rational x coordinate of P−Q has
numerator of degree 20 and monic denominator ((v−28)(v−41))². Thus it
has two simple zero-section meetings at 28 and 41 and no others,
including infinity, where the x-coordinate scaling has degree 16.
This is a literal norm-20 section in the common kernel. PGL₂(F47)
transports it to every ordered pair of cusps: a determinant in the
real field has a sixth root in F47[i] (the real subgroup is generated
by the 48th power of a primitive element). Both prescribed reductions
being zero force the corresponding two meetings, proving a minimum
of 20 in the common kernel.

Normalize the distinguished cusps to infinity and zero. The line-0
degree-(18,27) polynomial has norm 18 and vanishing reduction at
infinity. Its finite cusp residues A(b)/B(b) cover all eight
multiplicative cosets of the group of λ with λ⁶ in F47*. Explicitly,
relative to g=2+i the discrete-log residues modulo 8 have counts
(7,6,3,7,8,4,6,6). The transformation v→av+b with λ⁶=a and the
compensating scales send the value to λ A(b)/B(b), preserving its
norm and zero reduction at infinity. Hence every nonzero second
colour has a literal norm-18 representative with first residue zero.
The common kernel has covolume 47^11: covolume 47^7 of MW92 times the
index 47^4 from the two onto reduction stages.

## Density bound for all such tails

If minimum squared separation is q, the horizontal norm-20 section
forces q≤20. Within each vertical colour the point set in R⁴ has
squared separation at least q, since its full horizontal fibres are
identical. When q>18, any two different colours must have squared
vertical separation at least q−18, by the witnessed horizontal
difference. Count point intensity by disjoint balls. The upper
intensity is 47²·32/(π² q²) using colour counts separately, or
32/(π² (q−18)²) using one vertical set if q>18. Multiply the smaller
estimate by (q/4)^48/47^11 for center density. The same calculus as
in the derivative proof gives a maximum at either q=18·47/46 or q=20:
the separate-colour branch increases as q^46, and the logarithmic
derivative on the second branch is 48/q−2/(q−18), changing sign
only from negative to positive. Both endpoint ratios relative to
the existing cusp+D4 lattice are strictly below 0.116.

Run `python -B verify.py` with standard Python. It reads the pinned
polynomials in the sibling derivative directory and checks the exact
field arithmetic, both pole positions, complete second-colour coverage
and the two rational density comparisons.

## Even mixing the two cusp residues linearly cannot reach minimum 20

For a transformed degree-(18,27) witness, let u and v be distinct
finite real-field cusps in the source. Choose a base substitution
matrix with entries a=cu, b=dv, c,d in F47* and multiplier
λ⁶=cd(u−v). The new residues at infinity and zero are respectively
λ c⁻⁸ R(u), λ d⁻⁸ R(v), where R=A/B on the source finite cusps.
Thus their ratio is (d/c)⁸ R(u)/R(v). The eighth powers in F47* are
its 23 squares. For any fixed eighth power, the two choices of d/c
differ by sign. Since −1 is nonsquare, varying c and that sign
lets the determinant attain all values in F47*. All possible
sixth roots therefore range over the group generated by g⁸.
The factor d⁻⁸ is real, so the projective line of the second
residue runs through the six directions of its residue-log class
modulo 8.

For each of those eight classes we use the five pinned polynomial
witnesses and the distinct finite pairs u,v. The script
`python -B ratios.py` checks that the ratios cover all 2,208
nonzero field values in **each** class. The ratio zero is supplied
by the earlier section with first residue zero and every nonzero
second residue. The reversed construction covers cases with second
residue zero. Therefore every projective line in the four-dimensional
F47 vector space of the two additive residues has a norm-18 section
on it. Any additive quotient whose kernel on this residue space is
nontrivial has a norm-18 vector in its horizontal kernel. In
particular this defeats square-minimum-20 lifts that mix the two
cusp residues via only two linear colour coordinates. This
projective argument is not a literal enumeration of all nonzero
residue pairs and does not rule out arbitrary partial horizontal
fibres or metric changes.
