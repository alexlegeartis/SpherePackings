# Coupled E6 periods in dimension 12

Let the period be the coordinate lattice of two E6 factors with Gram
$B\otimes G$, where $G$ is the E6 simple-root Gram and $B$ is any
positive definite real symmetric 2-by-2 matrix. The phase group is
$(1/q)L/L$.

* For **q=2, arbitrary motifs** or **q=3, additive motifs**, no choice
  of $B$ beats center density $1/27$.
* For **q=3, arbitrary motifs**, the same bound holds if, after Gauss
  reduction and normalization of the squared packing minimum to 2,
  $B$ has both diagonal entries 1. For general $B$, four relaxed
  vertex masks are not excluded for nonlinear motifs.
* At the reduced metric $a=c=t=1$, the three-site additive motif
  below attains exactly $1/27$. This matches the 12D benchmark.

This is a family optimum, not a global upper bound. Arbitrary phase
orders, unrelated metric cross-blocks, and other 12D periods are not
covered. The comparison is to [Cohn's sphere-packing table](https://cohn.mit.edu/sphere-packing/).
The Eisenstein extension of E6 for the 12D comparator already appears
in [Conway and Sloane, *The Coxeter–Todd lattice*](https://neilsloane.com/doc/Me107.pdf).

## Reduction to exact finite Fourier bounds

Gauss reduce $B$ by an integral basis change of its two slots. A sign
change and swap give the form with entries $B_{11}=a$, $B_{22}=c$ and
$B_{12}=t/2$, with $0\le t\le a\le c$. This preserves the phase group.
Normalize the packing's squared minimum to 2. Pure E6 roots force
$a\ge1$. For each phase, an integral lift $(u,v)$ gives the squared
distance upper bound

$$
 \bigl(a\|u\|^2+c\|v\|^2+t\langle u,v\rangle\bigr)/q^2 .
$$

Only actual lifts are used, so incompleteness of the witness search
can only enlarge the admitted graph. Diagonal Weyl action and joint
sign reversal preserve the metric for all parameters; the equal-scale
calculation also uses slot swap.

Admitting any chosen list of phase orbits imposes linear lower
inequalities. In coordinates $(a-t,c-a,t)$ the recession directions
are nonnegative and hence their metric increments are positive
semidefinite. Log determinant is concave and does not decrease
along recession directions. Thus some vertex has no larger
determinant and admits all the chosen phase orbits. The checker
enumerates every triple of gate or cone hyperplanes using exact
integer determinants and checks the valid vertices. The equal-scale
calculation is the one-parameter counterpart.

If $s_j$ is orbit size and $f_j$ the averaged normalized
autocorrelation, then $0\le f_j\le1$, $f(0)=1$, and the Fourier
constraints use exact integer character sums (twice real part).
The LP objective is $\sum_j s_j f_j$; the pointwise bound is
$s_j f_j\le s_j$. A numerical LP only proposes nonnegative
dual multipliers. The checker converts them to fractions and pays
every positive column deficit using $f_j\le1$. This is a rational
upper bound and may then be floored. The center density of an
$M$-point motif is $M/[64\cdot3(ac-t^2/4)^3]$. Additive motifs have
power-of-$q$ sizes. The checker includes a four-point binary control.

## The sole possible improving additive mask

In the ternary case the only minimal-determinant mask permitting
an improving power-of-three size is rank seven at
$(a,c,t)=(9/5,18/5,0)$. The admitted mask forces
$d(u)+2d(v)\ge10$, where $d$ is the one-slot squared lift bound.
This implication is checked on every admitted phase orbit.

Suppose a rank-seven linear subspace meets this gate. Its first-slot
kernel at $v=0$ is nonzero. The only two nonzero $u$ with witness
depth at least 10 are the nonzero elements of the radical $R$ of
$G\bmod3$. Thus the subspace is the graph of a linear map from
the entire vertical six-space to $W=\mathbb F_3^6/R$, with each graph
entry expanded by its full fibre $R$. At a norm-two vertical root,
every horizontal residue in the fibre must have depth at least 6.
Every nonisotropic $W$ class has a literal lift of norm at most 4.
Therefore the pullback of the quadratic form on $W$ vanishes on all
72 vertical roots. Their evaluation rows span the 21-dimensional
space of quadratic forms mod 3. The image is totally isotropic, of
dimension at most two. The vertical kernel has dimension at least
four, and every nonzero element of it must have depth at least 6
because horizontal zero is an available lift class. The explicit
one-slot Fourier certificate bounds such a code by 27 points, a
contradiction.

## Matching witness and replay

At $a=c=t=1$ take three translates generated by phase $(r,r)/3$,
where $r=(2,1,0,2,1,0)$. For the two nonzero phases, the lift
numerator has minimum exactly 18. For the period the minimum is 2:
when both integral components are nonzero, their metric norm is at
least half the sum of their E6 squared norms. The period covolume
is $81/64$, giving center density $3/(64\cdot81/64)=1/27$.

## Closing the 45-site boundary mask

The cone LP alone leaves five nonlinear masks. At the vertex
$(3/2,11/7,10/7)$, the exact density threshold is between 44 and
45 sites and the rational dual bounds cardinality by exactly 45.
If equality holds, complementary slackness eliminates four orbit
columns and forces the unordered count for the two-element orbit
$\{g,-g\}$, with $g=(r,r)$, to equal 45. This count equals the
number of ordered translates by $g$ within the motif, so equality
forces invariance under the three-element subgroup generated by
$g$. The quotient motif must then have 15 points. The reduced
support graph for its 14 nonzero points after translation has
252 vertices and 10,206 edges. An exact branch-and-color search
(236 nodes) rules out a 14-clique. The checker uses an explicit
rational dual and reconstructs the equality equations exactly.
Four nonlinear cone masks remain open in this relaxation.
