# Local rigidity of dense shortened extended Hamming torus motifs

**Scope.** Let \(G=\mathbb F_2^r\), let \(A=\{a_1,\ldots,a_n\}\subset G\), and take the binary linear code
\[
 C_A=\{w\in\mathbb F_2^n:\ \sum_i w_i=0,\quad \sum_i w_i a_i=0\}.
\]
Put one phase \(w/2\) (optionally on either parity sheet) in \(\mathbb R^n/D_n\) per word, with the Euclidean \(D_n\) period fixed. Every nonzero word has weight at least four. For \(r\ge3\) and \(|A|>2|G|/3\), the motif has squared minimum 1 and is a strict local maximum of minimum distance modulo common translation: any sufficiently small nontranslation displacement of its sites lowers the minimum. This includes every such shortened extended Hamming motif with \(11\le n\le16,r=4\). For \(n=10,r=4\), every chart is locally nonimprovable; 4480 of the 5005 column subsets containing zero are strict, and the other 525 contain a strict 16-site submotif. The stated 4480 strict *certificates* do not claim that all other motifs admit nontranslation motions preserving the minimum. No global upper bound, change of period, or finite-superperiod rigidity is claimed.

## Contact graphs imply exact local rigidity

For a binary subgroup \(C\) of a code of distance at least four, let \(T_i(C)\) be the subgroup generated by weight-four words of \(C\) that are nonzero in its \(i\)-th coordinate. If \(T_i(C)=C\) for every \(i\), each Cayley graph on \(C\) with the indicated generators is connected.

Now displace each site \(p\in C\) by \(\eta_p\). For an original contact supported on four coordinates \(S\), write \(\Delta=\eta_p-\eta_{p'}\). When \(\|\Delta\|_\infty<1/4\), the unsigned nearest coordinate residues are \(1/2-|\Delta_i|\) on \(S\) and \(|\Delta_i|\) elsewhere. The parity correction term is at most \(2\min_{i\in S}|\Delta_i|\) regardless of the original sheet labels. Thus the new squared torus distance satisfies
\[
 d\le 1-\sum_{i\in S}|\Delta_i|+\|\Delta\|^2+2\min_{i\in S}|\Delta_i|
 \le 1-\tfrac12\sum_{i\in S}|\Delta_i|+\|\Delta\|^2.
\]
If no contact distances drop below 1, every motion difference along an edge of the \(i\)-th graph satisfies \(|\Delta_i|\le2\|\Delta\|^2\). Let \(\eta_0=0\) by a common translation and \(\epsilon=\max_{p,i}|\eta_{pi}|\). A path of length at most \(|C|-1\) in each graph then shows \(\epsilon\le8n(|C|-1)\epsilon^2\); hence \(\epsilon=0\) if initially less than \(1/[8n(|C|-1)]\). This is an actual finite-neighborhood statement, not only a linearized obstruction. If a strict rigid submotif has at least one contact, no nearby full motif can have larger minimum.

## Dense local additivity lemma for all binary group dimensions

Suppose \(0\in A\), \(|A|>2|G|/3\), and \(f:A\to\mathbb F_2\) satisfies \(f(0)=0\) and \(f(u)+f(v)=f(u+v)\) whenever all three arguments are in \(A\). Then \(f\) is the restriction of a linear function on \(G\).

For \(t\in G\), put \(B=A\cap(A+t)\), which is invariant under translation by \(t\). Connect points \(u,v\in B\) when \(u+v\in A\), and also connect \(u,u+t\). Across either kind of edge \(f(u)+f(u+t)\) is unchanged: for the first, apply local additivity to \(u,v\) and then \(u+t,v+t\); for the second it is tautological. If this graph is disconnected, split into two nonempty unions \(E,F\) of components. Both are invariant under translation by \(t\). Every cross sum lies outside \(A\), and so does its translate by \(t\), so \(E+F\subset A^c\cap(A^c+t)\). Fixing one member of \(E\) and of \(F\) shows both sizes are at most \(|A^c\cap(A^c+t)|=|B|-2|A|+|G|\). It would follow that \(|B|\ge4|A|-2|G|\); together with \(|B|\le|A|\) this contradicts the density condition. The graph is connected, so define \(g(t)=f(u)+f(u+t)\) independently of the choice of \(u\in B\). For \(s,t\in G\), a three-way intersection gives \(u,u+t,u+t+s\in A\); telescoping shows \(g(t+s)=g(t)+g(s)\). Taking \(u=0\) gives \(g=f\) on \(A\).

For any coordinate \(i\), translate the columns by \(a_i\), which does not change \(C_A\) due to its even sum requirement. A linear functional orthogonal to \(T_i(C_A)\) now corresponds to a function on \(A+a_i\) whose value at zero, subtracted from each entry, satisfies the local additivity lemma: the nontrivial local equations are exactly those from \(\{0,u,v,u+v\}\). It is therefore affine on \(A+a_i\). These are precisely the row span of the parity-check matrix, hence \(T_i(C_A)=C_A\). Finally \(|C_A|=2^{n-r-1}>1\) under the stated \(r\ge3\) conditions and generation by weight-four words proves an actual contact exists.

## The finite \(n=10,r=4\) edge case

For a column set containing zero, enumerate all weight-four supports with column XOR zero. Compute \(T_i(C_A)\) via row reduction. Let \(W\) be the weight-four supports lying in every \(T_i(C_A)\), and \(H\) their binary span. The verifier checks that \(H\ne0\) and that the supports in \(W\) containing each coordinate span \(H\). Thus \(H\) is a strict rigid submotif. Its dimension is 5 in 4480 and 4 in 525 of the 5005 subsets. Every column set is translated to one containing zero without changing the even code, so this covers every \(n=10,r=4\) case.
