# A primitive 144-site 11D packing matching the lower bound, with an exact 68-coset cubic backbone and a genuinely off-grid deformation

The known comparison is 72/(2^11) = 9/256 at minimum distance two from the binary (11,72,4) codes of Östergård, Baicheva and Kolev, https://users.aalto.fi/~pat/72.html . No new density record or historical priority is claimed. The finite witness was constructed from optimal codes through Hadamard triality and exact Hall matching between large stocks. All 144 coordinates are explicit in motif.json; python verify.py motif.json verifies the claims below independently of the search.

Let D4 be the even-sum integer vectors in four coordinates. Our period Λ=2Z^7 × 2D4 has covolume 2^7×(2^4×2)=4096. Center labels belong to the group V/Λ for V=Z^7×D4*, with 8192 elements. The binary outer distance and exact two-sheet inner D4 distance are computed independently per pair; an odd-sum nearest quotient in the inner block is repaired at the cheapest coordinate. The nonzero periods have length at least two; 4,308 out of the 10,296 distinct label pairs have minimum squared distance exactly four and none smaller. Thus the center density is 144/4096=9/256. Any translation symmetry differs by two centers and therefore lies in V. Checking its residue against all 144 labels leaves only the identity, so Λ is the full period group; a rotated cubic 2Z^11, of covolume 2048, cannot itself be a period lattice.

For an ambient vector t let its overlap be the number of motif residues that remain in the motif after adding t. Any subpacking invariant under a Euclidean lattice P must lie in X∩(X−t) for every t in P. For every norm-two vector t of V, the only overlaps strictly above 136 occur at the fourteen pure outer ±2e_i (overlap 144), which span just seven dimensions. For t outside V the overlap is zero. Every rotated cubic 2Z^11 has eleven independent length-two directions, so no such periodic subpacking of X can have intensity exceeding 136/4096=68/2048. The eight directions of overlap exactly 136 are the ± axes of a four-dimensional Hadamard frame, all inducing the same nonzero element k=30 of order two. Together with the seven pure outer axes they generate a rotated cubic period, and the common intersection with its k-translate realizes the 68-coset maximum. This rules out attaching one or two defects to a retained 70- or 71-coset cubic backbone. The 144-site *unperturbed* motif nevertheless lies in a rotated unit integer grid. This distinction matters: any subset of any such grid at separation two has center density at most 9/256 by partition into side-two binary cubes and A(11,4)=72.

**Off every rotated integer grid.** Move label 4167 by +e_1/16 and label 6169 by +e_6/16 (outer coordinates, one-based), including all their Λ-copies. Each inter-label original contact incident to a moved label has zero component on its chosen axis: the corresponding outer bit agrees at all 57 or 58 sharp labels respectively. A component ±2 on that axis would have all other components zero, hence could only equal a period between copies of the same label. So the squared lengths of all inter-label contacts are unchanged or increase, and same-label differences stay fixed. All remaining vectors had integer squared length at least five in the rotated frame; since the difference of two new displacements has norm at most √2/16, none fall below two. The new packing still has center density 9/256. Its differences include 2e_1 and the moved label 4167 minus fixed label 5675, whose scalar product is 17/8. No translate of any orthogonal image of Z^11 can contain this packing, because its lattice differences would have integral scalar products. The 142 fixed labels constitute one V-class and each displaced singleton a different V-class. A translation outside V has at most four overlapping labels (at most two sourced from the 142-class and two from singletons). A translation in V cannot interchange unequal V-classes, and can match a singleton only when it belongs to Λ; for a nonperiod its overlap is at most that of the former unwiggled motif. This proves unchanged full period group and 68 upper bound. Neither moved label belongs to the 136-site cubic core, which proves equality also for the off-grid packing.

**A guest that can move independently, including aperiodically.** Alternatively move those two labels by one unit, not one sixteenth, to positions U=4103, V=4121. They differ by k and together form one coset of the rotated cubic lattice H generated above. Removing them leaves 142 fixed labels. Each new label has precisely 129 wrapped squared distances 5 and 13 of squared length 9 to that fixed scaffold. The distance from the entire guest coset g+H to the scaffold is therefore at least √5. In H's orthonormal frame index a guest by a∈Z^11, position g+2a. Add the cyclic transverse perturbation ε((-1)^a2,(-1)^a3,…,(-1)^a1), with ε=1/32. Each old nearest pair now has squared length 4+4ε²; an old nonnearest guest pair is still at least 2√2−2√11 ε. Guest-to-scaffold separation is at least √5−√11 ε. All three bounds strictly exceed two, including after moving every guest independently again by any vector of norm at most 1/4096 (subtract twice this for a pair). The whole family, aperiodic choices included, retains its density: bounded displacement changes only surface-order counts on large boxes. This is a positive source of off-grid motifs and simultaneous changes of many labels, not permission to insert more points. A genuine gain must exceed this fixed site count or reduce its volume; a small amount of guest clearance alone does neither.
