# Index-two Leech layers with binary shifts in dimension 27: a family ceiling

Let H be **any index-two sublattice of a Leech lattice scaled to squared minimum 32**. Let V be a three-dimensional lattice of arbitrary Gram matrix, and choose any homomorphism from V to the torus of H with image killed by two. Over each v in V put the translated copy H+f(v). If the resulting lattice packing retains squared minimum at least 32, its **center density at the inherited minimum 32** is at most 2 sqrt(2)/5, less than the known lattice lower bound 1/sqrt(3). In fact the minimum is exactly 32 because H contains shortest vectors, as also follows from its representatives below. The two numbers are about 0.565685 and 0.577350. This does not constrain nonbinary phases, an arbitrary 24D horizontal sublattice, or unrestricted 27D lattices/packings. No record table is changed. The unrestricted 27D construction of [Vardy (1999)](https://doi.org/10.1090/S0002-9947-99-02169-8) attains 1/sqrt(2); the 27D lattice baseline is [Bacher (1997)](https://doi.org/10.1007/s002220050180) (see also the [Nebe–Sloane density catalogue](https://www.math.rwth-aachen.de/~Gabriele.Nebe/LATTICES/density.html)).

## Idea and exact finite test

Write G for the binary extended Golay code, D24 for the even-sum integer vectors, and z=1+4e0 (all-one vector plus four in coordinate 0). The horizontal scaled Leech lattice is the union of K and z+K, where K=2G+4D24 (0/1 representatives for G). The binary generators are extended cyclic shifts of polynomial 2787, encoded from its lowest-degree coefficient in the least significant bit. This yields a self-dual doubly even code of minimum 8. The normalized lattice (divide by sqrt(8)) is even unimodular, and the odd coset has no vector with every entry ±1 by the even carry constraint and doubly even code. Thus its minimum is four; this is the usual Leech lattice. In particular, H below contains a 4(e_i-e_j) with i and j avoiding the mark or in one dodecad block.

Since the normalized lattice is unimodular, index-two sublattices correspond to nonzero Leech classes h modulo two, using its integral pairing. These classes have three orbits (types 2, 3 and 4); see [Allcock, Orbits in the Leech lattice](https://www.emis.de/journals/EM/expmath/volumes/14/14.4/Allcock.pdf) and [the explicit orbit description](https://mmgroup.readthedocs.io/en/latest/guide.html). The following norms are in the integer coordinate model before division by sqrt(8). In each case H consists of the Leech vectors x with x·h/8 even.

* Take h=4(e0-e1), of squared length 32, or h=4(e0+e1+e2+e3), of squared length 64. Write M for its support (mask 3 or 15). Both pair evenly with z. In both cases replace G in K by R={r in G : r·M=0 in F2}, without changing z or D24. The second representative is nonzero modulo twice Leech (a weight-four binary mark is not Golay); a norm-8 vector in the normalized lattice cannot be in the class of a norm-4 vector, since the two half-sum and half-difference vectors would both be nonzero, and their squared norms sum to 6 < 8. Thus these representatives have types 2 and 4.
* For type 3 take h=2O, where O is a binary dodecad containing coordinate zero (mask 6678945). Its squared length is 48. Again z pairs evenly. Write O' for the complementary dodecad. All of G is now used, but a representative 2r+4d requires the carries on O and O' each to have parity wt(r restricted to O)/2 mod 2. The two block half-weights have the same parity, and are integers by self-orthogonality of G. The pure period is four times the product of the two even-sum lattices in the two blocks.

Let F consist of the classes x in (1/2)H/H of squared depth at least 24, i.e. every x-h0, h0 in H, has squared norm at least 24. The replay constructs F with exact integer operations, with full 24-bit binary addresses ADDING BY XOR. Results:

| mark / type | integer representatives in F | half-integer representatives in F | total | ordered triples a+b=c wholly in F |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| two / 2 | 50648 | 47104 | 97752 | 0 |
| dodecad / 3 | 24553 | 24576 | 49129 | 0 |
| four / 4 | 28096 | 24576 | 52672 | 0 |

Here half-integer means *all* 24 coordinates are in Z+1/2. For clarity about exhaustiveness: in the two marked cases write an integer class as p+2b+4e e0. The parity masks p are in R modulo its all-one vector (10 bits). The chosen linear quotient of even b by R contributes 12 bits, and the extra carry parity contributes one. In the dodecad case p is in G modulo ones (11 bits), with two required parities on b. There are ten remaining b-bits modulo G and two carry parities. The last bit in either case adds z/2. The programs take *actual sums* of 2-torsion generators for p, recording their coordinate carries; the section for even b is 2b plus four times the parity of wt(b)/2 (separately on both blocks when appropriate). The half-weight character polarizes the bitwise overlap, which is exactly the carry in adding two masks. Thus these are independent linear group addresses for all 2^24 classes, not XOR on raw coordinate labels.

Depth arithmetic: subtract either 2r or z+2r, then minimize modulo the pure carry period. In doubled coordinates, each integer period component is 8; its closest residue u has absolute value at most 4. If its chosen correction parity must change on a block, the least penalty to the squared doubled norm is min(64-16|u_i|) on that block. Apply this independently on each required block. This is also the independent direct distance routine used for spot checks. The marked code speeds up the integer test by minimizing the number of errors outside the odd positions: if that number is nonzero, their tied ±2 choices can always repair the single carry parity. If zero, a necessary parity flip costs 8 on a nonempty odd support (16 when empty). On odd support 8 or 16 the exceptional side of size 16 is tested explicitly; on size 12 even this possible bonus is below 24. For half-integer coordinates each mismatch costs two above a total base six. A required parity repair costs four if there is a mismatch, twelve otherwise. For the dodecad the same rule applies separately on its two blocks. All contributing errors are generated by exact code/syndrome enumeration. The Walsh transform computes the sum of cubes of the unnormalized Fourier coefficients in signed 128-bit arithmetic: dividing that sum by 2^24 counts the ordered additive triples exactly.

## Why no choice of 3D layer metric rescues the construction

All half-class squared depths are multiples of four, since 2x is a vector of the scaled even Leech lattice. Thus a class outside F has squared depth at most 20. The preimage of F in V/2V is sum-free by the table. Any sum-free subset of the nonzero vectors of F2^3 lies in some affine hyperplane not containing zero: assigning value one to each member is consistent because any odd minimal linear dependence would have size three (or one), forbidden. Consequently for some nonzero linear functional the squared length of each vertical representative is at least 12 in the three nonzero classes of its kernel. In the other four classes it is at least 8: twice every nonzero vertical vector is an unshifted vector and must have squared length at least 32.

Use an obtuse superbase v0,v1,v2,v3 with sum zero for V ([Conway–Sloane, *Low-dimensional lattices VI*](https://neilsloane.com/doc/Me169.pdf)). Write p_ij=-v_i·v_j ≥ 0. Each of the seven subset sums of the first three vectors has squared norm the corresponding K4 cut sum. The Gram determinant of a basis is the weighted spanning-tree polynomial on the four vertices (the Gram is a reduced graph Laplacian). For each of seven nonzero functionals impose the seven cut lower bounds 8 or 12 above, plus the six nonnegativity bounds. The exact vertex enumeration in tail_exact.py obtains minima 432,432,432,432,432,432,400, hence squared covolume at least 400. It suffices to minimize on vertices: the recession cone is the nonnegative orthant, on which adding conorms does not decrease the tree polynomial; on convex combinations of vertices use concavity of log determinant. Every feasible graph has positive weights connecting its vertices because all nontrivial cuts are positive.

H has center density 1/2. The 27D center density at squared minimum 32 equals (1/2) (sqrt(8))^3 / covol(V), hence is at most 2sqrt(2)/5. This calculation gives a ceiling irrespective of whether its endpoint is attained in the actual layers.

## Replay

Run `python3 verify.py` using Python 3.10+ and a C++17 compiler. Temporary binary lists are generated and deleted automatically. The three files of deep classes can also be checked directly for additive triples by exhaustive pairs instead of the Walsh cubic moment.
