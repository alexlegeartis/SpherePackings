# 25D: a sharp obstruction for 16 complete Leech-section colors, and for two translated palettes on the FCC torus

No new global lower bound. This is a sharp **carrier theorem** identifying what a prospective better construction must change. Use center density (occupied fraction divided by unit ball volume).

## Proposition 1. Arbitrary tails, including nonperiodic ones

Write V for the coordinate 22-plane orthogonal to the first two standard coordinates of Leech, U for the coordinate 2-plane, and K=Λ24∩V. For each c in K*/K choose any set of sites T_c in R³. Consider centers (K+c)×T_c ⊂ V×R³ (union over the 16 colors). Assume spheres of radius r>0 about all centers have disjoint interiors. Then their upper center density is at most r^22/√2, in particular ≤1/√2.

In Leech's integral coordinates, scaled by 1/√8, all coordinates have the same parity, the mod-4 support has Golay minimum 8, and the coordinate sum is 0 mod 8 for even points, 4 mod 8 for odd. Thus S=Λ24∩U consists of (4a,4b)/√8 with a+b even; its covolume is 4. Primitivity and unimodularity give π_V(Λ)=K*, covol(K)=covol(S)=4, and |K*/K|=16. For completeness, restrictions of homomorphisms Λ→Z onto primitive K are surjective; unimodularity identifies them with projections, and the kernel of Λ→S* is K, so det_vol(Λ)=covol(K)covol(S*)=1. K has minimum 4 (for instance (0,0,4,4,0^20)/√8), hence every nonempty packing has r≤1.

Same-color tail centers are separated by at least 2r, since their full horizontal translates agree. By the proved Kepler theorem, their upper number per 3-volume is ≤1/(4√2 r³). This bound is uniform in translated boxes of increasing sidelength: repeat a finite cube periodically with buffer 2r, use Kepler, and let the sidelength grow. Each occupied tail contributes the horizontal intensity 1/covol(K)=1/4. Counting in products of a 22-cube and a 3-cube, with the usual vanishing boundary terms (likewise for balls by tiling), proves r^25·16/(4·4√2 r³)=r^22/√2. This does not assume common or periodic tails, linear coloring, or one mark per color and tail period; it does assume complete K-translates, all from one fixed K* palette.

Equality at r=1: take Λ24 and Λ24+h alternately at height j√2 in R^25, h=(4,0^23)/√8. In h+Λ, even relative points have minimum squared length 2 (zero residual would require the wrong mod-8 sum; nonempty Golay support already costs ≥4), odd ones cost ≥24/8. Thus successive layers are separated by norm²≥4 and further layers by the vertical distance; count gives 1/√2, Cohn's benchmark. Both stocks project to K*, since h∈U. This covers all norm20 and norm52 projected 23D carriers built from a normal in U whenever their shifts leave the same V palette, including arbitrary refinements of the 2D tail and second stock h.

## Proposition 2. Escape attempt: two arbitrary translated palettes but fixed FCC pure period in the tail

Take arbitrary t_1,t_2∈V and offer all 32 classes in (K*+t_1)∪(K*+t_2) modulo K. Let each selected label have arbitrary 3D phase modulo the same pure FCC period with minimum 2 (covol 4√2, squared covering radius 2). At radius 1, at most 16 marks can be selected; hence no improvement. The statement concerns two palettes and this particular fixed period: different periods, three palettes, and partial fibers are not ruled out by this argument.

Proof: K*=π_VΛ has strictly smaller covering radius than Λ, whose covering radius is √2. Indeed if z∈V had distance √2 from K*, it would also be a deepest hole of Λ since projection never increases distances. Every nearest λ∈Λ would then have to lie in V, since otherwise its projection is strictly closer. Nearest points to a deepest hole affinely span R²⁴: otherwise they lie on a hyperplane; a small displacement away from this hyperplane, or along its normal if the hole itself is in it, strictly increases all their distances, while nonnearest lattice points have a strict local gap. Contradiction; compactness on V/K* ensures R(K*)<√2. Thus some difference d∈K*/K makes t_2−t_1+d closer than √2 to K. For every c∈K*/K the two labels t_1+c and t_2+c+d have 22D distance <√2. Their FCC tail phases have distance ≤√2, contradicting the required squared separation 4. These are 16 disjoint forbidden pairs. A repeated label is likewise impossible (tail torus diameter √2<2). Hence ≤16 marks. This ceiling is sharp. In the preceding Λ25 construction the pure tail kernel is exactly √2D3: its triples are √2(a+j,b,j) with a+b even, equivalently √2 times the integer triples with even sum. Each of the 16 colors thus has a translated FCC tail lattice with one mark per pure period. The sole necessary density comparator is 17 versus 16 marks.

## Sources and exact scope

The three-dimensional bound is the [formal Kepler theorem of Hales et al.](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/forum-of-mathematics-pi/article/formal-proof-of-the-kepler-conjecture/78FBD5E1A3D1BCCB8E0D5B0C463C9FBC). The Leech covering radius √2 is from [Conway–Parker–Sloane](https://doi.org/10.1098/rspa.1982.0042), also derived in [Borcherds, The Leech lattice](https://math.berkeley.edu/~reb/papers/leech/leech.pdf). The comparison Λ25 center density is 1/√2; no new density record or unrestricted upper bound is claimed.
