# Sphere78 addendum: two-to-three augmentation is impossible even at arbitrary real offsets in the fixed cubic cell
Same 96 OBK public input codes and SHA-256, units and integer-certificate method as [single-trade proof](single_trade.md). For EVERY one of 96·C(72,2)=245,376 choices of two removed binary sites, a 70-point core in a common 2Z^11 period accommodates **at most two** arbitrary real additional sites at separation 2 with the core and with each other. Not an assertion about arbitrary enlarged periods or nonperiodic holes in this two-deletion statement.

Two new ingredients make the check complete:
1. Round each potential donor modulo 2 coordinatewise to its nearest binary v. A strict nonnegative integer mixture excluding v for the original C remains valid following two deletions unless at least one omitted center j is in its support. For each possible first such j, compute the strict mixture for C minus j, which remains valid unless the other removed center k lies in its support. If there is no strict certificate at a step, try every remaining label instead. Deduplicate unordered {j,k} and use rational box secants to test all remaining cells. Integer coordinates and conservative global bounds make the final tests exact; the simplex may fail to find coefficients but cannot accept an exclusion based on floating output alone. The 10,417,969 initial nontrivial two-removal candidates in the native tuned run all exclude, covering 10,418,351 boxes. Exact result: every legal y rounds to a v such that C minus {j,k} also accepts v as an actual binary site. This is not a generic statement for the torus.
2. Test all ordered admissible v,w at Hamming distance d<4, including v=w. Set J={i:v_i=w_i}. Just as for one deletion, exact squared-chord inequalities show ||x_J||²<4−d for x equal to twice the folded residual vector of *each* feasible y rounding to v. All 996,040 directed checks close strictly. Therefore two sites at torus distance≥2 must round to distinct binary sites at Hamming distance≥4. Three surviving donors would yield three distinct compatible binary sites, impossible by A(11,4)=72. The replay also explicitly checks that no legal triple exists among the frozen core's binary corner candidates for every removal, so this last implication does not actually require reusing the classification result. Across all cases 563,520 candidate binary corners counted with multiplicity.
