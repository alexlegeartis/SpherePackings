## Parent and covering input

Let \(L\) be Shioda's MW92 lattice with squared minimum 16 and covolume \(47^7\). Model it using the Mordell–Weil group of \(y^2=x^3+1+t^{48}\) in characteristic 47. Multiplication of the \(x\)-coordinate by a primitive cube root of unity gives an integral height isometry \(J\) of order three. The points \(P,JP,J^2P\) have the same \(y\)-coordinate and are collinear, so their group sum is zero. Thus \(I+J+J^2=0\) on the lattice. The parent minimum and determinant come from [Schütt–Shioda, Theorem 13.35 and §13.4.2](https://web.archive.org/web/20200209144320id_/http://www2.iag.uni-hannover.de/%7Eschuett/BookMWL17.pdf). The [existing source-to-Gram audit](../mw92_sections_20261115/) and [rotation-matrix audit](../cm58_20261116/FRONTIER.md#92d-exact-counterexample-rejects-the-deformation) give arithmetic checks of the parent and the order-three isometry.

If \(N\) balls of squared radius \(d\) cover \(\mathbb R^{92}/L\), their lifts cover Euclidean space with covering density at most \(N V_{92}d^{46}/47^7\). A strict inequality below the [Coxeter–Few–Rogers simplex bound](https://doi.org/10.1112/S0025579300002059) rules out that covering, including when the balls overlap. The verifier uses the [existing cubic lower estimate for the simplex bound](../adaptive_layers_20261115/COVERING_PROOF.md) with exact moments and a rational upper bound on \(\pi\). The certified squared depths \(d_N\) are in [results.json](results.json).

## Nine translates in 96D

Choose \(a\) in the parent torus at squared distance greater than \(d_1\) from zero. Put \(S(a)=\{0,a,-Ja\}\). The six nonzero ordered differences in \(S(a)\) are \(\pm J^i a\) for \(i=0,1,2\), because \(I+J=-J^2\). Choose \(b\) outside the seven radius-\(\sqrt{d_7}\) balls centered at \(0,\pm J^i a\). Label the nine offsets by \((u,v)\in\mathbb F_3^2\) using the sum of entry \(u\) of \(S(a)\) and entry \(v\) of \(S(b)\). Equal second coordinates and distinct first coordinates give horizontal separation squared greater than \(d_1\). Different second coordinates give a difference equal, up to a sign and power of \(J\), to \(b\) or \(b\pm J^i a\), with squared torus distance greater than \(d_7\).

Use the four-dimensional Gram in [results.json](results.json). Assign vertical coefficient \(z\in\mathbb Z^4\) the color
\[
( z_1+z_3+z_4,\ z_2+2z_3+z_4 )\pmod 3.
\]
The color map is onto and its kernel has index nine. The verifier enumerates every nonzero vertical vector of squared norm below 16 using the coordinate bounds \(z_i^2\le16(G^{-1})_{ii}\). Every short difference has a nonzero color with enough horizontal separation to reach squared distance 16. The period is the product of \(L\) and the kernel of the color map. Its nine translates cancel the vertical index in the per-center covolume.

## Lattice shears

For each vertical rank \(r\) from one through four, take the leading principal Gram in [results.json](results.json). The lattice is generated by \((L,0)\) and \((a_j,v_j)\) for the vertical basis vectors \(v_j\). Among the finitely many vertical vectors with squared norm below 16, orient each so its last nonzero coefficient is positive. In these four Grams that coefficient is always 1. To choose \(a_j\), exclude the centers \(-\sum_{i<j}z_i a_i\) for the short vectors with last nonzero coordinate \(j\). There are at most 1, 4, 8 and 18 such centers at successive stages. The depth \(d_N\) for each stage makes the total squared distance at least 16. Taking negatives handles the other orientation. All longer vertical vectors and all vectors in the embedded parent also meet the same minimum. The block triangular lattice basis gives covolume \(47^7\sqrt{\det G}\).
