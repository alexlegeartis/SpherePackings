# Sharp family cuts and an obstruction to the first 15D lift
For the published triangular Hamming–Witt packing P at commit 43df41742f55bceecca95081c4e50f874e143efd, normalized for minimum distance 2. Not global bounds.

## A sharp triangular capacity
Consider arbitrary codebooks on all sites of the unit triangular lattice, each consisting of binary 12-tuples, translated by either the zero vector or the all-1/2 vector, plus period 2 Z^12. Like-phase rows at adjacent normal sites must have cross Hamming distance at least three; each row independently has minimum Hamming distance four. An elementary triangle with a 2+1 split contains at most A(13,4)+A(12,4)=256+144=400 words in its three rows. Indeed the two like-phase rows concatenate with respective bits 0,1 to a single length-13 d4 code. In a monochromatic triangle all radius-one binary balls for all three rows are mutually disjoint, so its total is at most floor(4096/13)=315. Average the inequality over elementary triangles: the upper density of row sites is at most 400/3, including in the aperiodic case by boundary truncation. The new pack attains this bound on every triangle. The exact binary-code capacities are tabulated with their sources in [Brouwer’s table](https://aeb.win.tue.nl/codes/binary-1.html). This restriction does not apply to arbitrary real row offsets, deformed normals, or individual site motions.

## Why an FCC normal substitution alone fails in 15D
Replace the unit triangular normals by the unit-edge FCC lattice, keeping the same two horizontal phases and arbitrary binary rows. Every congruent regular unit tetrahedron has total at most 512: in the 2+2 case the concatenation bound applies twice; in a 3+1 case the monochromatic triple bound gives 315+144=459; for 4+0 the ball bound is 315. Average over translates of even a single such tetrahedron to get average at most 128, so center density ≤128 sqrt(2)/4096=1/(16 sqrt(2)), the classical 15D benchmark. The following exact FCC example attains this scoped bound.
Normalize normal sites x in D3 (integer sum even), scaled by 1/sqrt(2). Phase is x0 mod 2; in each choose an entire 128-word syndrome class with label (28 * [x1 odd]) xor [sum floor(x_i/2) odd], columns 16,…,27 as in P. Norm-squared 1 same phase flips x1, so horizontal syndrome difference is 28 or 29, missing odd Hamming columns, cross-distance ≥3. Mixed phases automatically contribute horizontal squared 3. Norm-squared 2 is a two-unit axial step; same phase with a parity toggle of sum floor(x_i/2), thus label difference 1 and even distance ≥2. Norm-squared 3 same phase has the two-unit component in direction 0 (otherwise mixed) and flips x1, again nonzero; minimum ≥1. Intrarow distinct sites or periods have squared distance ≥4. Larger normal distances are automatic. Thus the bound is attained. This is a family maximum/tie, not a 15D record.

## Direct lamination of P by translates: excluded
For any u in R^14, d(u,P−P)^2≤3. Thus two whole translated copies with a perpendicular layer spacing less than one are forbidden. Even aperiodic vertical stacking of translated copies cannot have 15D center density above that of P itself, strictly below 1/(16 sqrt(2)); a record would require spacing below 25 sqrt(2/3)/24. No arbitrary relative rotations or row surgeries are excluded.

Proof. Write u=(h,z) with z in the triangular normal plane. The normal-site color-zero sublattice is an index-three triangular lattice of side sqrt(3) and covering radius 1. The union of the other two colors likewise covers within 1 (already either single color does). At each nonzero-color normal difference v the mixed P−P fibers contain the full half-grid Z^12+(1/2)^12. Indeed the reference Witt code hits all sixteen even syndromes, as checked in verify_structural.py; the all-ones word has zero syndrome. Fix the color-zero Witt sheet at the origin. For a difference v of nonzero color use the differences with the integer sheet at v and in reverse with the integer sheet at −v: its two colors have opposite syndrome parity. These two contributions supply both halves of the full horizontal half-grid. At each zero-color difference the fiber contains a binary translate, modulo 2Z^12, of D=J xor J, the difference set of the 144-word Julin code. D contains all words of weights 0,4,8,12. These facts suffice and are independent of floating point.

Let r_i=dist(h_i,Z), S=sum r_i, R=sum r_i^2, with 0≤r_i≤1/2. If S−R≥1, use the nearest nonzero-color normal difference: squared horizontal distance to the full half-grid is 3−S+R≤2; the normal cost is ≤1. If S−R<1, then R≤S−R<1 and S<2. Use the nearest zero-color normal difference. Round h to an integer word adjusted for the translate of D. If that word belongs to D, the horizontal cost is R. If odd, one bit flip reaches an available weight divisible by four and the horizontal squared cost is at most 1+R≤2. If even but missing, its weight is 2,6, or 10; for any specified coordinate i, one can flip i and another coordinate of the same bit value to reach an available weight. Choose i maximizing r_i. The cost is ≤2+R−2max r_i≤2, since R≤S max r_i. Thus in either case d(u,P−P)^2≤3.
